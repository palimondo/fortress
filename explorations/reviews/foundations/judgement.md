# The foundations of the revival: the judgement

## 0. How to read this

- **The three layers**, his frame of 2026-10-26 05:25 UTC. L1, the language: the Working Draft (WD, the team's text of 2010-12, copied 2011-02-02), the revised specification, the papers of 2011, 2012 and 2019, the 2012 restart's Types chapter, the team's library as a statement of the language, and the team's later published word. L2, the team's implementation on the JVM: the compiled path's checker, code generator, class loader and run time, the compiler's prelude, and walk. L3, Java interoperability: how Fortress types and natives meet Java's. A ground in one layer does not by itself decide a question in another. Where a team text in L1 itself argues from L2 or L3 (as the 2011 paper argues from Java), the text is the team's L1 word and its argument is theirs to make; I count it as L1.
- **Verdicts.** *Holds*: the tenet is right and its sound reason exists in the right layer. *Holds, text*: right, but its entry states a weaker, misplaced or wrong reason, or lacks the reason the coordinator needs to weigh a trade-off. *Bends*: against a team text where the team left a way the revival did not take. *Reverse*. None of the nine is a bend or a reversal.
- **Consequential** is the coordinator skill's test: reverses, bends or reads anew a decision of his; touches microGPT; chooses where the three principles split or are silent; or cannot be undone. A ruling so marked is his; each carries a default.

## 2. The nine tenets

### Tenet 1. The goal and its measure (P-7, P-8). Holds.

- **Sound reason and layer.** The goal joins two layers on purpose: the intent is judged in L1 (the specification as the revival revises it, the 2012 Types chapter beside it); the measure is L2 (microGPT compiled to JVM bytecode, running fast). The language itself states the aim: the WD is "designed for producing robust high-performance software" (`philosophy.tex:14-18`, `:33-35`). P-8 is a rule of weighing, his words, and lists "the implementations" among the designers' sources: right, so long as an implementation's word is read as L2 evidence.
- **Where the entry crosses layers.** P-7's second sentence, "where the team's later work, in the compiler or in the papers on types, contradicts the original text, the text is updated to follow that work", is his 10-12 words and is the mechanism of P-26. "Later work in the compiler" is two things: a rule the team's checker enforces on purpose (L1 intent shown in L2 code) and an accommodation of the JVM or of a checker gap (L2 only). P-7 should not restate the rule; it should point to P-26, where the distinction lives (ruling 5).
- **Risk, not defect.** The team's wind-down named a conflict between the two halves (the type system against "a virtual machine not designed to support it"), and Steele's 2016 slide 45 proposed "a less complex type system". No measurement settles it for microGPT: no compiled kernel exists, no Java baseline is written. The tenet's two halves can pull apart at phase 5 and 6; that is the goal's own risk, known since 2026-09-08, and not a reason to change it.
- **Default:** stands. P-7's revision sentence points to P-26. Not consequential (a text edit the coordinator shows him).

A JVM limit the team hit is evidence about L2, not a reason to rank an L1 text. (b) Its list of the later word mixes layers and dates: "the number types of the compiler's prelude made siblings in 2009 and 2010" is L2 code, earlier than the WD's last edit (2010-12), earlier than the 2011 paper, with no stated reason (`6896886fb` is a code-generator commit whose message ends "this would be a good time to get coercion working"). ## 3. The prelude question: when a design of the compiler's prelude binds the one library

**The coordinator's proposal:** when the team's later word on the language backs it, as the exclusion rule backs the flat number types; a bootstrap stub does not count.

**Ruling: right, with three sharpenings.**

1. **"Backs" is tested in L1 first.** A paper, the Types chapter, a specification sentence, a commit message or code comment that states the shape as the language's. The exclusion rule passes (four texts). The flat numbers pass through it and through the WD's "mutually exclusive".
2. **Where the L1 text is silent or ambiguous, the team's compiled checker enforcing the shape as a rule is the team's own reading of their text, and binds.** This is row 816's case: `apis.tex:252-256` reads as "no body needed" and is silent on the declaration; `source-code.tex:384-385` reads as the member declared either way; Chase's 2012 export checker counted every api member and refused a component that left one out. The run-time death (`invokeinterface` on the trait's interface) is the cost of the other reading, not the ground. So P-158 holds, and its stated reason should be this, not "by P-26, the compiled path's way wins over the interpreter's library" alone.
3. **"Later" is a date, and a stub is a stub by the team's words.** A prelude line counts as the team's later word only if it is later than the library's text on the same shape and no later L1 text contradicts it. A line the team called "for now", "bogus", "bogo", "hack", "temporary", or that works around a checker gap (static arguments written at every generic call, fixed arities in place of varargs), is an accommodation and does not bind.

**Applied to the sweep.**

- **Where the revival kept the library's text against a backed prelude design: one place, row 816.** Reversed by P-158. Nothing else in the sweep's 29 rows qualifies.
- **`fail(s: String): Zilch`** (Steele, `0886e9654`, 2011-06-13, replacing `fail[\T extends Any\](s: String): T` and adding a parser production). A deliberate design, and L1 in kind (a declared bottom result). It does not bind: the later L1 word contradicts it. The WD forbids writing `BottomType` (`types-vals-vars.tex:514-515`); the 2012 Types chapter makes `Bottom` uninhabited and inexpressible; POPL 2019 says `Bottom` "is intentionally not expressible in the surface syntax" (`ParkPOPL2019.txt:438`), and its [Solve-Step] binds an unfixed parameter to the intersection of its upper bounds and the expected type, never `Bottom`. The revival's way (the generic `fail` kept, the checker's instance rule P-48) is that rule. Walk cannot evaluate `Zilch` (`SkZilchLocal`, `InterpreterBug`). The record weighed the shape without the commit; the outcome is right. **Default:** keep; a FACTS line records the commit and why it does not bind. Not consequential.
- **`comprises T` on the order traits** (Steele, `655e65d53` 2012-06-05 adds it beside the F-bound; `59fdeff62` 2012-06-06 removes the F-bound). A deliberate design, L1 in kind (the self-type idiom; Naden: "The self-type idiom is realized with the comprises clause"). It does not bind the spelling: Steele's own message says the self type "may be an intersection of the declaring trait and the comprises clause", which is the fact P-146 gives `self` in bodies and P-157 in the exclusion test, read from the F-bound; Naden's last word, 2012-08-31, says the idiom "should be ... a separate construct which cannot be used as a first class type, but instead only in bounds" (`justificationOfRTR.tex:626-630`), the bound-based form; POPL 2019 has no self types. The prelude's spelling costs walk a stack overflow (row 407), the checker a stack overflow at eight clauses (route-c experiment §2b), and closes each trait to its parameter (`LexicographicOrder` had to take it too). The record's history of the idiom stops at 2011-12-06 and must be completed with the two commits. **Default:** keep the F-bound; a FACTS line records the two commits. Not consequential. (P-146's signature half and P-157's reasons were ruled in J1023 and J1024 and are not re-ruled here.)
- **`String`'s concatenation with `self` on the left only, right operand `Object`** (chf 2009; Steele widened the right operand 2011-10-12, "supports knapsack code"). A working shape with no recorded reason for the left-only restriction; no later L1 text backs it; the library's api had both sides for walk, and the specification's juxtaposition rule allows `1 || "a"`, which the tests assert. Measured compiled: the prelude's shape refuses `1 || "a"` (`SkCompiledLeft`). It does not bind. The `Object` right operand is a printing device beside the one library's `Any` device; J1024 item 14 defers the tuple-rendering device to the names batch. **Default:** keep the top-level operators. Not consequential.
- **Taken from the prelude, rightly, each with an L1 ground or a stub-free design:** the flat numbers (P-43), `IntLiteral` (P-76), ranges over `ZZ32` (P-77, his JVM reason, L2 by choice and recorded), each integer type's own `MIN`, `MAX`, `MINMAX` (P-47), an integer power at its base's type (P-153), `TotalComparison` below `Comparison` alone, `Comparison`'s lazy `LEXICO`, an unchecked `MatchFailure` (the specification agrees), `Range excludes String`, the `abstract` restatement (P-158). ## 4. The bound: P-42 holds, on these grounds

**L1.** (a) POPL 2019, the type group's last published word, Steele and Ryu among the authors: "By default, each type parameter has an upper bound Any" (`ParkPOPL2019.txt:418-419`). (b) The WD's `Object` sentence (`trait-parameters.tex:49-50`) is absent from the 2008 release (page 67), enters the repository in two bulk imports of 2009-11, the first in a chapter headed "Not Yet Implemented Features"; its composer is not recoverable. The same WD section says "Type parameters are instantiated with types such as trait types, tuple types, and arrow types" (`:53-54`), and the WD puts tuples, arrows and `()` outside `Object` (`types-vals-vars.tex:136`, `:215-216`); so under the WD's own `Object` default no unbounded parameter could take what the WD says parameters take. (c) The library, the team's statement of the language's library, declares its element parameters unbounded and instantiates them at tuples (`Generator[\E\]`, `zip`'s `(E,F)`); only six functions write `extends Any`. (d) The 2012 Types chapter states no default and makes `Any` the top with `Object` excluding `Void` and every tuple type (`types.tick:232-238`); Welterweight and the 2011 paper have `Any` as the top. (e) The team's own 2008 commit left the bound "(either Object or Any)" open. Naden's August 2012 theory calls `Object` "the distinguished top type" in a calculus with no tuples or arrows, where the two names coincide; it is not a vote for `Object` over tuples.

**L2, the archaeology's finding and how to read it.** Steele implemented the WD's sentence on 2012-05-28 ("amazingly, this was not yet implemented"), inserting `extends Any` into the compiled libraries and nine tests wherever they instantiated at `()`, a tuple, an arrow or `Any`; commented it out on 05-29 "so that interpreter tests will pass"; and on 05-30 made it a setting, on in `useCompilerLibraries`, off in `useInterpreterLibraries`. The brief's phrase "Steele's 2012 code chose `Object` as the language's rule" reads too much into it. His message says he was implementing a sentence already written, not deciding one; when the sentence broke walk's tests he did not change walk or the library, he confined the sentence to the compiled path; and he had to write `Any` by hand everywhere the compiled world needed tuples. No team text says the code generator, class loader or RTTI needs `Object` (archaeology §2.6, §5). The team's texts after 2012-05-30 (Types, 05-31; Naden, 08-31; POPL 2019) do not carry `Object` as the default. **The bound, in plain words.** A type parameter is a placeholder for a type, like the `T` in `List[\T\]`. Its bound is the largest type the placeholder may stand for. `Any` is the type of every value. `Object` is the type of every value except tuples (pairs such as `(3, 4)`), functions, and the empty value `()`. You decided that a placeholder with no written bound means `Any`.

- The language, layer one, says `Any`. The team's last paper, from 2019, with Steele and Ryu among its authors, says in so many words that the default bound is `Any`. The team's library, which is their statement of the language's library, writes its placeholders without a bound and fills them with tuples. The 2011 draft's sentence saying `Object` is not in the 2008 text, appears in 2009 in a list of "Not Yet Implemented Features", and sits beside a sentence in the same section saying placeholders take tuple types, which `Object` would forbid.
- The team's build on the virtual machine, layer two, does not need `Object`. In May 2012 Steele implemented the draft's sentence on the compiled path, saying it was "amazingly" not yet implemented. The next day it broke the interpreter's tests. He did not change the interpreter or the library. He made the sentence a setting that is on for the compiled path and off for the interpreter, and he wrote `Any` by hand in every compiled library and test that needed tuples. That is enforcing a sentence on one path, not choosing the language's rule. No team text says the compiled path needs `Object`. A probe this week turned the setting off: tuples and functions as type arguments compile and run exactly as they do today when `Any` is written. With the setting on, as the compile command has it today, a call `id((3, 4))` compiles and silently runs `id(3)`.
- Java, layer three, maps its own `Object` type to Fortress `Any`, not to Fortress `Object`. Nothing in the record ever used Java as a reason for the bound.
