# Option 2 of question A: is it sound?

## The verdict, for Pavol

Option 2 is sound in the form the note gave it, and it contradicts no rule. In that form an arm is chosen on its declared parameter types, reading a type parameter as "some type within its bound", which is what answer 9 decided the specification will say. Only after the choice does answer 8 pick `ZZ64` and convert `z`. For your pair, `T extends Number` beside a plain arm over `Any`, the generic arm then runs at `ZZ64`, through the class the compiled path stamps for `ZZ64`. The specification needs one new sentence: the conversions that the chosen arm's inference needs are part of the call, and the call then dispatches on the converted values. The checker must rank arms on their declared types, not on their instances. Walk must choose on declared types, convert, and dispatch again. But this form does not reach the library's usual generics, whose bound names the type itself (`T extends Equality[\T\]`). No such `T` holds a `ZZ32` and a `ZZ64` as they are, so the plain arm still runs there, exactly as under option 1. Making those generics win too needs a stronger rule: the promotion's conversion never counts against a generic arm. That stronger rule breaks the specification's promise of one most specific arm: a set the checker accepts then has a call with two applicable arms and neither more specific, and under a rough build of that rule another set type-checks and then stops at run time. It would need a new tie rule and a new proof, and that is a judge's work. On speed: the generic arm is called with no dispatch test, through a class stamped for `ZZ64`. Its values stay boxed, and the conversion adds one call per call. So "fast" here means "no dispatch", not "unboxed".

## The two readings

Every check below uses these two names.
- **Reading A** (the note's option 2, answer 9's wording). A declaration applies to a call when the arguments lie in its declared domain, a generic domain read as "for some instance within the bounds" (the 2011 paper: a declaration "is applicable to a type if and only if at least one of its instances is", `Papers/Types/setup.tick:393-397`). The most specific applicable declaration is chosen on declared domains (`overloading-judgement.md:84`, `:97`). Then answer 8 instantiates the chosen generic, which may convert arguments. For the pair of the question, `(ZZ32, ZZ64)` lies in `∃T <: Number. (T, T)` (take `T = Number`), so the generic arm applies without coercion and is the more specific.
- **Reading B** (Pavol's wider words, "fast generics over Any"). A conversion made by answer 8's promotion never counts against a generic arm. So a generic arm that fits the call only by promotion still beats a plain arm that fits it as it is. The opt2 variant builds this reading roughly: a generic candidate that only the coercion attempt admits joins the first attempt, and a generic candidate's coercions do not count in the ranking (`opt2.patch`).
- For a bound that already holds mixed widths (`Number`, `Any`, `AnyIntegral`), the two readings choose the same arm, and opt2's results on those shapes are reading A's. They part only where the generic arm fits the call by promotion alone: an F-bounded generic (`T extends Equality[\T\]`, the library's `Integral[\T\]`, `StandardTotalOrder[\T\]`) or a narrow bound (`T extends ZZ64`).

## 1. The specification's coercion order

**Verdict.** Reading A holds, with one sentence written. Reading B contradicts the promise of one most specific arm, unless a rule is written.

- **The order.** Coercion resolution first looks for a declaration applicable without coercion; only if there is none are coercions added (`Specification/basic/conversions-coercions.tex:472-476`; with the sets Σ and Σ′, `:533-548`). Today's text infers static parameters "before checking the applicability" (`Specification/basic/overloading.tex:170-175`, the dotted form `:205-207`) and "before comparing" (`:292-295`). Answer 9 already rewrites those lines: applicability and specificity on the quantified types, and "inference instantiates the chosen declaration; it does not precede the comparison" (`overloading-judgement.md:84`).
- **Reading A under that text.** Σ holds both arms of the pair, and the most specific is the generic arm. The coercion phase is never reached. So the order stands as written. What is new: the chosen arm's instance converts `z`. The sentence to add, at `conversions-coercions.tex:472-476` and in the Σ branch at `:539-541`: the static arguments of the selected declaration are inferred as the inference chapter says, and the coercions that instance needs are inserted into the call like any statically chosen coercion. And at `:584-591` ("coercion is resolved statically"): these coercions are static too, and the rewritten call then dispatches on the converted values. The inference chapter that rung T writes (`Specification/basic/inference.tex`, today a note) states the promotion as the instantiation of the chosen declaration. Nothing else in the two chapters changes beyond answer 9's revision.
- **What keeps holding under reading A.** The Meet Rule (`Specification/advanced/overloading.tex:247-274`), the two uniqueness facts (`:449-470`) and their proof (`Specification/appendices/overloading-coercion.tex`; its lemma at `:75-96` assumes that no declaration applies without coercion). They all speak of applicability and specificity on declared domains, which reading A does not change. "Ambiguous coercion" belongs to the Σ′ phase, which a generic arm that holds the call on its declared domain never enters.
- **Measured.** The checker refuses the generic arm beside `op(a: ZZ32, b: Any)` alone as an invalid overloading ("Invalid overloading of op", `captures/O2MeetNo.stock.txt`, `.rule.txt`), as the Meet Rule says it must. With their meet `op(a: ZZ32, b: Number)` added, it accepts the set (`O2Meet`).
- **One thing reading A needs from the checker.** The checker ranks candidates on their *instantiated* domains (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/scala_src/useful/STypesUtil.scala:1068-1095`; the comparison at `:1094`), not on declared ones. The promoted instance `(ZZ64, ZZ64)` and the meet `(ZZ32, Number)` are incomparable under the specification's relation: position 1 favours the meet (`ZZ32` excludes `ZZ64`, converts into it, and rejects it, since `IntLiteral` excludes `ZZ64`), position 2 favours the generic (`ZZ64 <: Number`); `conversions-coercions.tex:486-516`. Today's union instance is just as incomparable with the meet. Stock, rule and opt2 all chose the meet arm, in both declaration orders (`O2Meet`, `O2MeetRev`). That answer comes from the sort's input order, not from a rule. On declared domains the meet lies inside the generic arm's domain (`T = Number`), so it wins by rule. So rung I must compare declared domains whenever a promoted candidate is ranked, as answer 9's text already asks.
- **Reading B.** The generic arm then applies outside its declared domain. The rules are checked on declared domains. For plain declarations, the Meet Rule already covers the types a declaration converts from (the sets S and T, `advanced/overloading.tex:252-274`). Nothing covers the types a generic arm reaches by promotion. One set that the checker accepts and walk loads shows the gap (`O2Tie`, every capture):
  - `g[\T extends ZZ64\](a: T, b: T)` beside `g(a: ZZ32, b: Any)`. The declared domains, `(ZZ64, ZZ64)` and `(ZZ32, Any)`, share no argument.
  - `g(z, w)` fits the plain arm as it is and the generic arm only by promotion (`T = ZZ64`, `z` converted).
  - Neither domain is more specific, whether the generic arm is read on its declared domain or on the promoted instance: position 1 favours the plain arm (`ZZ32` converts into `ZZ64` and rejects it), position 2 the generic arm (`ZZ64` below `Any`).
  - Stock, the rule, and walk in both modes run `g z32any[ ZZ32 , ZZ64 ]`, the arm applicable without coercion; reading A gives the same. opt2 runs `g generic[ ZZ64 , ZZ64 ]`: with no most specific candidate, its sort's input order picked the generic arm and converted `z`.
- Two other sets that reading B reaches resolve by the specification's coercion-aware relation, so they show no tie (`O2Excl`): `lo[\T extends ZZ64\]` beside `lo(a: ZZ32, b: ZZ64)` (the plain domain is the more specific: `ZZ32` converts into `ZZ64` and rejects it; opt2 runs the plain arm), and `two[\T extends ZZ64\]` beside `two[\U extends ZZ\]` under `two(a: Any, b: Any)` (the `ZZ64` arm is the more specific: `ZZ64` converts into `ZZ` and rejects it). The second still stops at run time under opt2 (check 7).
  - So reading B contradicts "among all applicable declarations, one is more specific than all the others" (`basic/overloading.tex:288-291`) and the uniqueness facts (`advanced/overloading.tex:455-463`). The proof's lemma, which needs no declaration to apply without coercion, has no case for promoted arms. A tie rule and a new lemma would be needed.

## 2. Static and dynamic agreement

- **A more specific arm that fits the converted values runs.** Take the generic arm beside `op(a: ZZ64, b: ZZ64)` (`O2Z64`). The rule and opt2 both choose the generic arm at `ZZ64` statically (by reading `rule.patch`: the plain arm needs a coercion, so it is not in the first attempt, which holds only the promoted generic). This is the specification's dynamic rule: the declarations applicable at run time are considered (`basic/overloading.tex:262-276`), and the dynamic choice is more specific than the static one (`advanced/overloading.tex:466-468`; the implementation strategy at `:72-77`). ## 3. A plain arm more specific in one position

**Verdict.** No contradiction under reading A. The overloading rules force a meet declaration, and the meet declaration wins. With `op(ZZ64, ZZ64)`, the plain arm runs at run time.

- **`op(a: ZZ32, b: Any)`.** Beside the generic arm alone, the checker refuses the pair as an invalid overloading (`O2MeetNo`). With the meet `op(a: ZZ32, b: Number)`, `op(z, w)` runs the meet arm, unconverted, on stock, rule and opt2, in both orders (`O2Meet`, `O2MeetRev`). No conversion happens and nothing is ambiguous: the Meet Rule is doing its job. Walk refuses the set at load in both modes, which is rung W's to lift.
- **Its F-bounded form** (`eqop[\T extends Equality[\T\]\]` beside `eqop(a: ZZ32, b: Any)`) is refused by the checker, with the meet `(ZZ32, ZZ32)` or without it (`O2FbMeetNo`, `O2FbMeet`, stock and rule). - **`op(a: ZZ64, b: ZZ64)`.** This plain arm is more specific on declared domains, and it equals the promoted instance. `op(z, w)` needs a conversion under either arm (`O2Z64`). Under reading A the generic arm is the only one applicable without coercion, so it is chosen and promoted. The converted call then dispatches to the plain arm at run time: measured on the rule and on opt2 (check 2). The coercion theorem is not involved, since Σ is not empty. Under option 1 both arms land in Σ′ with the same instance domain `(ZZ64, ZZ64)`. The specification compares Σ′ on parameter types after inference (`conversions-coercions.tex:542-548`), so the two tie there, and option 1 needs declared domains to break the tie. This is not a contradiction for option 2. It is the walk rule of check 2.

## 6. The speed premise

**Verdict.** It holds in part. The generic arm at `ZZ64` is a stamped copy called with no dispatch test. It is not unboxed, and option 2 adds a conversion per call.

- **What holds** (`captures/javap-O2Explicit.txt`, the option-2 call written out on the rule build). `op[\ZZ64\](z, w)` compiles to three steps:
  - a static field load of the class stamped for `ZZ64` (`O2Explicit⚙$op⟦FZZ64⟧…`);
  - a static call `coerce_ZZ64(FZZ32)` for `z`;
  - one interface call `apply(FZZ64, FZZ64)`.
  
  That class is the template `op⟦T⟧` with `T` renamed at load (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/runtimeSystem/InstantiatingClassloader.java:214-238`, `readAndExpandGenericThing`; FACTS, "Generic instantiations are stamped out at class-load time"). No run-time test chooses the arm.
- **The plain arm's cost.** It is reached through the overload's dispatcher `op(Any, Any)`: two `getRTTI` interface calls and up to three `runtimeSupertypeOf` tests, then a call to `op?(Any, Any)` (same capture).
- **What does not hold.**
  - The stamped class renames; it does not change representation (`explorations/repo-internals.md:131`). Its parameters are `FZZ64` references, and its body is the template's code. What that body does with a `T` was resolved against the bound when the template was checked. Nothing is unboxed.
  - Option 2 adds one conversion per call, an `FZZ64` made from the `FZZ32`.
  - The two arms run different bodies written by the programmer. Choosing an arm is not choosing between two compilations of one body.
  - What the stamped class does give is a call site that sees one type, which the JIT can inline.
- No timing was taken. None would decide between the options.

## What a judge would still have to decide

- **Which reading "option 2" is.**
  - Reading A is sound and needs the one sentence of check 1, the declared-domain ranking in rung I, and the walk steps of check 4. It leaves the library's F-bounded generics to the plain arm.
  - Reading B also reaches the F-bounded generics. It needs three things: a tie rule for calls where no candidate is most specific (`O2Tie`; for example, compare the arms that need no conversion alone); an extension of the Meet Rule's coercion clause to the types a generic arm reaches by promotion; and a new lemma for the appendix's proof.
  - Pavol's idea of 13:51 UTC ("if there is a conversion to a specific type, it should be applied", POSITIONS 2026-09-28) goes further than reading B. It would let any conversion reach a more specific arm, plain arms included. For example, `f(x: ZZ64)` beside `f(x: Number)` would take a `ZZ32` by conversion. That reverses the coercion order of `conversions-coercions.tex:472-476` for every overloaded call. The evidence of checks 1 and 7 bears on it in the same way.
- **What walk does when it chooses an arm at run time.** The specification resolves coercion statically (`conversions-coercions.tex:584-591`). Walk has only run-time types, and rung K's rule promotes at run time for every generic call (`same(n, w)`, check 2). Whether walk promotes from run-time types, or instantiates a run-time choice without conversion, is batch N's question for rung K under either option. Option 2's walk steps need its answer.
