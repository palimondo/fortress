## Timeline

1. Course correction, 23:04–23:33. Pavol rejected the previous page — "it fails utterly as a pedagogical explainer that should enlighten the transformer black box for people … you locked onto first BIG operand notation a just scaled it though de-risking ladder" — and, asked to choose, weighted transformer-teaching and notation equally and put the emphasis on the notation level. His formulation set the round's forcing function: "the ability to express the transformer in fortress as close as possible to the way how transformer or other building blocks of the microGPT are expressed in AI literature, like in a paper". The coordinator restated it as minimizing the distance between the Fortify-rendered definition and the literature's notation for each block, and named the concrete gap: the existing program stops at the index-formula level everywhere, while the papers' register for attention is vector/matrix level.

2. Design spec, 23:43–23:45. On Pavol's "Yep. Go.", the six-point spec was written into the design journal `explorations/microgpt-native.md` as the section "Course correction: the paper register" and committed as `e8526cc60`, so that the worker's brief could reference it verbatim rather than restating it.

3. Delegation and parallel page work, 23:45–23:50. The implementation worker was launched into a git worktree (`.claude/worktrees/agent-adbf76042b49fe58b`) with the journal section as its spec, `microgpt_native.fss` as its starting point, the accumulated syntax-trap list, and the three gates. While it ran, the coordinator drafted the braided page template with figure slots and built one render sheet it could build without the worker's output — `explorations/fortify/native-attend.tic`, the attention excerpt at the index-formula rung — committed as `7fe9e1ec3` when the stop hook flagged the untracked files.

4. Implementation, 23:45–00:05 (worker). The worker copied and transformed `microgpt_native.fss`, keeping the autodiff engine, golden values, tokenizer, Adam and sampling, and rewriting the notation layer and the model-level definitions. Gate 1 passed on the first attempt and on every later run; the delivered file gave 3.212 → 2.568 over 30 steps and 4.62 s/step against `microgpt_native`'s 4.81, on 1264 parameters — identical to the previous two programs, so the notation change moved no weights. Three render-driven iterations followed, each re-gated: dropping `konst` from softmax's shift, splitting `nll` so Fortify renders `p_y`, renaming `kh2`/`vh2` to `K`/`Vv` (which also removed a LaTeX "Double subscript" error that the worker verified was present in the already-committed `microgpt-native-forms.tic` too), and pushing the ZZ32→RR64 coercions down into the notation layer.

5. Integration and independent re-verification, 00:06–00:12. ## Gaps found

The run's gap table is the "Revival worklist extracted from this audit" in `explorations/expressiveness-review.md`: 8 rows — declaration-site covariance, coercion declarations, multifix operator dispatch, big operators as plain declarations, the algebraic-constraints library, `Vector`/`Matrix` unsealed from `Number`, static-argument inference, and dimensions and units — each with a spec citation, a probe under `explorations/spec-probes/`, and a payoff. It rests on 20 findings classified 6 WORKS-today, 8 SPEC-ONLY, 6 DELIBERATE, plus 3 places where the spec is silent and the library is the only authority; the implementation report contributes a further five syntax traps and five confirmed idioms. The merged ledger `explorations/fortress-gap-ledger.md` did not exist at the time (created 2026-09-08, commit `b9c8ee33e`), so none of these rows was in it; all were new.

## Findings

The round was steered by a rejection rather than by a plan: the design spec exists because Pavol described a forcing function — the rendered Fortress definition should sit as close as possible to the literature's formula — and the coordinator wrote that spec into the design journal and then briefed a worker by reference to it, so the worker's authority was a committed document rather than a paraphrase in a prompt. The split of work put the taste decisions (which notation, which page structure) in the coordinator and the mechanical decisions (which spellings the interpreter accepts) in the worker, and the worker's brief anticipated the main risk by telling it in advance that a failed overload "is a result we want documented" — which is what happened, and the failure became the round's headline finding. Verification was doubled rather than trusted: the coordinator re-ran the golden check and the training on the integrated file before committing, and again after the audit fold. The round's own weakness was found by its own third worker: across the whole microgpt design phase the specification was consulted zero times against 106 interpreter runs and 29 library reads, and two of the dead ends it had routed around — the vector×matrix overload and the `SUM` closure — had sanctioned escapes in chapters nobody opened, while an inherited parse rule turned out never to have been reproduced. Time went almost entirely into the notation contests and their re-gating; the engine, the goldens and the training loop were carried over from the previous program unchanged, which is why gate 1 passed on the first attempt and why the parameter count stayed at 1264 across all three programs. The relation of result to effort is visible in the timing row: the paper-register layer costs nothing at runtime (4.62 s/step against 4.81), so the entire round's cost was notation work and its verification, and the review it triggered produced more durable material — 20 spec-cited findings and 17 probes — than the rewrite itself.
