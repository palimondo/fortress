## 1. What the specification settles, and how

For a numeral operand the specification settles the answer, and the skeptic's derivation is right:

- `literals.tex:132` numerals "are not directly converted to any of the number types"; `:142-148` the libraries "define coercions from numerals to integers (for simple numerals) and rational numbers (for compound numerals)"; `:162-163` "Numerals containing a radix point are actually rational literals; thus 3.125 has the rational value 3125/1000".
- `lexical-structure.tex:1137-1138`: a numeral is compound if it contains a `.`.
- `numbers.tex:461` declares `round(self): ℤ` on ℚ, and `:470-472` is the half-to-even rule stated of "this rational number". The chapter has one section, "Rational Numbers" (`:16`), and no chapter covers the float types (row 329's own text; ledger `:340`).

So `round(2.50000000000000001)` is 3, `round(0.50000000000000001)` is 1, `round(3.49999999999999999)` is 3 and `round(2.49999999999999999)` is 2. The interpreter's own rational body gives exactly these on the same values written as quotients (`probes/skeptic/SkNearTie.txt:21-24`). The sentences are identical in `Specification-1.0-frozen/basic/expressions/literals.tex:148`, `:162`, `:170`.

One passage neither party cited had to be checked, because it is the only one that could pull the numeral back to a double: `literals.tex:170-173`, "If such static expressions are mentioned as part of a floating-point computation, the compiler performs the rational arithmetic exactly and then converts the result to a floating-point value, thus incurring at most one floating-point rounding error." It does not reach `round(numeral)`: the only `round` the specification defines that applies to a rational is ℚ's (`numbers.tex:461`), no floating-point operand is present, and the prose has no float `round` at all. It does reach `round(x + 0.5)` with `x` a float, which is the class rung R repaired and which this passage leaves exactly where the rung put it.

For a `Float` or `RR32` value the prose is silent on the `round` method, and the ground is Pavol's decision at `POSITIONS.md:49` ("Agreed" to half to even on both paths), with `opr-overview.tex:178-179` and `:211-212` (IEEE 754 round-to-nearest for the arithmetic operators) as corroboration. That is the split the skeptic asked the report to state, and it is the right one.

## 2. Where this ruling goes further than the skeptic: the mechanism and the fix

The skeptic locates the defect at "the numeral's conversion" (`FortressLibrary.fss:422` over `FFloatLiteral.getFloat`, `FFloatLiteral.java:39-40`) and names one fix, `FloatLiteral`'s four methods over the exact decimal. Both statements are true and both are too narrow. The library declares the numeral a floating-point value: `object FloatLiteral extends RR64` (`FortressBuiltin.fss:194`) and `trait RR64 extends Number comprises { Float, FloatLiteral, RR32, QQ }` (`FortressLibrary.fss:425`, `.fsi:338`; map `:378`). The interpreter has no coercion mechanism at all (map `:288`: "no occurrence of Coercion or coerce anywhere under interpreter/"), so the coercion to ℚ that `literals.tex:146-148` describes cannot exist in `walk` in the form the specification describes. The compiler prelude has no ℚ (zero occurrences of `QQ` in `CompilerBuiltin.fsi` and `CompilerLibrary.fsi`), declares `trait FloatLiteral excludes {RR32, RR64}` (`CompilerBuiltin.fss:991`) and coerces it to `RR64` (`:927`). The numeral types of `literals.tex:83-131` (`NaturalNumeral`, `RadixPointNumeral`) occur nowhere under `ProjectFortress/src`, `Library` or `LibraryBuiltin`; row 325 (ledger `:336`) already records the integer half of that absence.

So the defect is the compound-numeral half of a design-level gap: on both paths a numeral with a radix point is a floating-point value, not the rational the specification makes it. The four integer-valued methods are where it is visible without a comparison of digits. The skeptic's fix is one candidate among three, and it carries a cost the skeptic did not state: after it, `round(2.50000000000000001)` is 3 while `2.50000000000000001 = 2.5` stays true (`FFloatLiteral.seqv`, `FFloatLiteral.java:42-52`, compares the double's bits) and `2.50000000000000001 - 2.5` stays `0.0` (`trait Number`'s arithmetic over `asFloat`, `FortressLibrary.fss:405-421`). One value would round as a rational and do everything else as a double. The candidates and costs are in section 5; the row opens with the fork stated, not with one fix asserted. The XXX gate is right under either repairing candidate, because both make the four assertions pass (the design candidate through the rational body, which `SkNearTie.txt:21-24` already shows giving the four answers).
