# Where the design intent is written down

The specification says what the language is, not why; the why sits in five places, and this file says which place holds which area.

The five places: `Papers/` (five in-repo paper directories, four of them buildable); the Guy Steele corpus indexed by `research/README.md` (links, plus per-session local copies under the gitignored `research/decks/`); `research/extracts/` (our committed working notes on those sources); the specification's own draft-only apparatus (the `\note{}` boxes and the Internal Document appendix, both suppressed in release builds); and the source tree itself (tool docs, long design comments, aspirational test directories, commit messages).

Two structural facts to carry into the rest of this file.

The rationale that is in the specification is in the *draft* build only: `Specification/fortress/fortress.tex:24-37` defines `\note` as empty when `\release` is set, and `Specification/appendices/appendices.tex:24-26` includes the Internal Document chapter only in the non-release build; "Internal Document" appears 0 times in the text of the published `Specification-1.0-frozen/fortress.1.0.pdf` and twice in the in-repo draft render `Specification/fortress.pdf` (599 pages), which also carries 36 occurrences of the editorial "Victor:" notes that the 1.0 PDF has none of.

The deepest layer of the design record is *not* in the repository: 27 `\note{}` passages point at Sun-internal email threads by subject and date (`grep -rn 'email titled' Specification --include='*.tex'`, e.g. `Specification/appendices/future.tex:65`, `Specification/basic/conversions-coercions.tex:28`, `Specification/basic/evaluation/parallelism.tex:17`), and `research/README.md:103-105` records that no archive of the project mailing lists was found anywhere as of 2026-08-21.

## 4. The specification itself

Searching the LaTeX for the word "rationale" finds exactly one passage, and it is about component upgrade, not about the language: `Specification/basic/components/basic.tex:372`.

The rationale the spec does carry is in four other forms.

`Specification/preliminaries/intro/philosophy.tex:12-40` is the design-goals statement: a growable language, "secure Fortran", the list of languages Fortress borrowed from; `Specification/preliminaries/intro/nutshell.tex:88-95` is the one place the spec explicitly motivates a design decision ("These design decisions are motivated in part by our goal of making the scientist/programmer's life as easy as possible…").

The 230 `\note{}` boxes are the implementers' margin: they are draft-only, and they range from status ("not yet supported") to open argument. Distribution by file, highest first: `basic/lexical-structure.tex` 19, `basic/traits.tex` 15, `basic/types-vals-vars.tex` 14, `basic/memory-model.tex` 9, `basic/expressions/literals.tex` 9, `basic/declarations.tex` 9, `basic/components/source-code.tex` 9, `basic/functions.tex` 7, `basic/components/intro.tex` 7, `basic/objects.tex` 6, `advanced/parallelism-locality/defining-generators.tex` 6, `preliminaries/overview.tex` 5, `basic/tests.tex` 5, `basic/dimensions.tex` 5, then 4 or fewer in `basic/matrix-unpasting.tex`, `basic/expressions/typecase.tex`, `basic/evaluation/parallelism.tex`, `basic/conversions-coercions.tex`, `basic/trait-parameters.tex`, `basic/overloading.tex`, `basic/operators/opr-fixity.tex`, `basic/evaluation/values.tex`, `basic/evaluation/reduction.tex`, `appendices/ascii-to-unicode.tex`, `advanced/parallelism-locality/shared-local.tex`, `advanced/overloading.tex`, and others.

The substantive ones, as samples: `basic/memory-model.tex:439-441` ("The following is controversial. I think this is effectively the condition the X10 guys are trying to capture formally…") and `:376-379` ("The following doesn't fully address Bill Pugh's concerns…"); `basic/dimensions.tex:244-254`, Guy's explanation that unit information is static and is omitted from the run-time representation exactly when type information is; `basic/conversions-coercions.tex:19-37`, an open-questions list for coercion including tests, tuple and arrow types; `basic/traits.tex:174-182` and `:195-197`, Victor's observation that a trait can explicitly extend itself through hidden type variables.

`Specification/appendices/internal-document.tex` is the spec's own rationale appendix, included only in draft builds, and it inputs two files: `appendices/FAQ.tex` (235 lines of answered design questions — why multiple dispatch rather than single, `:59`; why spawn counts as IO, `:51`; core versus standard library, `:121`; why not single inheritance between objects, `:136`; juxtaposition as the only non-ALLCAPS operator name, `:90`) and `appendices/future.tex` (756 lines of proposed features organised as Syntax and Evaluation, Types, Traits and Objects, Functions and Overloading, Expressions, Operators and Coercions, Exceptions, Components and APIs, Others, Tools — largely a digest of meetings and emails, with the participants named and their positions recorded, e.g. the array/tensor discussion at `:63-76`).

The formal apparatus is the fourth form: `Specification/appendices/calculi/` holds four core calculi (basic, `where`, overloading, acffd — `appendices/calculi/calculi.tex:18-21`), and `appendices/overloading-function.tex:12` and `appendices/overloading-coercion.tex:12` are the proofs that the overloading rules admit no ambiguous or undefined call, and that coercion resolution is well defined.

`Specification-1.0-frozen/appendices/FAQ.tex` and `future.tex` are byte-identical to the draft's (diff), so the frozen tree adds nothing here; what differs is that the release build hides them.

`Documentation/Specification/` is a second, later, partial spec source tree in Fortify `.tick` form (build instructions at `Documentation/Specification/Prose/README.txt:12-24`, directory roles at `Documentation/Specification/README.txt:13-25`), carrying 35 `\note{}` passages of its own; its committed render `Documentation/Specification/fortress.pdf` is 65 pages, built 2012-05-31, against the draft tree's 599.

## 5. Design notes inside the source tree

Prose documents that state design, not usage: `Fortify/fortify-doc.txt` (489 lines, the Emacs-based ASCII-to-typeset conversion and the identifier-to-symbol rules) with `Fortify/example/README`; `ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/parser/parser-doc.txt` (98 lines, the Rats! module structure of the grammar, with NYI markers); `ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/syntax_abstractions/productionsAndAst.txt` (127 lines, which productions a user grammar may extend — "Only those marked \"x\" can be extended currently"); `ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/parser_util/precedence_resolver/operators.txt` (1,093 lines, the operator groups by meaning and shape that the precedence relation is built from); `ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/linker/README` (the linker's purpose and file-by-file map); `BasicCoreFortress/README.txt` (how to run the core-calculus interpreter); root `README.txt` (the Subversion-era orientation, describing a project layout that no longer holds).

Long comments that explain a design choice rather than a mechanism, found by scanning every `/* … */` block of 14 lines or more under `ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/{compiler,scala_src,runtimeSystem,interpreter}`: `runtimeSystem/BaseTask.java:26-35` (why a task is not forked in its constructor — "Given the plethora of final fields in the usual Fortress task, such a step would be a memory model disaster!"); `compiler/NamingCzar.java:264-289` (the six-level ladder from a Fortress name to an ASM type, and the two translations needed for foreign interfaces); `compiler/codegen/CodeGen.java:869-906` (why forwarding methods are generated for every trait after the first, and the normalized-inheritance assumption it rests on), `:1015-1049`, `:1147-1183`, `:2313-2340`, `:5161-5196` (RTTI classes); `runtimeSystem/InstantiatingClassloader.java:56-58` ("This code steals willy-nilly from the NextGen class loader"), `:532-552`, `:690-721`, `:935-957` (how arrow and tuple interfaces are synthesized on demand), `:1181-1202`; `runtimeSystem/Naming.java:13-16` (why this class must not depend on the compiler); `runtimeSystem/InstantiationMap.java:195-224` (the name-rewriting state machine); `interpreter/evaluator/BuildEnvironments.java:31-68` ("This comment is not yet true; it is a goal."); `interpreter/evaluator/transactions/manager/KarmaManager.java:17-37` and `EruptionManager.java:17-42` (two of eleven contention-management policies, each with its heuristic explained).

Only one comment in the whole source tree uses the word "rationale" (`interpreter/glue/prim/Reflect.java:333`), and only four say "we chose / we decided / the reason" in any form (`interpreter/evaluator/transactions/manager/FortressManager5.java:22`, `interpreter/evaluator/Evaluator.java:849`, `compiler/codegen/CodeGen.java:2206`, plus the Reflect one); intent in this tree is expressed as mechanism, and 556 `TODO` markers carry what is left of the "not yet" record.

The aspirational test directories are themselves a statement of intent — what was meant to work and did not: `ProjectFortress/not_passing_yet` (62 files, e.g. `WhereConstraints.fss`, `UnitExpr.fss`, `Expr.Array.Pasting.fss`, `XXXextendOprParam1.fss`), `long_term_not_working` (6: abstract, closures, fields, inheritance, overriding, wordcount), `not_working_static_tests` (43), `not_working_library_tests` (29), `not_working_compiler_tests` (10), `obsolete_interpreter_tests` (3).

Commit messages: sampled with `git log --grep` over the historical era (`--until=2013-01-01`), they are overwhelmingly implementation logs, and design statements appear in them only in passing — the clearest samples are `128f313b5` (2009-11-17, jmaessen: coercion of `IntLiteral` by `ZZ32`, "as we gradually migrate to a flat numeric hierarchy"), `8e3968ea7` (2009-11-11, skilpat: coercions from union types "as per Guy's blog post" — the blog post is not in the repo), `c35aac139` (2012-04-24, David Chase: "Preliminary disabling of contravariance (in overloaded dispatch)", the retreat the JuliaCon deck describes at slide 44), `32153798d` (2008-04-27, jmaessen: a juxtaposition/type-inference fix that "opened a rift in space-time"), `821e29621` (2008-02-07: BIG operators implemented, "'BIG juxtaposition' is PROD not PRODUCT").

Caveat on that sampling: path-scoped history is unreliable in this repository — `research/authorship.md:14-22` records that the hg→git conversion severed 146 parent links, so `git log -- <path>` dead-ends (it reports one commit for `Papers/`), while `git log --full-history --all -- <path>` over-reports (404 commits for `Papers/`, most of which do not touch it). The `--grep` samples above walk all commits and are sound as samples; they are not an exhaustive search.
