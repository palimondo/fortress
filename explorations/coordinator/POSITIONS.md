# Pavol's positions, decisions and known ground

## The goal

- P-7 **The goal is to finish what the designers intended, measured by one program, microGPT, compiled to bytecode and running fast.** The judge is `Specification/`, the Working Draft of February 2011 as the revival revises it, with the 2012 Types chapter cited beside it (P-25). The language is not redesigned. The specification is a living document that states the revival's changes (P-95). The floor to measure against is a pure-Java microGPT, not yet written (PLAN phase 6).
- P-8 **Where the designers left no word, he decides by the goal, a compiled microGPT running fast, not by type-theoretic purity, and stays true to the spirit of Fortress.** He accepts the language's unfinished corners rather than stall in theory. He has not read the specification, the type papers or the library, and takes the designers to have known more than he does. So a review checks his decisions against their intent, for errors of commission and of omission; its items are decisions in this file. Detail: `reviews/decisions-review/judgement.md`, "In short".
- P-9 **The search is for microGPT's most readable expression.** In his words, "we are searching for the optimal expression of the microGPT algorithm for readability." The two sources are Fortress's mathematical notation and Iverson's APL notation, with Hsu's data design for data-flow algorithms. The flat program in Hsu's style, C4, fell short, so polishing happens in the vocabulary, the operations the model is written in. C4 is the APL program's target form. Its hyperparameters are tuple bindings, the form an APL strand assignment expands to; the semicolon form of those lines is set aside.
- P-10 **The APL side quest is closed: its remaining ground, tacit functions, characters and nested arrays, is not pursued.** Its sub-language expands APL to Fortress code over native arrays, and its APL microGPT program stays in the walk check (P-30).
- P-11 **The 2D notation is a gimmick: no work on it unless he asks.** Fortify's mathematical rendering bakes one view of a computation into the syntax, though the same mathematics can be written many ways. APL's one glyph for each transformation does that better. It is presentation only, off the path, and no argument for Fortress.
- P-12 **The type checker on interpreter programs comes after the switch-over.** He wants the checker usable on the programs that walk runs, and sees it as low-hanging fruit. Today it checks only against the compiler's prelude (F-9). It comes once walk also takes the static coercions that the checker inserts (P-59).

## History he knows (do not re-explain)

- P-16 **He reads the project's end as the price of doing no work on performance.** DARPA's HPCS did not take Sun into its third phase in 2006, and the project went on at Sun Labs until 2012. The team worked on syntax, and the compiler that was to make the mathematical types fast never came; the funding was cut for good reason.
- P-17 **Unboxed doubles and arrays of doubles are required: without them the revival misses its point.** The code generator boxes every number, and boxing is the main cost on microGPT's array kernels (F-11). Boxing is measured on microGPT-shaped kernels, not on a scalar loop with literals and a `:=` accumulator, which microGPT never runs. The storage is P-84's decision; unboxed arithmetic is PLAN phase 6 (P-33).

- P-19 **The tree is not described as the third-party port's migration grafted on its history.** The files that still say so wait for the lineage cleanup (PLAN, "Off the path, parked"), which `coordinator/lineage.md`, "Files that still repeat it", lists. ## Decisions on record

### The standard and the sources

- P-25 **The specification stays the standard, the Types chapter beside it.** Where the Types chapter of the team's unfinished 2012 restart (F-166) covers a topic, it is cited beside `Specification/` as the designers' later word. On the rules that the revival revises, it is their latest intent, which the goal is to finish. It covers the exclusion rule, as instantiation exclusion in its two-part form, the `covariant` modifier, and `Bottom`, which no program can write.
- P-26 **The type group's late positions outweigh the specification's early text where they conflict.** The early text was a wish. The type group then made the language run, for performance and with specialization a must, and learned what is feasible. So their later word wins: the exclusion rule, specialization kept, the compiler prelude's tower flattened in 2009 and 2010 (P-43), Naden's 2012 write-up, the POPL 2019 paper. The implementers' later word also outweighs unfinished text. The revival does not second-guess them: it knows less than they did.
- P-27 **The library's own practice is the standard.** What microGPT invented goes into the standard library, in the library's own style. Whoever extends the library first studies how it does the same kind of thing, and follows that pattern; exclusion, for one, is all over it. He had to find the diagonal's override himself (P-88) because that study was skipped. No fix is designed from the specification alone or from a workaround on the user's side. A fork put to him names the library's own way first, and a fork resting on a rule that refuses something says what the library does instead, by protocol principle 2 (moves to the coordinator skill's planning part).
- P-28 **Fleshing out the standard library is a very important task.** Next is the algebraic layer: `Ring`, `Field` and what the matrices and tensors of machine learning need, so that the library's traits reach them. It goes into the standard library itself (P-27), not into a tree of the revival's own.
- P-29 **Walk's speed is irrelevant to every judgement of Fortress's performance.** Walk is hundreds of times slower than Python's interpreter. So no performance decision of any kind, and no edit of C4, the flat model program, for speed, until microGPT runs compiled. The gate never runs the two microGPT programs, too long-running to be tests: it compiles them and records the phase that each reaches. A run of them, once possible, goes to a stage that does not gate.
- P-30 **The microGPT walk check after a batch runs in under a minute.** It runs about two forward-and-backward passes against the reference values, which shows that the model's mathematics still works and that the library's changes did not break it. The full check programs run only when asked. A slower check is to him a waste of time.
- P-31 **Development-loop performance is a first-class concern.** When an iteration waits too long on something, find the cause and fix it.
- P-32 **The territory map (`coordinator/map/`) maps the tree down to where the design intent is written, in the papers as well as the specification.** It exists so that nothing on record is found again from scratch. The boot order is followed as written, `INDEX.md` included (moves to the coordinator skill's core).
- P-33 **The plan (`coordinator/PLAN.md`) has six phases, approved with their workers.** Phases 1 and 2 are landed: the wrapping operators, the revised specification, the run-time sizes, the flat number tower and the overflow natives. 3 is the checker at a true zero; 4, the switch-over, natives then names; 5, microGPT compiles: its static types, the array design and the code generator's holes; 6, microGPT fast. Where the work stands: PLAN's "Where we stand". Re-approval row 41 (ledger row 320: a component named apart from its api does not link) joins the switch-over's design. His answers come in the plan's order; the re-approval rows and the other off-path items are parked until relevant (moves to the coordinator skill's planning part).

### The one library and the compiled checker

- P-37 **One library, the interpreter's, is the library the compiler checks.** The interpreter's `FortressLibrary` and `FortressBuiltin` become the compiled path's library. He sees the compiler's prelude (`CompilerLibrary`, `CompilerBuiltin`, `CompilerAlgebra`) as a bootstrap to remove, so no declaration goes into it, and its three files are deleted at the switch-over with their tests kept. Code-generator rungs go on. How the checker comes to accept the library is a plan shown to him step by step, not a question to ask again. Still to build: PLAN phase 4.
- P-38 **The compiler prelude's defects are not repaired: they go with it at the switch-over.** Its `Maybe` block and its `REM` guard `if other = -1 then 0` stay as landed until the prelude is deleted, and their tests stay. Then the compiled tests respell its reversed wrapping and saturating operators (row 348), `ZZ64`'s compiled shift closes (row 381), and the specification's `shift` reaches the compiled path (row 383). `XXXShiftDeclRungI` is then promoted, and the switch-over batch finds the compiler tests that bind `shift`, a reserved name from then on. Still to build: PLAN phase 4.
- P-39 **The checker count is measured in every gate and never turns the gate red.** The gate reports the count stage on its own. The distance to the switch-over (F-53), over all apis and components, is a report-only stage of the phase-3 gates, whose work drives it down.
- P-40 **Sizes are checked, and a size left unknown is an error only where it is used.** The checker checks `nat` and `int` static parameters and refuses a size beyond `NN32` or `ZZ32` (F-140). Storage refuses a JVM array length over 2^31-1. A size that inference leaves unknown is an error only when it reaches a type, a name or a value. How the checker represents it is not his choice. A size that a call cannot fix refuses the call, also inside an overload set, instead of dropping the arm (row 400, its compiled half open). Every static argument but an operator counts in the exclusion rule; booleans join once the checker has boolean parameters (row 406). A size used as a value converts to `ZZ32` as a numeral does (row 485). The api's 25 unused sizes (F-35) go only by a diff shown to him with the switch-over's design.
- P-41 **At run time a size travels as the descriptor that a type argument has (design B).** It fills the slot that the team's code generator reserved. Each number has one descriptor, from a table, with no holder class, as the team instantiates types. Operator static arguments stay compile-time names (F-39): he sees no need to unify them.
- P-42 **An unbounded type parameter's implicit bound is `Any`, not `Object`.** `Any` holds tuples, functions and `()`, which `Object` does not, and the library instantiates its generic containers at tuples, arrays indexed by pairs among them. The specification states the bound, with a callout that keeps the Working Draft's `Object`. The compile path's `Object` bound departs from it until the switch-over (row 412).
- P-43 **The exclusion rule stays, and the number tower is flat (route A).** The compiled checker keeps the multiple instantiation exclusion rule. So the library's numbers are flat, as the team (Chase, Ryu, Maessen and Allen) flattened the compiler prelude in 2009 and 2010. The fixed widths, `ZZ`, `QQ` and `RR64` are siblings under `Number`, each with its own algebra, and `Number` has no catch-all operators. Why: the flattened prelude is the team's last record of the Fortress way, and its route leads to the POPL 2019 paper, which keeps the rule. He cares that specialised code runs fast, not how a specialisation is chosen. F-46 prices a return to route C.
- P-44 **`AnyIntegral`'s closure stands, and the checker reads `comprises` the 2012 way.** The closure `AnyIntegral comprises { ... }` stands, with a `NOT YET` comment in the team's wording. So do the generic scalar-extension block, the `AnyAdditiveGroup` marker and the `Array3` exclusion. A generic child of a closed trait may extend it when every type that the checker knows below it is listed. The clause's text and walk stay as they are. Row 487 records the hole that every reading but the 2008 one shares: a program's own `Integral` subtype in another component is not caught.
- P-45 **Overloads may differ in their static parameters (answer 9).** The specification drops its sentence that forbids it and states the type group's 2011 model, which the checker runs; the old sentence stays as superseded text. The checker keeps the paper's three overloading rules, checks the Return Type Rule over every instance, and requires two generic declarations in the more-specific relation to agree on their static parameters position by position, as Java's overriding rule does (row 398). Walk dispatches on declared domains, not by the order of the declarations. The library repairs its refused families with its own exclusions, and no model line changes. The judgement's smaller choices took the record's defaults, listed for his review in `PLAN.md`.
- P-46 **A conversion makes a call possible, and never changes which declaration runs when one already fits.** Declarations are chosen on declared domains, by applicability and then specificity (P-45). The chosen one is instantiated by answer 8's promotion (P-74), its coercions inserted statically. So beside a plain arm over `Any`, a generic arm over `T extends Number` runs at `ZZ64` for a mixed-width call. A numeral whose candidate types are incomparable reads as `ZZ32`, or as `ZZ64` or `ZZ` by its magnitude. Why: generic code is never fast, and he wants code that a JVM can specialise. His own broader rule, to convert toward a specific declaration before a generic one, is not taken. Both paths infer static arguments with coercion and the same promotion, and P-49 orders the checker's attempts. Still open: the dispatch between two generic declarations (row 537, PLAN phase 5).
- P-47 **Each integer type declares its own `MIN`, `MAX` and `MINMAX`.** It follows `QQ`, `RR64`, the compiler library and the specification's `ZZ`, so that under walk the family answers at the promoted type. Row 484's `NN32` half, a numeral beside an `NN32`, is still open.
- P-48 **A type parameter that a call's arguments do not fix takes its bound, never `Bottom`.** Its instance is the intersection of its upper bounds, constrained by the call's static return type, never the arguments' union or the value's type alone. The rule is the team's later word (POPL 2019). He ranks the team's later work above his own reading. It sets the instance, not which declaration runs (P-46). MicroGPT's types are concrete, so it is untouched. The library keeps the team's unbounded declarations. Still to build: a compiled dispatcher that carries the static return type (row 538, PLAN phase 5).
- P-49 **The checker tries a call without the expected type first, and with coercion only after (row 508).** It keeps the first attempt, by subtyping, when its result converts to the expected type, and only then tries coercion with the context. So a declaration that fits the arguments as they are is not passed over for one reached by coercion.
- P-50 **A `label` body takes the expected type of the whole `label` (item 48 (a)).** The specification types a `label` as the union of its body's last expression and its exits' `with` values. So the checker passes the expected type of the whole into the body and into the exits' values, as for a `typecase` clause, and the inference chapter lists both contexts. P-51 is the other half of item 48. Row 642 stays `CONTESTED` only because the ledger's tool cannot close it.
- P-51 **A call written as another call's argument is retried against each declaration's parameter type (item 48 (b), row 455).** When the checker's attempts at a call keep none, an argument that is a call with inferred static arguments is checked again, with each candidate's parameter type as its expected type. The trigger is wide: any inferred static argument, not only a result-only type parameter. Why: it makes the inference rules one rule everywhere, past the cases the team built. Its probe moves no error of the distance. Its rung promotes the expected failures `XXXInferContextDrops` and `XXXInferLoneUnionClosedTrait`, whose refusals it repairs. Still to build: the checker rung in PLAN's batch 14.
- P-52 **A range of rank 2 or 3 checks containment corner by corner (item 50, row 657).** The bounds check behind `narrowToRange` compares the other range's bounds by the ranges' point-by-point order, `PCMP`, not by the tuple order, which serves sorting. So `((0,0):(9,9)).narrowToRange((2,-1):(5,5))` raises `IndexOutOfBounds`.
- P-53 **`String`'s `left` and `right` answer `Just(c)` (item 42, row 606).** For a non-empty string they return `Just` of the first and of the last character, as their declared `Maybe[\Char\]` and every other `left` and `right` of the library do. So walk prints `Just` of the character, not the character.
- P-54 **`QQ`'s `ceiling` and `truncate` keep `ZZ` and raise at +∞, −∞ and 0/0 (item 44, row 635).** The declared integer result and the peer methods win over the specification's paragraph that returns the argument at an infinity, and that paragraph is revised. `floor`, `round` and the two brackets raise too. A rung after the switch-over makes `QQ` follow the float ruling (row 330): the methods keep the type, and the brackets, `round` and `truncate` give ℤ.
- P-55 **A size may hold arithmetic, compared by its written form once numerals are folded (item 15, 15a with (b)).** The checker accepts a sum, difference, product or power in a size. `2 3` folds to `6`, but `s0 s1` is not `s1 s0`. The class loader computes the value when it makes a class. A size name may equal a numeral, as the team's Q&A answers, so the `N[\0\]` arm of a `typecase N[\b0\]` is reachable. The library keeps the team's sizes written as products and sums. Two later array forks stand at defaults while his answers are pending: fork 3, a size known only at run time, at way 4a, where his reading of P-40 is asked; and fork 2, the bound of `Vector` and `Matrix`, at the two-trait bound (PLAN items 13 and 52).
- P-56 **The open range `(:)` keeps its wildcard type, and its five cutting methods fail (item 49, row 656).** `TrivialOpenRange` stays `OpenRange[\Any\]`. A range over `ZZ32` is not a range over `Any`, so no body can type its `truncL`, `truncR`, `every`, `imposeStride` and `atMost`. They are `fail` bodies with a message, the team's own device for such a body. Nothing in the tree calls them on `(:)`. Way (c), `(:)` over `ZZ32`, is not taken: probe P3 found it changes 64 small programs' values.
- P-57 **`LexicographicReduction.isLeftZero` takes `TotalComparison`, so the team's answers are reached (row 582).** The team's overload `isLeftZero(_:Comparison)` is declared over `TotalComparison`, so the inherited `ReductionWithZeroes.isLeftZero` no longer shadows it. `LessThan` and `GreaterThan` answer `true`, and `EqualTo` `false`. `isLeftZero(Unordered)` is refused under walk. Nothing calls `isLeftZero`. Why: it cleared the count stage's last api error (F-53).
- P-58 **Every native binding is a `builtinPrimitive` call naming a static helper (item 35).** Each binding stays the library's own form, a body that is exactly `builtinPrimitive("…")`, and its string names a static helper of `nativeHelpers/`, not an interpreter glue class. Walk loads the string through a loader beside its glue classes. The compiled path rewrites each body at the parse into a synthetic `import java` and a call before the checker, and refuses a string that names no helper. Way 2, `import java` blocks in the library, is not taken. Still to build: the natives rungs, PLAN phase 4.
- P-59 **Walk chooses coercions on the value until the switch-over.** Walk has no static types, so it chooses a coercion by the value, where the specification and the compiled path resolve it statically. This is accepted as the interpreter's limit (item 16), stated in one callout of the coercion chapter, with `XXXCoercionStaticRungC` and `XXXCoercionStaticNarrowRungC` as its record. After the switch-over walk takes the static coercions that the checker inserts. MicroGPT's static types are concrete, so both paths agree on it.
- P-60 **The specification reads a `comprises` clause at the level of values (PLAN item 26, row 491).** "Covers" is defined once, and the Meet Rule has a closed-trait case. A call whose argument sits between closed traits is typed by the intersection of its candidates' return types, with run-time uniqueness in place of static. Why: the proof addendum (`reviews/comprises-type-level-proof-addendum.md`) shows this sound for ground declarations, `G[\ZZ32\]` among them, with the coverage search local to overload checking. A generic declaration that needs a run-time instance is outside it. The specification states that boundary. The checker's condition is narrower, no static parameters in the whole family (row 543), and walk trusts the clauses' closure unchecked (row 551), both listed for his review.
- P-61 **The separate analysis session's scope.** Its contributions are reviews and their documentation commits on a branch of their own from `main`, not an implementation batch or a change to the agreed route.
- P-62 **The `covariant` keyword is future work, off the path to microGPT.** Row 404 records the hole: the checker accepts variance, and neither path carries it to run time. Its expected-failure tests are owed in a batch rung. The ledger's worklist item 12 is declaration-site variance, carried to run time on both paths. When it lands, the specification's sign-refined number types come back as covariant static parameters.

### Numbers

- P-66 **The integer principle: Java's and the JVM's defaults stay where they make sense, and are corrected where they fail high performance or mathematical precision.** The team did so with rounding and the sign of `GCD`. A hardware behaviour that bytecode can express gets its own explicit spelling, as the specification's wrapping and saturating operators do. Where the JVM prevents a correct implementation, the project gives way for now. The peers to compare are Julia, X10, Swift and Rust.
- P-67 **`round` on an exact half goes to the even integer, on both paths.** It is the specification's rule for rationals, IEEE's default and the team's own rational body. Built.
- P-68 **`floor` and `ceiling` on the float types keep the float; ⌊x⌋, ⌈x⌉, `round` and `truncate` return the unbounded ℤ.** The library's own `truncate` and `round` are tidied to match. Still to build: a library rung after the switch-over, which first counts the interpreter tests whose output changes (row 330).
- P-69 **`LSHIFT`/`RSHIFT` are bit operators on the fixed widths; `shift` is exact on ℤ.** A count at or beyond the width gives 0 or the sign, and a negative count reverses. Shifted-out bits fall silently, as in every peer language. Compiled code shifts the machine word, never through ℤ. A count may have any integral width (row 380). Built on both paths; the compiled path gets `shift` and the any-width count at the switch-over (P-38).
- P-70 **`GCD` and `LCM` give nonnegative results, and `LCM` raises an overflow error when the multiple does not fit, on both paths.** The specification says so. Built (row 334).
- P-71 **`|0|`, `-0` and `0 DIV -1` do not overflow: an overflow guard tests for the most negative value itself, not for `a = -a`.** The compiled natives and their siblings with that guard are fixed. Built (row 333).
- P-72 **`narrow` out of range truncates to the low 32 bits, signed and unsigned, on both paths.** The team's boundary test `tests/UnsignedTest.fss` asserts it. Built (row 346).
- P-73 **Fixed-width overflow raises `IntegerOverflow` under `walk`, as the specification says and the compiled path does, `NN32` and `NN64` included.** Code that means to wrap uses the specification's operators ∔, ∸ and ⨰, fully parenthesised, since their precedence is stated only among themselves. The saturating operators wait for a step of their own. Built (row 379).
- P-74 **Mixed integer widths convert by declared coercions (answer 8, to PLAN item 8): each wider type declares a `coerce` from each narrower one, as the compiler prelude does.** An exact conversion is implicit and a lossy one explicit: `ZZ32` and integer numerals coerce into `RR64`, and `ZZ64` into `RR64` stays explicit (P-66). A generic call or a range over mixed widths infers the narrowest type that both sides coerce into. The specification's `widens` is deferred. Built; no model line changed.
- P-75 **`NN32` coerces into `RR64`, since the conversion is exact (P-74).** Not built: with that coercion, a `ZZ32` with an `NN32` is ambiguous between `RR64`'s and `ZZ64`'s operators on both paths, where P-74 gives `ZZ64`, and two team tests fail (row 575). It waits as PLAN item 37, with its candidates; not building it is the default.
- P-76 **The numeral switch, split by where the static types are: a numeral's type is `IntLiteral`, which each number type converts from, in the library now and under walk once walk has static types.** It is the compiler library's working design, taken over the specification's unfinished numeral types. The library half (Q-lib) is built, with the team's held-back `IntLiteral` arithmetic enabled now that coercion exists. Walk's half (Q-walk) waits for the switch-over to give walk the checker's static types (PLAN phase 4), and rows 79, 395, 443, 454's walk half, 486 and 511 wait with it.
- P-77 **Scalar ranges are over `ZZ32` only, the JVM's largest array size, as the compiler library declares them.** A range over another integer type is a static error, and a wider counter is a `ZZ32` loop that widens its index. A range of rank 2 or 3 is a tuple of `ZZ32` ranges, and the public range traits stay generic in their index type. Built.
- P-78 **`SUM` and `PROD` without the `Number` catch-all: one generic reduction each over the library's algebra, taking its identity from the static argument (answer 7, to PLAN item 7).** A clause form whose element type nothing at the call fixes writes its static argument, as the team's tests do; `SUM e` and `DOT` keep their text. The model's two changed lines, `MicroGptFlat.fss:28` and `:43`, are approved. `BIG MAXN`, `BIG MINN` and `BIG MINMAXN` are gone, and `MAX` and `MIN` get no identity on the integers or the floats (F-60). Built.
- P-79 **The team's demos that write no static argument for a generic reduction stay as they are, and are respelled later only if they still fail.** When a batch has a free slot, it first checks each demo again under P-48's bound rule (row 425). It then writes the static arguments that a demo still needs, with a demo stage that only reports.
- P-80 **The sign-refined number types are a superseded design: a program tests such a subset at run time.** The number chapters test a value's sign by comparison, and a float's finiteness by `check` or `check*` on `RR64`. `advanced-lib/numbers-advanced.tex` stays word for word, marked superseded in the S1 form (P-96), so that the original design stays on record. Built. Their return with covariance is P-62's.

### Arrays and the model program

- P-84 **Storage is `double[]`, unboxed, and arrays keep the team's `nat` sizes.** A high-performance language cannot box its array elements, and the sizes are central to the team's design. So both are built together: sized types, the checker taught `nat` parameters, in the library's own patterns. How `double[]` goes under a float vector is array decision A (P-85).
- P-85 **The array design's three questions are open, with no default, and return after the switch-over with array decisions A, B and D (PLAN item 13).** The three: a store per element type chosen by the factory, the size also kept as a field of the store, and a factory for a size known only at run time that returns the unsized `Array`. A is how `double[]` goes under a float vector, B where unboxed arithmetic happens, D sizes in the model's own types (P-87). Also open: why `RR64` and not `RR32`, since LLMs train at FP32 at most; the one reason given is the Python reference's float64 output. P-55 holds the forks taken before the switch-over.
- P-86 **The integration review's checks stand for the array work.** A generic `Vector` or `Matrix` body is checked against what each operation needs, with no blanket ring bound that would exclude integer arrays. An empty reduction is checked for its type and its value. A small product through `PView` and a transpose is checked, with controls. The checks: `reviews/numeric-hierarchy-integration-review.md` section 5.
- P-87 **The model's text is the notation, and the vocabulary is repaired around it.** An operator in the model never becomes a function call to satisfy an overload check. Any repair that changes a model line is shown to him as a diff before it is built. Decision D, the sized signatures of the flat program C4's vocabulary, is such a diff (`reviews/decision-d-diff.md`); nothing of it is applied before PLAN phase 5. The ledger is one file; two views are derived from it (moves to the coordinator skill's record part).
- P-88 **The model's vocabulary leaves `+`, `-` and `MAX` between an array and a scalar to the library's scalar block, and its diagonal keeps its own product.** `Diag` extends `Matrix` and overrides `mul`, as the type system promises (row 299). A read-only matrix trait, `ReadableArray2` under `Array2`, is a library change for a later ledger row, not a vocabulary hack.
- P-89 **`fill` takes a value, `tabulate` a function (answer 10).** It is his answer to PLAN item 10. `fill` keeps the value form, every peer language's meaning of the word, and the function form takes Scala's name `tabulate`. Built; no model line changed.
- P-90 **`Maybe`'s empty case is the team's parametric `Nothing[\T\]`, in the one library and the specification.** The bare `Nothing` is future work: it needs a `where` clause that binds a type variable in an `extends` clause, which neither path implements, and it is not tried before then. Gating row 331 on static-argument inference instead is parked (PLAN, "Off the path, parked").

### The suites and the gate

- P-102 **Test first, the test kept.** Every sealed-tree edit starts from a failing test added to the corpus: the worker writes the test first, verifies that it actually fails, makes the fix, proves it passes, and the test stays in the corpus from then on; the full suite stays green. The test needs no commit of its own: the test and the fix may land in one commit. How the test is written is in the `fortress-repo` skill's tests part. Whether the test was seen failing first is read from the worker's transcript, not shown by a committed capture; the skeptic checks it there and runs no build to repeat it (moves to the coordinator skill's batch part).
- P-103 **The suite's verdict is the check.** A test passes or fails, as the team designed the suite, and a value that matters is asserted in the test with the mechanisms the suite already has (an `assert` in an interpreter test, `run_out_equals` in a compiled one). What a test prints beyond its checks does not matter. So no expected-output files are added; none of it is offered to him again. No batch compares the corpus's printed outputs, and no baseline is run to compare against (moves to the coordinator skill's batch part).
- P-123 **Row 366's three-line fix waits for a rung of its own, and route C gets no entry in the specification's Proposed Features.** Both are batch 5's defaults, which he took: the fix is not in design B as he decided it (P-41), and Appendix I already carries route C, the alternative to the flat tower (P-96). - P-125 **The suites run Fortress in parallel, pinned at four threads in `build.xml`.** Parallelism is native to Fortress, so a one-thread suite has a blind spot, and sparing a four-CPU machine is no reason for one. ### How we work

- P-104 **Nothing is built or run twice on the same code.** "We don't want to re be rebuilding stuff that doesn't need to be rebuilt." The design, agreed with him and built into the batch script: the old code, the batch's base, is built once per batch, by the coordinator, before the launch, in a worktree nobody compiles or runs in (the script's `args.baseBuild`); "We just mechanically take the latest build and share it between each rung from the same baseline": every rung worktree is seeded from that one build in seconds (`coordinator/tools/seed-worktree.sh`), never built clean; the worker builds only what its own edit changed; the skeptic builds nothing to check, "very smart skeptics that just like reuse everything that was built during the rank phase as much as possible and just do their independent checks": it reads test-first from the worker's transcript, runs its own small programs on the new code with the rung's build and on the old code from the batch's one base build, through `coordinator/tools/old-fortress.sh` with a private caches folder under the rung's worktree, so that no two agents compile into one cache. What the skeptic finds it fixes itself, test first, and a judge rules on a fix only where it is contested (P-118, "The judge's rulings."), his saving of the refusal chain; the fix is a new code state, built once and its suite run once, with no second skeptic after it and a repair round only on a judge's word. No stage re-runs a build, a suite or a measurement on a code state another stage has already run, and no agent reads again a log of output that has not changed: "I want to eliminate all waste of this type. Where we are re-executing stuff for no reason." How a worktree is seeded and what a worker clears after an edit or a failed build is engineering, measured before it is built into the batch script (`coordinator/build-cache-exploration.md`; the blinded engineers' variants in `coordinator/process-engineering/cache-sharing.md`; the redesign, `coordinator/process-engineering/batch-redesign.md`).
- P-105 **The coordinator watches what it launches.** Only a long or large run the coordinator launches outside a batch, a probe that builds and runs suites for an hour or more, is watched against the estimate it was launched with, so that none runs away as probe P1 did (`coordinator/process-engineering/p1-cost.md`); when it passes its estimate the coordinator reacts, by an instruction to the worker or a stop, and its brief carries what keeps it efficient, a wait polled under the prompt cache's life among it. Ordinary workers carry no such procedure, and no ask is added before a probe: "I don't want you to become paranoid about running probes"; "I don't want the cargo cult of ... monitoring here. Only longer runs. ... I don't want you to burden with ... procedure on each call to workers." P1's waste was one the worker knew how to avoid: "an instruction to that worker would have corrected it." Watching the spend is the coordinator's, never the worker's: a brief does not ask a worker to measure, project or cap its own token spend, "it has its job. It is not its job to monitor the token spend. It's yours."; the coordinator reads the spend from the agents' transcripts (`explorations/coordinator/tools/spend.py`).
- P-106 **A deeper pass, not a halt.** When something goes wrong or turns out undecided, the answer is a deeper pass, not a halt or a rollback. On a red gate over a merged batch, no bisection of commits: "we should identify the source of the conflict holistically and rework that part in the merged batch", because the goal is one tree with everything running rather than the largest subset that happens to be green. On a divergence the specification does not settle, no stop: "it is not a reason to stop the climb, it is the reason to think harder, review the architecture and holistically decide on the best path forward and execute that one." What reaches him are the forks already reserved in `PLAN.md`, not new ones invented at the point of difficulty.
- P-107 **Forks are probed before a batch is briefed.** "if there are known decision forks, which we can evaluate using probes, we should be doing these during this phase, during this planning phase": a fork a probe can settle is probed first, so that the decision is his and the worker gets a clean task, never a decision left to a worker and reviewed after the fact. Rows are taken one per message with refreshers (other languages, what makes sense in a high-performance language), not in groups.
- P-108 **A brief describes the problem, not the solution.** "How do we prevent the myopic view? … I want to have full creativity of solutions": a worker that has not read our notes lists every way the language and the library offer before a design choice reaches him; a rule that blocks an option is answered with how the library gets around it (protocol principle 2). A worker that records timings records the CPU too, so that comparisons can be guessed at.
- P-109 **The mission briefing.** "this whole exercise is about in-context learning. You were not pre-trained on Fortress. And the language was not finished." The planner, who has the whole map, gives each rung a mission briefing from the map that its agents read in their first turn; the share of agents that use it is reported with each landing (it was about 80% once and fell to 25-35%: "We need to get back to that level"). ## How he wants to be spoken to

- P-140 **The register.** "Drop the smart Alec essayist register before I blow a fuse": plain sentences, no framing flourishes, no closing lines that sum up in a phrase; advice as instructions, not as observations; replies very brief, plain English, no internal jargon. A wall of text is one he cannot absorb; the failure mode "is focused on over-compressing into a clever turn of phrase, but utterly fails in communicating clearly." Label which part answers which question when several are asked; do not restate what is in this file or in FACTS.md; when a concept is new to him, define it (he asked for reshape, ravel, planes, gather, outer product, Ring, Field, nat parameters, matrix multiplication). The coordinator's replies take the register set for the skills, ASD-STE100 about halfway (the entry P-134, "The skills are written for a reader new to the repository ..."): one instruction per sentence, active voice, short sentences, one word for one thing, no shorthand; it had a good effect on the clarity and the tone of the workers' reports. Phrases that read as a flourish are out: a coda tacked on after a semicolon ("by his decision", "the way through is his", "a session cannot wake itself otherwise"); a sentence ends where its point ends.
- P-141 **Asks and decisions.** Decisions are presented one at a time as: what we do, what it changes, with a default he can accept without reading the argument; the argument lives in the committed review, not in chat. An ask for a yes states, in the message itself, what the worker would do, what it produces, what it touches in the tree, what it costs, what the yes commits him to afterwards and what a no costs ("What are the consequences of the yeses? What does that work even want to do? ... this is just pressuring me to give approval without understanding"); one ask per message; no label stands in for an explanation, and a new name is defined in the sentence that uses it; the recommendation comes after the explanation, in one line, never instead of it.
