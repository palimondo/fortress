# The process-engineering study

## The question

Pavol, 2026-10-03, early morning UTC: whether the way the work is run spends the weekly token pool well, and how to divide the pool between improving the process and moving the climb forward. His threads, in his order:

- Why sharing the build between worktrees was called impossible for two weeks, and whether a model given the goal finds the answer ("machine empathy": "this empathy for machine, not wasting resources, is a crucial component of good engineering taste"; "Three seconds versus 0.07 ... if it's better, then let's use it").
- Where each role's tokens go inside a batch, and what is shared between agents that could be done once ("I am perfectly aware that checking and rechecking is the necessary part ... I just want to understand whether that's it").
- Whether one fixed batch workflow, reused for every step of the climb, holds the project back against what a designer would build from the goals alone ("I want to see those designs for the ultra code workflows, but not run them").
- Whether the identical start of every agent can be read from the prompt cache.
- Whether the rewrite of the `fortress-repo` skill (2026-10-08) changed the quality of the batch workers' work or its token efficiency (the curator's closing question, 2026-10-09).

## The pending items, settled

The three items the study listed on 2026-10-07, in their order. Two are settled and one is dropped from the order.

1. **The second round redesigned (the judge, the repair and the second skeptic after a refusal): settled, built, run three times.** The curator's two questions were answered by the design that ran as batches 11 to 13. (a) A second-round agent no longer exists to be briefed narrowly: there is no second skeptic and no repair round (none was needed in the six rulings). (b) His proposal was built: the skeptic fixes what it finds, builds and tests only its own fix, and a judge rules only on a fix the skeptic marks contested (`batch-redesign.md`; POSITIONS, "The judge's rulings.", commit `fc21b93c0`); the merged-diff review reads each fix in its maker's transcript, and the gate runs. Measured (`skill-effect.md`, sections 4 and 5): the second round fell from 25%, 20%, 28% of a batch to 2.7%, 5.4%, 6.3%; 57 skeptic fixes (9 defects of the change) and 6 rulings, all upheld, none reverted; the reviews found no unsound fix. The cost that stayed is clause (c) of the contested rule, 0.90M over the three batches for six rulings; the curator narrowed it on 2026-10-09 (option 1, `a1902d0d4`), and batch 14 is the first to show what that saves.
2. **The Fable comparison of the designs with batch 10 and `labor.md`: settled by `batch-redesign.md`, not written as a separate note.** The redesign was that comparison put to work: it took the six designs and the measured roles, its section 3 says what was taken and what not, and a Fable review stood in for the curator (section 11). The explainer page (https://claude.ai/artifact/Wy1hJLharoRXVa2uZsr2m6) was not updated; nothing in the record shows it needed to be, now that the redesign ran.
3. **The same two designers at the whole project's scope (microGPT compiled and fast): dropped from the order, not done.** It was a design exercise with no estimate asked, and it had no go. The redesign was done at batch scope and three batches have run on it. If the curator wants it, it is one line in "Waiting on Pavol" below.
