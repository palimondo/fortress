# Array fork 2, judged: the bound of `Vector` and `Matrix`

## In short

- **The question.** `Vector[\T extends Number, nat s0\]` and `Matrix[\T extends Number, nat s0, nat s1\]` add, subtract and multiply values of `T` in their bodies, and the scalar-extension block adds, subtracts, `MIN`s and `MAX`es a `T` with each element of an `Array[\T,I\]`; `Number` declares `asFloat` and `=` and nothing else. Which bound on `T` gives those bodies their arithmetic? It holds 25 of the 153 errors the compiled checker reports on the one library: 15 arithmetic refusals at `(T, T)` or `T`, the 8 of the scalar block, and 2 "does not satisfy the bound" sites at the factories `vector` and `matrix` (section 1).
- **What the history says.** The team's bound did carry arithmetic. Their `Number` extended `AdditiveGroup[\Number\]`, `MultiplicativeRing[\Number\]` and `StandardMinMax[\Number\]` and declared `+`, `-`, juxtaposition and `MIN` on any two numbers, every result an `RR64` (`git show 75cca6683:Library/FortressLibrary.fsi`, lines 273-313). Route A, his decision, took that arithmetic off `Number` and gave it to each sibling at its own type (POSITIONS, "The exclusion rule stays and the tower is flat (route A)"; `d846e3644`). The fork is the obligation route A left on the arrays (FACTS, "Route A's generic container obligations still need body-level checks"). The bound that says on the flat tower what the team's bound said on theirs is "a number that is a ring at its own type".
- **The library's own way.** Since answer 7 the library bounds generic arithmetic by its algebra trait at the element's type: `SUM[\T extends AdditiveGroup[\T\]\]`, `PROD[\T extends MultiplicativeRing[\T\]\]` (`Library/FortressLibrary.fss:3284-3301`), and where one trait is not enough it writes two, `MaxSumReductionPair[\T extends { AdditiveGroup[\T\], StandardMax[\T\] }\]` (`:3262`, `:3268`). Its products are methods so that views override them: `TransposedMatrix` overrides `scale`, `rmul` and `lmul` (`:2800-2802`), and his diagonal overrides `mul` (POSITIONS, "The vocabulary's scalar drops and the diagonal").
- **The recommendation.** The library's two-trait bound, each declaration asking for what its body uses: `Vector`, `Matrix`, their storing objects, factories, norms and products take `T extends { Number, MultiplicativeRing[\T\] }` (29 component lines, 32 api lines); the scalar block takes a bound per operator, `{ Number, AdditiveGroup[\T\] }` on `+` and `-`, `{ Number, StandardMin[\T\] }` on `MIN`, `{ Number, StandardMax[\T\] }` on `MAX` (8 and 8). `Number` stays, so its exclusions, its `asFloat` and the specification's "all elements of vectors and matrices must be numbers" stay; the algebra trait brings the arithmetic. No body, model or specification line changes. On the flat tower every number type is a ring at its own type, the integers included (`Integral[\I\] extends MultiplicativeRing[\I\]`, `Library/FortressLibrary.fsi:443`), so no integer array is excluded. **Default: the same.**
- **Measured today** (section 3), on a copy of the library through the gate's distance stage and under walk: the type-check stage clears all 23 arithmetic and block sites and uncovers the one parent-trait error at `scale` (`:2399`), as read; the two factory sites stay, as read; walk's seven array tests print the same as on the unchanged copy. And one thing not read: the checker's overloading stage crashes on the api and the component, and the export stage with it, "ClassCastException: UnionType cannot be cast to BaseType" in the team's `TypeSchemaAnalyzer.boundsSubstitution` (`TypeSchemaAnalyzer.scala:474-477`, David Chase, 2012, "TODO: FIX THIS FOR OPS", unchanged since), because the checker reads the closed `Number` as the union of its members and the meet of two bounds then comes back a union. One call is refused for the same reason (the norm's `squaredNorm(me)`, `:2483`). So the way needs a checker fix first, and the 09-29 timing question is not answered: the Meet Rule check that was slow then did not run today.
- **A batch 13 rung, in two halves** (section 5): the checker's `boundsSubstitution` taught a bound list whose meet is a union, test first with an expected-failure compiled test; then the 77 library lines, the distance stage before and after by row and line. It clears 23 of the fork's 25 and uncovers 1: 153 to about 131.
- **What it leaves.** The 2 factory sites, a checker slip no bound cures (section 2.2); the bodies typed by the parent trait, 10 with `:2399`, which need their own list of ways; row 437's numeral `0` in `matrix(v)`, whose repair `v.zero` the bound now permits; a `Vector` of numerals (`IntLiteral` is a `Number` and not a ring), which nothing on record makes; walk's `Bottom` for a two-bound parameter that no call fixes (row 591), which the arrays' calls all fix; the code generator's erasure of a two-trait bound, unmeasured, which `MaxSumReductionPair` already carries to the switch-over.

## 0. Words used below

- A *bound* is the `extends` clause of a type parameter: `T extends Number` admits only number types for `T`. A bound with two traits, `T extends { A, B }`, admits only types that extend both.
- A *self-typed trait* is a trait whose parameter is the type that extends it: `RR64 extends MultiplicativeRing[\RR64\]`. The papers call it the self-type idiom (section 2.4).
- `AdditiveGroup[\T\]` declares `zero`, `+`, binary `-` and unary `-` on `T` (`Library/FortressLibrary.fsi:259-265`). `MultiplicativeRing[\T\]` extends it and adds `one`, `TIMES`, juxtaposition and `^` (`:270-277`). `StandardMin[\T\]` declares `MIN`, `StandardMax[\T\]` `MAX`, `StandardMinMax[\T\]` both (`:189-218`).
- `Number` is the closed trait over every number type: `comprises { RR64, RR32, QQ, AnyIntegral, IntLiteral }`, declaring `asFloat` and `=` (`:281-289`). *Closed* means the `comprises` clause lists every type below it.
- The *flat tower* is route A's number tower: the eight number types are siblings under `Number`, each extending `AdditiveGroup`, `MultiplicativeRing`, `StandardPartialOrder` and `StandardMinMax` at its own type (`:291-293` for `RR64`, `:391-392` for `QQ`, `:443` for every integer type through `Integral`, `ProjectFortress/LibraryBuiltin/FortressBuiltin.fsi:47-48` for `RR32`).
- The *scalar-extension block* is the eight operators `+ - MIN MAX` between an `Array[\T,I\]` and a `T`, either way round (`Library/FortressLibrary.fss:4716-4726`, api `.fsi:2681-2691`).
- The *distance* is the gate's full checker measurement of the one library; its per-site list is `explorations/compile-ladder/gate/distance-sites.tsv` (153 errors, batch 12's landing). The stage runs every stage of every unit, type checking, overloading (the Meet Rule and the return-type rule between overloads) and the export check (component against api), and marks a stage that died with a `#crash` row, whose errors are then not counted.
- A *where clause* is the specification's device for constraining a static parameter beyond its bound, also on one method of a trait (`Specification/basic/trait-parameters.tex:305-330`); "not yet supported" (`Specification/basic/conversions-coercions.tex:15`), refused by code generation (FACTS, the territory map).

## 2. The nine steps

### 2.1 The mathematics

- `+` and `-` on vectors or matrices need the elements to form an additive group (an abelian group under `+`).
- Scaling (`scale`), the elementwise product (`pmul`), the dot product (`dot`) and the matrix products (`mul`, `rmul`, `lmul`) need a ring: a group under `+` with an associative `×` that distributes over it. `Vector[\T\]` with these six methods is a module over `T` with a bilinear form, and `Matrix[\T\]` is its ring of endomorphisms (my reading of the declarations).
- Division is not declared on the traits, so no field is needed.
- The norm needs a square root, which the library takes after `asFloat` (`Library/FortressLibrary.fss:2483`), so it needs `Number`'s `asFloat` and gives `RR64`.
- `MIN` and `MAX` need an order with a least and greatest of two, not a ring.
- An integer vector is a module over the integers: it adds, scales and has a dot product; it does not divide. A ring bound admits it. A vector of numerals (the type `IntLiteral`) has no algebra of its own in the library (`FortressBuiltin.fsi:178`): a ring bound refuses it, `Number` admits it.

### 2.2 What each path does today

- **Walk** runs every `Vector` and `Matrix` operation and the scalar block for all seven number types on the flat library, except `matrix(v)` for `NN32` and `NN64` (row 437), since it checks no bound against a body (FACTS, "The shipped `RR64` does not satisfy ..."; `ProjectFortress/tests/FlatTowerRungF.fss`, 72 assertions over seven number types).
- **The checker** refuses the 15 and the 8 (section 1): a body bounded only by `T extends Number` has no evidence for its arithmetic (FACTS, "Route A's generic container obligations ...").
- **The two factory sites are a checker slip, not the bound's.** The message comes from `TypeWellFormedChecker.scala:154-167`: for each written static argument of a trait type, it tests `analyzer.subtype(argument, bound)` in an analyzer extended with the enclosing declaration's static parameters (`:130-137`). At `vector[\T extends Number, nat s0\]():Vector[\T,s0\]` (`:2454`) that test fails, while at `tabulatedVector[\T extends Number, nat s0\](f:ZZ32->T):Vector[\T,s0\]` three lines later (`:2457`) the same test on the same bound passes. The 09-29 measurement showed the same two sites under every way, and under the two-trait bound reported once per bound (`reviews/array-design-ways.md` section 7; `sites-RINGNUM-vs-L0.txt` at `fb10482ba`); today's shows them again, naming the two-trait bound (section 3). So `T` reaches the test with no bound in scope at the first declaration of each overloaded factory pair, whatever the bound is. I did not trace the line of the checker that loses it; it is a row of its own (section 5).
- **A bound list on an overloaded declaration crashes the checker's overloading stage when one bound is a closed trait** (found today, section 3; read in the code after). `TypeSchemaAnalyzer.boundsSubstitution` (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/scala_src/types/TypeSchemaAnalyzer.scala:441-490`; David Chase, 2012, `5a68404fd`, with the comment "TODO: FIX THIS FOR OPS"; unchanged by the revival) computes each parameter's bounds as `conjuncts(imageTa.meet(e))` and casts every conjunct to `BaseType` (`:474-477`). The analyzer's `meet` normalizes an intersection (`TypeAnalyzer.scala:78-79`), reads a closed trait as the intersection of its name with the union of its `comprises` members (`:495`), and distributes an intersection over a union (`:636-638`). So the meet of `{ Number, MultiplicativeRing[\T\] }` is a union of five intersections, `conjuncts` of a union is the union, and the cast throws. The overloading checker reaches it through `subtypeEDInner`, `normalizeED` and `reduceED` (`:171`, `:216`, `:421`) when it compares two overloads whose parameter carries the bound list. `MaxSumReductionPair`'s two-trait bound never meets it: an object is not an overload. This is the same `comprises` expansion whose exclusion tests cost 39 minutes on 09-29, before rung O's rewrite (`reviews/array-design-ways.md` section 7, `distance-RINGNUM-stack.txt`); today it ends in a crash instead (my reading of the two stacks).
- **The compiled run** has never run a `Vector` or `Matrix` of the one library (the switch-over has not happened; row 307), so what a two-trait bound becomes in bytecode is unmeasured. The library's `MaxSumReductionPair` and `MinSumReductionPair` carry the same shape to the switch-over whatever this fork decides.

### 2.3 The specification

- The element bound is `Number`, three times: "An array of two dimensions whose elements are a subtype of `Number` is a matrix", "A one-dimensional array whose elements are a subtype of `Number` is a vector" (`Specification/basic/expressions/aggregate.tex:152-170`); "all elements of vectors and matrices must be numbers" (`Specification/preliminaries/overview.tex:917`); the coercion chapter's example `trait Vector[\T extends Number\]` (`Specification/basic/conversions-coercions.tex:230-236`).
- The arithmetic lives in algebra traits, not in `Number`. The chapter "Algebraic Constraints" gives `Ring[\T extends Ring[\T,⊕,⊖,⊗\], opr ⊕, opr ⊖, opr ⊗\]` and `Field` as self-typed traits with operators as static parameters and `where` clauses (`Specification/advanced-lib/algebraic-constraints.tex:1541-1545`, `:1761-1766`). The library that states that chapter exists only commented out, under `Library/incomplete/` (`reviews/decisions-review/judgement.md` section 1). The shipped library's `AdditiveGroup[\T\]` and `MultiplicativeRing[\T\]` are its fixed-operator form.
- The revised numbers chapter says the flat tower: the number types "are siblings under the trait `Number`: each is a subtype of `Number`, and none is a subtype of another" (`Specification/basic-lib/numbers.tex:32-33`), and each carries the four traits at its own type (`:224-229`, `QQ`'s listing).
- A bound with two traits is the grammar's own: a static parameter is `Id Extends?` (`Specification/basic/trait-parameters.tex:40-41`), and `Extends` is `extends TraitType` or `extends { TraitType, ... }` as a trait's own clause is.
- A method asking more of `T` than its trait's bound is a `where` clause (`trait-parameters.tex:305-330`), "not yet supported" (`conversions-coercions.tex:15`), and no batch has planned it (`reviews/array-forks-now.md` section 3).
- So the specification says: elements are numbers; arithmetic is a self-typed algebra trait; a bound names what the body needs; asking per method waits for `where`. The two-trait bound makes no sentence of it false. A ring bound alone would make `overview.tex:917` false, since a ring that is not a number could then be an element (my reading).

### 2.4 The papers

- The self-type idiom is the papers' own: "The self-type idiom is realized with the **comprises** clause which contains the instantiation of the self type" (`Papers/Types/journal/justificationOfRTR.tex:580`); the Welterweight draft has a section "Self-typed generics" left commented out (`Papers/Welterweight/dispatch.tick:195`). `T extends MultiplicativeRing[\T\]` is that idiom applied to a bound.
- The per-method alternative is cited and never formalised: the dispatch paper's bibliography carries Day, Gruber, Liskov and Myers, "Subtypes vs. where clauses: constraining parametric polymorphism", OOPSLA 1995 (`Papers/Dispatch/main.bib:966-972`), the paper that where clauses come from. Nothing in `Papers/` gives Fortress's rule for them.
- The compiled encoding: "mentions of static parameters are replaced with their upper bounds" (`Papers/Implementation/MethodMapping.tex:134`). What a two-trait upper bound erases to is the code generator's question at the switch-over, not this fork's (section 2.2).
- The 2012 note on the return-type rule (`Papers/RuntimeInstantiation/2012-6-15 return type instantiation restrictions.txt`) concerns overloads with generic return types; the array family's overloads all fix `T` by their arguments, so it adds nothing here (my reading).

### 2.5 The library's own way, first

- **Generic arithmetic is bounded by the algebra trait at the element's type.** `SUM[\T extends AdditiveGroup[\T\]\]` and `PROD[\T extends MultiplicativeRing[\T\]\]` (`Library/FortressLibrary.fss:3284-3301`, api `.fsi:1965-1976`), `SumReduction` and `ProdReduction` (`:3274`, `:3290`), `additiveIdentity` and `multiplicativeIdentity` (`:3223`, `:3240`), `SumProdReductionPair[\T extends MultiplicativeRing[\T\]\]` (`:3257`). This is answer 7 (POSITIONS, "`SUM` and `PROD` without the `Number` catch-all (answer 7)").
- **A bound with two traits where one is not enough.** `MaxSumReductionPair[\T extends { AdditiveGroup[\T\], StandardMax[\T\] }\]` and `MinSumReductionPair[\T extends { AdditiveGroup[\T\], StandardMin[\T\] }\]` (`:3262`, `:3268`). These are the revival's, written by route A's rung (`d846e3644`); the team's library has no two-trait static-parameter bound (`git show 75cca6683:Library/FortressLibrary.fsi`, grep for `extends {` in a static-parameter list, none), and neither is overloaded or has a closed trait among its bounds. The revival's tests pin the form under walk: `ProjectFortress/tests/GenericBesidePlainTwoBounds.fss` (a generic declaration with two open bounds beside plain ones, a valid overloading), and `XXXInferSeveralBoundsWalk.fss` (row 591). No compiled test has one.
- **The team's bound meant arithmetic.** Jan Maessen wrote `Vector[\T extends Number, nat s0\]` on 2007-12-13 (`1c492019f`) when `Number` was a ring at `Number` (section 2.7). The bodies' `e + v.get(i)` had the type `RR64` under that `Number`, not `T`, so even then the bodies were not well typed at `T`; the team never ran the checker on them (FACTS, "The shipped `RR64` ...").
- **Products are methods so that views override them.** `TransposedMatrix` overrides `scale`, `rmul` and `lmul` (`:2800-2802`), and the top-level `DOT` and juxtaposition forward to the methods (`:2463-2478`, `:2839-2871`). His diagonal does the same with `mul` (`explorations/run-c4/src/FlatArrays.fss:36-41`; POSITIONS, "The vocabulary's scalar drops and the diagonal"; row 299). A bound per method would need `where`; moving the products out of the traits would take the overrides with them.
- **A top-level operator carries its own bound.** `pmul[\T extends Number, nat k\]` (`:2460`), the sized `DOT`s, `SUM`, `PROD`: each generic function names what it needs. For the scalar block, whose eight operators are top-level, a bound per operator is the library's own form.
- **`Number`'s closure is doing work.** `Number comprises { ... }` is closed, so the checker and walk see that an element type excludes `String` and the arrays; the 09-29 measurement of the ring bound alone lost that (section 2.9, 6c).

### 2.6 The peers

From the record's survey (`reviews/array-design-ways.md` section 7): Rust's `ndarray` bounds its linear algebra by one blanket trait, `LinalgScalar: Copy + Zero + One + Add + Sub + Mul + Div`; Scala's Spire gives `Ring[A]` with parallel additive and multiplicative hierarchies, which the library's `AdditiveGroup`/`MultiplicativeRing` pair mirrors; Julia checks no bound statically. From training, unverified here (my reading): Haskell's `Data.Vector` puts no constraint on the container and `Num a =>` on each function, and Rust's own `impl<T: Add<Output = T>> Add for ...` bounds each impl; both are the per-operation shape, possible there because a constraint attaches to a function or an impl, which is what a `where` clause would give a Fortress method. Spire and `ndarray` show that a blanket ring on the linear-algebra container is the common practice where the elements are numbers.

### 2.8 The derivation from his positions

- **"The library's own practice is the standard."** The library's bound for generic arithmetic is the algebra trait at the element's type, and its form for two capabilities is the two-trait bound (section 2.5). A fork put to him names that way first; this judgement does.
- **"The exclusion rule stays and the tower is flat (route A)."** `Number` carries no arithmetic by his decision; a bound that wants arithmetic must name the algebra. 6f, arithmetic back on `Number`, reverses it; the rest do not.
- **"The integration review's checks"**: "checked per operation's required capability, with no blanket ring bound that would exclude integer arrays." The review's premise was the flat *sketch*: "The flat sketch gives `Integral[I]` `AdditiveGroup[I]`, not `MultiplicativeRing[I]`" (`reviews/numeric-hierarchy-integration-review.md:49`). The built tower gives `Integral[\I\] extends { StandardTotalOrder[\I\], MultiplicativeRing[\I\], AnyIntegral }` (`Library/FortressLibrary.fsi:443`), so a ring bound excludes no integer array: the check's reason is met. Its letter, "no blanket ring bound", is bent on the two traits, whose six products need the ring in every body, and kept on the block, where each operator asks for its own capability. The check's first half, "checked per operation's required capability", is what the recommended way does declaration by declaration (section 4).
- **"Storage is `double[]`, unboxed."** Untouched: the bound names the element's algebra, not its store; decision A's `RR64` store satisfies it.
- **"The model's text is the notation."** No model line changes: `RR64` extends `MultiplicativeRing[\RR64\]`, so `Diag extends Matrix[\RR64,s,s\]` and every `vector[\RR64,n\]` of C4 satisfy the bound as written.
- **"Fleshing out the standard library ... the algebraic layer (Ring, Field and what ML's matrices and tensors need)."** The arrays' algebra reaching the element through the library's own ring trait is the first step of that layer; the specification's `Ring` and `Field` with operator parameters (6e) are the later one, after `where` clauses.
- **"The array design's three questions are open ... after the switch-over."** Building fork 2 before the switch-over reverses that placement for this fork; `reviews/array-forks-now.md` section 3 flagged it, and PLAN's batch 13 line already brings forks 3 and 2 to him next (`coordinator/PLAN.md:122`).
