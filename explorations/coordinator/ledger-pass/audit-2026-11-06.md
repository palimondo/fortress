## 5. Summary

**The rewrites made the rows better.** On the same six checks, the failure rate of every check fell, from between 12% and 37% to between 3% and 10%. Keep rose from 47% to 85%. The fault that the health check found most often, a claim left behind by its notes or the tree (its section 5.5, item 1), is nearly gone in the sample. The pass changed 86 claims, and the sampled claims match the code they cite, line for line, at `c124c6b65` or the commit they name. Reproducers that did not reproduce were cleared (`none` rose by 26; rows 97 and 315 in the sample). The repair-history wording in notes fell by about a third. Nine rows were closed or retired, and four moved into or out of CONTESTED. Every citation that I followed resolved, apart from the off-by-one slips listed above.

**The faults that remain are of four kinds.**

1. **Cross-row consistency, which one rewrite at a time does not see.** Row 199 points to "row 219's wall", while the same run made row 219 POSITIVE-VERIFIED. Row 97 states a rule more widely than F-27 allows. Row 747 is kept open beside two FIXED siblings with the same mechanism, and does not say why its case differs.
2. **Class and status set by the defect's look, not by what the specification says.** Row 292 is `library gap vs spec` and NEGATIVE-VERIFIED, where the specification asks only in margin notes, and the library records a deliberate trade.
3. **A status that its own notes do not support.** Row 747 is NEGATIVE-BOUNDED with no reproducer and no run since `0856f3e6a`.
4. **Wording that leaves a referent open.** Row 202 has "the paper", and row 30 has "a reading, not a row".

**One risk the pass adds.** It fills notes towards their 700-character cap: 219 of the 372 rewritten rows now have notes of 650 characters or more, against 171 before. The health check found that refused notes on full rows are how claims drift. The next batch that touches these rows will meet `ledger.py note`'s limit sooner, so a correction to such a row has to go through `rewrite`, not `note`.
