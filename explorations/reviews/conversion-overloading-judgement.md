# Conversions and overloading: the judgement

## For Pavol

### Where you are

- On the fork of question A you chose option 2, "the generic arm, at `ZZ64`", on the condition that it is sound. Then you asked for the wider picture: one rule, not "rule on the rule", and your own idea weighed beside the others.

- This judgement takes it from there. It measures nothing; every number is on record.

### The terms, as this note uses them

- A **declaration** (an "arm") is one definition of a name. `op(a: Any, b: Any)` is one; `op[\T extends Number\](a: T, b: T)` is another.
- A call **fits** a declaration when each argument's type is the parameter's type or below it. Among the declarations a call fits, the **most specific** runs: the one with the narrowest parameter types.
- A **conversion** (the specification's word is coercion) is declared by the type it goes into: `ZZ64` declares `coerce(x: ZZ32)`. Since your answer 8 only exact conversions are automatic: a narrower integer into a wider one, and `ZZ32` or a numeral into `RR64`.
- A **generic** declaration has a type parameter with a **bound**. Read as the type group's 2011 model reads it, which is your answer 9: it fits a call when some type within the bound fits. `op[\T extends Number\]` fits a `ZZ32` and a `ZZ64` as they are, with `T = Number`.
- A **self-naming bound** is `T extends Integral[\T\]`: `T` must be a type that implements `Integral` at itself, so `T` is one integer type, never "some number". A `ZZ32` and a `ZZ64` fit such a generic only after one is converted. It is the library's usual bound.
- A **plain** declaration has no type parameter. A **catch-all** is a plain declaration over a wide type, `Any` or `Number`, that fits nearly everything.
- An **instance** of a generic is the generic at one type, `op` at `ZZ64`. On the compiled path each instance is a class stamped at load time from the generic's template.
- **Promotion** is your answer 8: a generic call over mixed widths takes the narrowest type every argument converts into, `ZZ64` for a `ZZ32` and a `ZZ64`, and converts the narrower argument.

### The two rules that met on your fork

- The designers' rule, in the coercion chapter: if any declaration fits without conversion, choose among those; convert only when nothing fits. The checker's team code has ranked this way since 2009. Walk has since rung C.
- Your answer 8: inside a generic call, convert to the narrowest type rather than keep the common parent type unconverted.
- Your fork: `op[\T extends Number\](a: T, b: T)` beside `op(a: Any, b: Any)`, called with a `ZZ32` and a `ZZ64`. The generic at `ZZ64` needs one conversion. The catch-all needs none.
- Batch N's two shadows ran the catch-all there (the note's way 1). That reading is out. It decides what fits by the instance the promotion picks, so a declaration can fit a wide static type and not fit a narrower one, and the run-time choice can be less specific than the static one. That breaks the specification's promise and splits the two paths (measured, `O2Wide`).

### The one rule, and why there is no contradiction

- The rule: **a conversion is applied to make a call possible; it never changes which body runs when a body already fits.**
- Answer 8 obeys it. Choosing `ZZ64` over `Number` inside one generic does not change which body runs. It is the same body, stamped at a narrower type. So the designers' order and your answer 8 are one rule at two levels: first the declaration (the body), by the designers' order; then the instance (the type the body is stamped at), by promotion.
- On your fork: does the generic fit as it is? Yes, on its declared types, with `T = Number`. It is more specific than the catch-all, so it is chosen. Then promotion stamps it at `ZZ64` and converts the `ZZ32`. That is your option 2, in the form the soundness check found sound ("reading A", the note's way 2).
- What it does not reach: a generic with a self-naming bound beside a catch-all. Such a generic does not fit mixed widths as they are, so the catch-all, which does, runs. The library has no such pair, and neither does microGPT; measured, nothing in the tests, demos or microGPT reaches the fork at all. With no catch-all beside it, a self-bound generic is promoted and converted like any other (measured, `O2FbLone`).

### Your idea, and what it becomes

- Your words: "if there is a conversion to a specific type, it should be applied. And only if all combinations are exhausted and we haven't reached a specific implementation, then we go to a plain implementation."
- Its sound form is the note's way 4: list every declaration and every instance that fits, with or without conversions; if one is more specific than all the others by the coercion chapter's own relation, run it, converting what it needs; otherwise fall back to the designers' order. It is C#'s rule.
- It gets three things right. It treats generic and plain arms alike. It makes answer 8 a special case. It reaches the self-bound generics.
- I recommend against it, for five reasons.
  - It treats a conversion as free. On the JVM a conversion is work: a new `ZZ64` object made from a `ZZ32`, a big-number rational made from an integer. Way 4 adds a conversion to every call where a converted arm outranks an arm that fits as it is. The designers' order never converts when an arm fits.
  - Most specific is not fastest. Under way 4, `z1 =/= z2` for two `ZZ32`s runs `QQ`'s `=/=`, converting both to rationals, because `QQ` is below `Any`. It stays so until a library line removes `QQ`'s redundant `=/=`. Every such shape in every future library file is a trap of the same kind.
  - The speed you want comes from the library, and the team's own compiled library shows how. It declares no `=` over `Any` or `Number`; each number type declares its own, and equality of other objects comes from `Equality[\T\] comprises T`. Under the designers' order, `z = 0` then reaches `ZZ32`'s own `=` with the numeral converted, because no catch-all fits first. Your idea at the library level is exactly that discipline: a specific arm at every type, no catch-all that fits first. At the language level it is C#'s order, and only C#'s.
  - The designers are unanimous. Their specification, their checker (Hilburn, 2009) and their compiled library all follow the designers' order; their later model (2011, 2012) is silent on conversion. Java, C++, Swift and Julia run an arm that needs no conversion before one that does; only C# ranks by the conversion's target. Your words of 09-23: "We are not Naden. We know nothing here."
  - Its price. It reverses the designers' sentence for plain arms too: `f(ZZ64)` beats `f(Any)` for a `ZZ32`. Two gated tests and one planned assertion are restated. A call that runs today, rung Q's `ee(5)`, becomes a refusal. The proof appendix is rescoped. Walk needs a further condition to stay sound where a variable's declared type is wider than its value. The code generator's defect for two competing generic arms must be fixed first. And a probe of both paths comes before any batch.

### Decision 1. Which order governs when conversions meet overloading

### 1. The rule

**Decided: way 2**, reading A of `option-2-soundness.md`: the coercion chapter's order over declarations, with applicability and specificity taken on declared, quantified domains as answer 9 words them, and answer 8's promotion as the instantiation of the selected declaration, its coercions inserted statically and the rewritten call dispatching on the converted values.

**Stated for the specification** (rung T's inference chapter; batch 7b's rung S for the coercion chapter's cross-reference):

> *Resolution* (`Specification/basic/conversions-coercions.tex:472-476`, `:533-553`, unchanged in substance). For a static call `f(A)` or `A₀.f(A)`, let Σ be the declarations of `f` applicable to the call without coercion, a declaration with static parameters being applicable when some instance within its bounds is (`Papers/Types/setup.tick:393-397`; answer 9), and compared on those quantified domains. If Σ is not empty, its most specific declaration is selected. Otherwise, among the declarations applicable with coercion, the one whose parameter type is no less specific (`⪯`, `:486-513`) than every other's is selected; the overloading rules guarantee that it exists and is unique, and where they do not, the call is a static error.
>
> *Instantiation* (the inference chapter). The static arguments of the selected declaration are then inferred: a static parameter takes the narrowest type, under `⪯`, that every argument constraining it is substitutable for (`:417-432`), its bounds permitting; for number arguments this is the narrowest type every one of them converts into (answer 8). The coercions this instance needs are inserted into the call statically, like any coercion resolved statically (`:584-591`).
>
> *Dispatch* (`Specification/basic/overloading.tex:262-276`). The rewritten call dispatches at run time, by subtyping and without further coercion, to the most specific declaration applicable to the converted values.
>
> *Numerals* (Q1; decision 4 below). Where a numeral's candidate types are incomparable under `⪯`, in either step, the numeral is read as `ZZ32`, or as `ZZ64` or `ZZ` when its value does not fit.

**Reasons.**
- One principle at two levels, not two rules. A conversion makes a call possible and never changes which body runs when a body already fits (`conversions-coercions.tex:279-282`, `:472-476`). Answer 8 chooses among instances of one body; it never chooses between bodies. So the designers' order and answer 8 do not conflict once applicability is read on declared domains, which answer 9 already decided (`reviews/overloading-judgement.md` section 3.4: "inference instantiates the chosen declaration; it does not precede the comparison").
- Sound, with one sentence. `option-2-soundness.md` section 1: the Meet Rule, the uniqueness facts and the appendix lemma speak of declared domains, which reading A does not change (`Specification/advanced/overloading.tex:247-274`, `:449-470`; `Specification/appendices/overloading-coercion.tex:75-96`). Section 2: the converted call's re-dispatch to `plain64` is type-safe by the return-type rule over every instance (measured, `O2Z64` on the rule and opt2 builds). Section 3: the Meet Rule does its job (`O2Meet`, every build). Section 4: it is answer 9's own order.
- The designers' record is unanimous for this order, and the type group's later word does not contradict it. The coercion chapter (2007-2009; `0f49d8698`). The checker's coercion-first ranking (Hilburn, 2009-08-18, `fc99e1ef2`; `ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/scala_src/useful/STypesUtil.scala:1085-1095`, opened and read). The compiled library, whose `=` and `=/=` are per type with no arm over `Any` or `Number` (`ProjectFortress/LibraryBuiltin/CompilerBuiltin.fsi:123-124`, `:167-168`, `:229-230`, `:299-300`, `:359-360`, `:412-413`, `:445`), general equality being `Equality[\T\] comprises T` (`Library/CompilerAlgebra.fsi:24-26`), so that under this order `z = 0` reaches `ZZ32`'s own `=` with the numeral converted: by reading, Σ is empty, and `(ZZ32, ZZ32) ⪯ (ZZ64, ZZ64)` and `⪯ (ZZ, ZZ)` because `IntLiteral`, the one type that converts into `ZZ32`, excludes both (`CompilerBuiltin.fsi:390`).
