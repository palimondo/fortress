**The six rungs, two sentences each.**

## The six rungs

### P. The checker's exclusion relaxation

**The measurement.** `perf-probes/prelude/exclusion-trace.md` bucket 2: of the 93 errors the checker reports over the interpreter's library, **61 at 26 sites** are caused by `checkP` and by nothing else — `checkEC` fires once in the whole set, `checkO` never, and `checkCC`, which fires alongside `checkP` at 29 of them, establishes nothing on its own (measured: relaxing `checkP` and `checkCC` together gives a byte-identical error list to relaxing `checkP` alone, `02-dropP.out` against `03-dropP-dropCC.out`). Relaxing `checkP` in the positive direction takes the count to **33**, and confining that relaxation to the two `TypeHierarchyChecker` call sites gives **33 as well, with the same three residual sites** (`12-hierarchyOnly.out`). Per-error attribution, with the generic each pair inherits at two instantiations, is `exclusion-trace/sites.tsv`.

**Why the clause is the checker's defect and not the library's.** `Specification/basic/trait-parameters.tex:339-345` says trait declarations are allowed to extend other instantiations of themselves. The specification's list of what makes two types exclusive has four entries — an `excludes` clause (`types-vals-vars.tex:212-214`), object-ness (`:222-224`), propagation to subtypes (`:163-164`), and the arrow, tuple, `()` and `BottomType` cases (`:215-216`) — and two instantiations of one generic are not among them. The library's shape is not exotic: because its algebra traits are parameterised by their own subject type (`Equality[\T\]`, `StandardTotalOrder[\T\]`, `Integral[\I\]`), any nested tower inherits one of them twice, so `checkP` declares `ZZ32` and `ZZ64` to share no value. The interpreter's own exclusion relation (`FType.java:196-297`) has no instantiation clause at all, and the designers' survey says the same: "two instantiations of `Array` are neither ordered nor excluding".

**The two caveats the trace carries, which the rung must carry too.** First, **the specification's own example cannot be put through this front end** — as printed it gives "T is undefined" twice, because a where-clause variable is not in scope in an extends clause, and rewritten with two static parameters it is refused earlier as "Cyclic type hierarchy" with or without the relaxation (`09-spec-self-instantiation.out`, `09a-…`, `17-SelfInst2-*.out`). So `trait-parameters.tex:339-345` is cited as text, and the demonstration is `MinP.fss`, the library's own shape in three traits, where the specification offers no rule that makes the two types exclusive at all. Second, **the trace does not settle soundness**: dropping `checkP` in the positive direction only makes `excludes` and `notExcludes` no longer complementary — `definitelyExcludes(List[\ZZ32\], List[\Boolean\])` becomes false while `notExcludes` still emits the equality constraint `$i=$j` that static-argument inference runs on — and nothing in the trace measures what the inference path does with that. The gate for this rung is the full suite, which the trace never ran, and nor did the triage (§ 6): no suite has yet run with the relaxation in any placement, and covering the overloading check widens what that run has to show. The clause must not be deleted: under `negate = true` it is what `notExcludes` (`TypeAnalyzer.scala:367-370`) uses to emit those constraints, in the team's own words at `:361-365`.

**The decision the rung carries: where the relaxation goes, now settled by measurement to include the overloading check.** `definitelyExcludes` is called from the two hierarchy checks (`TypeHierarchyChecker.scala:178, :187`), from `CoercionOracle.scala:78, :82` and from `Formula.scala:189, :191, :295, :304, :307`, and `excludes` from `OverloadingChecker.scala:478, :491`, which is the Incompatibility Rule's certificate. This record first recommended the two hierarchy call sites alone, because both placements gave 33 on this library and the narrow one left overload checking and coercion with the strong rule. The triage measured that reason away (`perf-probes/nat/triage.md` § 2, § 5): the two agree on today's library, where early errors keep the overloading check off `RangeInternals` and `FortressLibrary`, but once L's bounds let it run on `RangeInternals`, the strong clause, still feeding `OverloadingOracle.excludes` — "the meet of the two domains is Bottom" (`overloading/OverloadingOracle.scala:190-194`), Bottom when `normConjunct` finds two excluding conjuncts (`TypeAnalyzer.scala:632-640`) — makes a subtype in the nested tower exclude its own supertype and refuses pairs where the Subtype Rule plainly holds, `combine2D(ExtentScalarRange, ExtentScalarRange)` against `combine2D(ScalarRange, ScalarRange)` among them. Measured on today's tree with L's library: the hierarchy call sites alone, **41** (r11); the relaxation raised for the overloading check too, **23** (r17: 19 `RangeInternals` errors gone, `CAP` 5 and `combine2D`/`combine3D` 14, and one exposed, an `IN` pair that only the strong clause certified); with everything applied (the triage's ZALL3, memo off), 487 against 203. **So the rung covers the overloading checker's exclusion test as well as the two hierarchy questions** — the direction the interpreter already takes, its exclusion relation having no instantiation clause at all. The measured form raises the relaxation for the whole overloading check (`OverloadingChecker.checkOverloading`, `:159`; the triage's `-Dprobe.triage.dropPOver`, `triage/overloading-checker.patch`) and leaves `CoercionOracle` and `Formula` with the strong rule; on this library it gives the same error list as the broad placement inside `pExcInner` (203 and 203, triage § 2), and what either does to coercion and inference elsewhere is unmeasured. The probe's switch twins every analyzer memo while the relaxation is raised (`triage/p-only.patch`), so that no answer computed under it is read outside it; a scoped placement has to solve the same, and the broad one does not. The rung records its choice between the two and what it leaves unmeasured.

**The test, first.** `ProjectFortress/compiler_tests/ExclusionRelaxRungP.fss` + `.test` in the form of `compiler_tests/AtomicTopLevelVar.test` (`tests=ExclusionRelaxRungP`, `link`, `run`, `run_out_contains=PASS`, `run_out_does_not_contain=FAIL`): the shape of `perf-probes/prelude/exclusion-trace/MinP.fss` — `trait G[\X\] end`, `trait A extends G[\ZZ32\] end`, `trait B extends { A, G[\Boolean\] } end`, no `excludes` and no `comprises` in the file — with a `run()` that prints PASS. Capture the four errors before the edit (two family A, two family B, every one attributed `clause=P`; `17-MinP-stock.out` is the trace's own capture of them, `17-MinP-dropP.out` the relaxed run at 0 errors and rc=0). The test must stay a *positive* test: it is the specification's rule, so a program of this shape compiling is the assertion. The overloading half has no program of its own in this record: on this rung's own branch its effect is unmeasured, and on the count it shows only once L's bounds let `RangeInternals`' overloading check run, so its evidence is the triage's captures (r11 against r17) and its verification is the merged tree, the second declared exception to rule 2 ("How it is run"); a program reducing one of the refused pairs, as `MinP.fss` reduces the hierarchy half, would be the better test and was not attempted.

**What it clears, and the stop.** On the count stage: 93 → 33 on this rung's own branch for the hierarchy half (r13, and the trace's `12-hierarchyOnly.out`), the overloading half's effect there unmeasured and named in the report; on the merged tree with L, 23 where the hierarchy half alone gives 41 (r17, r11). On the ladder: nothing declared. The rung stops and reports rather than half-landing if the suite goes red in the inference path and the fix for that is a second design decision — that is the one thing the trace says it cannot predict. It must not touch the library, must not remove `checkP`, and must not change `notExcludes`.

### L. The library's five plain defects

**The measurement.** Trace bucket 1 and families C, D and F of `coordinator/two-libraries.md` § 3, re-counted for that brief rather than taken from the record: 26 errors of family C at 15 sites, 2 of family D at 2 sites, 1 of family F (plus 1 secondary), and one of the two family E errors free. In the trace's own composition of the 32 that remain after P (`§ 7`, `19-lib-L1b-dropP.out`), these are 26 + 2 + 2, and the second family F error is the one P's relaxation unmasks.

**The five, each with its site and its rule.**

1. **Family C, fifteen `RangeInternals.fsi` declarations, 26 errors.** Four traits require `[\I extends Integral[\I\]\]` (`:44`, `:121`, `:235`, `:304`) and are then instantiated from declarations whose own parameter is unbounded (`object RightScalarRange[\I\](r: I, str: I)` at `:309`, `emptyScalarRange[\I\]()` at `:578`) or bounded only by the non-parametric `AnyIntegral` (`sized1Range[\I extends AnyIntegral\]` at `:580` and the run of declarations from `:582` to `:616`). The interpreter checks no static-parameter bound, so nothing ever reported them. The fix is the bound, and only the bound: `I extends Integral[\I\]`. **The stop is here:** a fix that would change a declared *type* rather than a bound is out of scope for this family and the rung reports it instead.
2. **Family D, the duplicated declarations in `trait String`, 2 errors.** `Library/FortressLibrary.fsi:2311-2312` declares `abstract splitWithOffsets()` and `abstract split()`, and `:2327-2328` declares both again concretely; the checker calls it "multiple declarations … with the same parameter type: ()". One pair goes; which one is the rung's call, argued from what `Library/FortressLibrary.fss`'s `trait String` actually defines.
3. **Family F, `List.fsi:99`.** `zip[\F\](other: List[\F\]): Generator[\(E,F)\]` re-declares, with a wider return type, the `zip[\F\](g:ZeroIndexed[\F\]):ZeroIndexed[\(E,F)\]` it inherits (`FortressLibrary.fsi:1187`); `Library/List.fss` has no body for it at all. The rung decides between narrowing the api's return type and dropping the redundant declaration, and argues it from the absent body.
4. **Family F, the one P unmasks, `FortressLibrary.fsi:789`.** `Condition[\E\].map[\G\](f: E->G): Generator[\G\]` against the `SequentialGenerator[\E\].map` it overrides at `:762`, which returns `SequentialGenerator[\G\]` (`Condition` extends it at `:772`). ### R. `round` on a float, half to even

**The history he asked for first, which is on file and does not contradict the reading.** `compile-ladder/rung-rr64-functions/round-history.md`: the specification sentence was published in Fortress 1.0 on 2008-03-31; the half-to-even rational body and its assertions were written 2008-07-21 (`dc41d5000`, gls); `Float$Round` = `Math.round` came three months later (`e67394471`, 2008-10-25, jmaessen, "Rounding now available for all numeric types."), to serve a demo that needed a perfect-square test, where no tie can arise. ### S. The default rendering of an object with no `asString`
