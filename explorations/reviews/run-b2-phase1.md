# Phase 1 — independent review of Run B2 (`explorations/run-b2/`), alone

Written without opening `explorations/run-b/`. Method follows the earlier Phase 1 reviews (`explorations/reviews/blinded-fable-phase1.md`, `astra-phase1.md`), lighter: run the checker and the demo and record them verbatim, judge every rendered pair beside its formula and its reference Python, regenerate one figure through the pipeline, table the two earlier adopt lists against what the run did, check the language claims against the merged ledger with probes only where a new load-bearing claim is unmarked, and read the process record and the transcript against the brief. The standard is `experiment/RUN_B_BRIEF.md` on `origin/claude/worker-brief-fable-vnnuv8` as adapted by the "Run B launched" section of `explorations/microgpt-run-b-handover.md`. Reviewer probes, commands and timings: `explorations/run-b2-review-probes/` (uncommitted), indexed in `explorations/run-b2-review-probes/COMMANDS.md`.

## 1. Does it run?

The article's last section (`explorations/run-b2/article.md:281-292`) gives the recipe, and it is complete and correct: two components, run from `src/`, no build step, no flags.

**Checker, one thread** (`run-b2-review-probes/check_t1.out`): `ALL CHECKS PASSED`, exit 0, 47.8 s wall. Every line is identical to the shipped `explorations/run-b2/checks/check_run.txt` except the four timing lines, so the shipped log is the run this program produces: step 1 logits 2.220446049250313E-16, per-position losses 4.440892098500626E-16, loss 8.881784197001252E-16, nine gradients from 1.1102230246251565E-16 (wte, wpe, lm_head) down to 1.3010426069826053E-18 (attn_wq), lr exact, parameters after Adam ≤ 1.3877787807814457E-16; step 2 the same picture with logits at 3.3306690738754696E-16; three replayed samples `org`, `stclyzqwpactmmcx`, `ku` identical token for token. Step time 11.1 s and 12.3 s, forward 0.85 s and 0.76 s, forward plus backward 8.5 s and 9.7 s.

**Checker, four threads** (`check_t4.out`): `ALL CHECKS PASSED` in 32.6 s, and the numbers do not move at all — every PASS line and every max-abs-diff value is byte-identical to the one-thread run; only the timings change (step 5.5 s and 6.2 s, forward plus backward 3.9 s and 4.5 s, a 2.0× speed-up on the training step). That is worth recording against the article's stated justification for its 1e-9 tolerance, "the tolerance is there for the day the reductions run in parallel, when the order changes and the last bits with it" (`article.md:24`): at four threads the order does not change the last bits on this workload, so the sentence is a forecast, not an observation, and the run never tested it.

**Demo, one thread** (`demo_t1.out`): 2 m 35.8 s, twelve steps whose losses reproduce `checks/demo_run.txt` to the last digit (3.3659669475848513 … 2.934480617624684); the five samples differ (`sdin`, `hoin`, `fk`, `j`, `xnann` against the shipped `haen`, `xaik`, …) because `MicroGPTDemo.fss:44` draws from `random(1.0)`, which the article states (`article.md:184`).

Nothing under the historical tree was touched, no flags beyond the environment the brief prescribes, and no heap or stack option was needed.

## 2. Goldens and what the check covers

The goldens are derived here, not borrowed: `tools/derive_goldens.py` executes the pinned `microgpt.py` (sha256 `d47d88c2…ccee`, printed by the checker itself as its first line) and instruments two steps and three samples, `tools/gen_fixture.py` emits `src/MicroGPTRef.fss` in the reference's own layout, and the article records that the result agrees field for field with the blinded run's independently derived `checks/checks_2steps.json` (`article.md:28`). `reference/microgpt.py` and `reference/input.txt` are present on disk and excluded by `explorations/run-b2/.gitignore`, as the brief requires.

Coverage is what the brief asked for and no less: the real 4,192-parameter configuration on the real documents, two steps, all 27 logits at every position, per-position losses, the loss, all nine gradients, the learning rate, all nine parameter families after Adam, and three samples with the reference's uniform draws replayed (`src/MicroGPTCheck.fss:52-95`). The convention bridge — the reference's (out × in) matrices transposed and split per head — lives in the checker (`MicroGPTCheck.fss:13-41`), not in the core; `grep` for `ref_` or any fixture constant in `src/MicroGPT.fss` returns nothing. The core is 169 lines with comments and blanks stripped.

Two things are not verified. There is no finite-difference check of the final program's gradients: the finite differences were run on the three skeletons only (`probes/skelA.out`, `skelB.out`, `skelC.out`), and the shipped engine is checked solely against the reference's own backward pass, so a shared error of convention between the two would not be caught — an unlikely but unclosed hole the article does not name. And nothing beyond step 2 is checked against a golden except the single third-step loss quoted at `article.md:20`.

## 3. The rendered pairs, judged by eye

Provenance first. Every `def_*.tic` body occurs verbatim in the shipped program text — all 23 of them, checked mechanically against `src/MicroGPT.fss`, `MicroGPTCheck.fss` and `probes/skel*.fss` (21 match as one contiguous block; `def_logits` and `def_node` match line for line, having been assembled from two regions of the same file); no figure is a hand-typeset approximation. Regenerating one figure through the four-step pipeline (`figures/render.sh def_attention.tic`, 12.6 s, `run-b2-review-probes/regen/`) reproduces the shipped PNG bit for bit (max pixel difference 0) and the shipped SVG identically as a multiset of lines, one glyph `<path>` emitted in a different order. Four `.tic` files in `figures/` are stale leftovers that no longer match the program — `engine.tic` still carries the retired `w (|hs|)` workaround, `train.tic` the hand-rolled `BIG MIN` tokenizer, `carriers.tic` a hand-rolled `infinity`, and `model.tic` — none of them is embedded in the article, but they sit beside the live ones with no mark saying so.

The formulas are typeset at parity through the same LaTeX and dvisvgm pipeline (`figures/formulas/*.tex`), the Python is quoted verbatim with line citations (`article.html` carries `def rmsnorm(x): …` with the caption "microgpt.py, lines 103-106"), and 27 `<details>` blocks disclose the ASCII source as typed. The by-eye test is fair.

Five read as the formula, eight are close, one does not. The article's own table (`article.md:194-207`) claims "yes" for nine of twelve and "close" for three; on the two where I disagree with it the evidence is in this directory. Adam first: respelling its three fractions tight — `m1/(1 - beta1^t)`, `v1/(1 - beta2^t)`, `theta - lr m_hat/(SQRT v_hat + epsilon_adam)` — sets them all as the paper's stacked fractions with the radical (`run-b2-review-probes/pairs/rvw_def_adam_tight.png`), and the patched program passes the checker with output byte-identical to the shipped run (`run-b2-review-probes/tightadam/tightadam_check.out`, 48.5 s), so the closer render was available at zero cost and zero risk. RMSNorm second: the article says the entry-by-entry form is forced because "the library has no row broadcasting" (`article.md:231`), and the library half of that is true — there is no `matrix + row vector` overload (`run-b2-review-probes/RvwBroadcast.out`) — but the matrix-level formula for a row scaling is `Y = diag(r)⁻¹ X`, and that spelling runs on the run's own `Mat` and agrees with the entry-by-entry version to 0.0 in the same probe. It costs an n × n product, which is a real objection at speed, but it is an objection the article never makes because the alternative was never tried.

Two further observations on the sheets themselves. `f_chain`, `f_gather_b` and `f_concat_b` are not formulas from the transformer literature but statements of this program's own rules, typeset in the same style as the papers' — legitimate as documentation, and the article does not pretend otherwise, but a reader comparing "the formula" with "the definition" is on both occasions comparing the run with itself. And the closer weight spellings do exist for the four unindexed weights: `_W_o` sets **W**_o and `_W_lm` sets **W**_lm (`run-b2-review-probes/pairs/rvw_names3.png`), against the shipped **Wo** and **Wlm**.

## 5. Language claims

`gaps.md` holds 39 rows numbered 84 to 122 in the ledger's own eight-column format, each with a reproducer under `probes/` and a saved `.out` beside it, and the ledger itself is untouched. Checked against rows 1 to 83: the topics of the new rows — value objects and `SEQV`, contracts, `test` and `property`, pasting and unpasting, `Map.union` and `BIG UNION`, the dotted-field superscript, object-expression field scope, the bar encloser, field-held closures, `List`/`Range` overloads, `−` versus `AND`, getter order, `∞`, `indexOf`, generator order over a string, API operator export, untyped `fn` parameters, prime identifiers — return no keyword hits in the ledger, so none of the 39 duplicates an existing row. Five claims the run's probes settled were correctly demoted to cross-references of rows 18, 12, 4, 34 and 66 rather than renumbered (`gaps.md`, closing paragraph), and row 109 is a retirement of the run's own first reading of `w |hs|` rather than a new gap.

The claims the article's notation actually depends on are marked and, where they are new, replicated: the `(B.data)^T` parser gap and the object-expression field collision by `probes/REPORT-g4ab.md`, and the bar encloser, field-held closures, the `List`/`Range` Meet Rule, `−` versus `AND`, getter order, `∞` and `indexOf` by `probes/REPORT-g4c-i.md`. I re-probed only what those reports do not cover and the ledger does not settle.

- **"no row broadcasting in the shipped `Matrix`"** (`article.md:231`, the justification for every entry-by-entry row operation) is the one load-bearing negative in the article with neither a ledger row nor a reproducer — precisely the shape gate 1 forbids. `run-b2-review-probes/RvwBroadcast.out` supplies both halves: the library really has no `Array2 + Vector` overload (the interpreter prints the whole 24-candidate overload set), and the diagonal spelling `diag(1/r) X` runs and agrees to 0.0, so the negative is true as stated about the library and false as an argument that the entry-by-entry form was forced.
- **`gaps.md` row 120, "a postfix operator declaration cannot be written in an API at all"**, is NEGATIVE-BOUNDED on one recorded failure (`probes/apix/v2/Core.fsi`, the `opr (self)^T` method form). A second spelling, the top-level function form `opr (A: Mat)^T: Mat` in the `.fsi`, fails the same way — `CoreT.fsi:8:1: Syntax Error` (`run-b2-review-probes/apiT/UseCoreT.out`) — while the same API with `transpose(A: Mat): Mat` parses and runs (`apiT/UseCoreT_control.out`). The bound is wider than the run recorded; the status is still correctly NEGATIVE-BOUNDED.
- **"Σ over anything"** (`article.md:153-157`) holds inside the core component and only there. A client that imports `MicroGPT.{...}` gets the library's numeric Σ back and `CastError` on a `Params` sum (`RvwSumTypes_noexcept.out`); the same client with `except { opr BIG + }` gets `Operator BIG + is not defined.` (`RvwSumTypes.out`), because a top-level `opr` is component-scoped — ledger row 30, which the run met and documented for `^T` (`MicroGPTCheck.fss:10`) but did not state for Σ.
- **The reduction identity.** `PlusReduction.empty(): Any = 0` is the ledger's row 45 hazard rebuilt at user level; it is unreachable in this program only because `concat` is always called with four heads, and neither `gaps.md` nor the article mentions it.

Everything else I sampled checks out: the marks in `article.md:239-277` each name a probe or a run whose output exists, `RETIRED` is used honestly for the run's own falsified reading, and the two NEGATIVE-BOUNDED rows are the two where the evidence is thin.

## 7. Closing

What the run got right that the tree did not have before: a differentiation engine with no mutable state anywhere — `value object Node(data: Mat, pullback: Mat → Params)` and nothing else, `src/MicroGPT.fss:64-81` — whose binary rules typeset as the equations they implement, `A.pull(C̄ (B.data)^T) + B.pull((A.data)^T C̄)` beside Ā = C̄Bᵀ, B̄ = AᵀC̄, and whose fan-out is a monoid sum rather than an accumulation into a slot; three shapes were built and finite-difference-checked before that one was chosen, which is the first time in this arc that an autodiff design was selected against rendered evidence rather than argued for. Beside it stands the first verification in this tree of an entire matrix-level layer against the reference's real configuration at machine epsilon, reproduced here in 47.8 s from a two-line recipe, and 39 new gap rows with a saved reproducer each — the value-object rules that the interpreter does not enforce, the `SEQV` defect that reaches the shipped `Maybe`, and the contracts, tests and pasting mechanisms that no prior run in this arc had touched (`explorations/run-b2/gaps.md`).

The faults that matter are three, and all three are of the same kind: a claim that was cheaper to make than to check. The article says the entry-by-entry row operations are forced by the library (`article.md:231`) when the diagonal spelling runs and agrees exactly (`run-b2-review-probes/RvwBroadcast.out`); it says Adam is "identical line by line" to the formula (`article.md:205`) when its four fractions set inline and a three-character respelling sets them stacked and still passes every check (`run-b2-review-probes/tightadam/`); and it justifies its tolerance by a parallel-order effect (`article.md:24`) that four threads do not produce (`run-b2-review-probes/check_t4.out`). Below those, the article was published twice before the replications of its own claims came back (transcript records 20395, 20460, 20513), the adopt lists it was told to work through left no adopt-or-reject record anywhere in `explorations/run-b2/`, and the transformer is taught thinly against the notation it is meant to balance — the feed-forward section of `article.md:83-89` carries three figures and no prose at all.
