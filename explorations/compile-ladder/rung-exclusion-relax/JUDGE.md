## 1. What the specification settles, and how

The specification is not silent. It speaks on both halves of the problem, and its two halves form one coherent design.

**(H) A type may extend two instantiations of one generic.** `Specification/basic/types-vals-vars.tex:184-189`: the subtype and exclusion relations "are the smallest ones that satisfy all the properties given in those sections (and this one)", and the properties that make trait types exclude are an `excludes` clause (`:212-214`), arrow, tuple, `()` and `BottomType` (`:215-216`), object-ness (`:222-224`) and propagation to subtypes (`:163-164`). None concerns two instantiations. The worker cited this and it is right. The worker cited `trait-parameters.tex:339-351` as text and conceded it is the covariant case that Naden's refinement permits (`Papers/Types/journal/justificationOfRTR.tex:536-537`). It missed the two passages that are decisive and invariant:

- `Specification/basic/trait-parameters.tex:359-367`: `trait C extends D[\T\] where {T extends Object}` — "In this declaration, trait C is a subtrait of *every* instantiation of parametric trait D. Thus, trait C has all of the methods of every instantiation of D." Under multiple instantiation exclusion, "any type which is a subtype of two distinct instantiations of a single generic type is equivalent to Bottom" (`justificationOfRTR.tex:345-347`), so `C` would be uninhabited; the specification instead discusses the inference restrictions `C` needs (`:367-379`), which only a live type needs.
- `Specification/basic/trait-parameters.tex:383-397`: `object Empty extends List[\T\] where {T extends Object}` — a single value that is a member of every instantiation of `List`. This is `BadList` (`justificationOfRTR.tex:89-98`) written on purpose, as the specification's canonical empty list. Note that its `first()` and `rest()` throw (`:392-393`): the specification's own example never produces a `T`, which is why it is sound in the specification's world.

So on (H) the specification settles it against the compiled checker's `checkP` (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/scala_src/types/TypeAnalyzer.scala:443-456`) and against the two hierarchy questions that enforce it (`scala_src/typechecker/TypeHierarchyChecker.scala:178`, `:187`). The worker's four errors on the MinP shape (`probes/test-preedit.txt:2-10`) are that enforcement.

**(O) Overloaded declarations share their static parameters, and a call site instantiates them once.** `Specification/basic/overloading.tex:100-108`: "it is an error for their static parameters to differ (up to α-equivalence), or for one declaration to have static parameters and another to not have them. Hence, static parameters do not enter into the determination of which declarations are applicable". With `trait-parameters.tex:374-379` ("It must be possible to infer which method is referred to at the call site ... type checking rejects the program" otherwise), this is the specification's dispatch model: static arguments are fixed at the call site by inference, every overload of the name is instantiated at those arguments, and dispatch chooses among declarations that all carry the same instantiation. In that model `Empty` is harmless: no overload of a name can be applicable to it at an instantiation other than the caller's, because no overload has an instantiation of its own.

**The two worlds.** The specification's world: (H) allowed, (O) enforced, call-site instantiation; no need for multiple instantiation exclusion. The 2011–2012 design's world: specialised generic overloads wanted (`tail[\X\]` beside `tail`, `justificationOfRTR.tex:76-84`), which (O) forbids outright; run-time instantiation for dispatch (`Papers/RuntimeInstantiation`, the title itself); and multiple instantiation exclusion adopted to make that sound (`Papers/Types/exclusion.tick:141-152`; `justificationOfRTR.tex:457-459`, `:476-478`, `:496-497`). Each world is coherent. The compiled checker implements the second (the worker showed this, correctly). The interpreter is on the specification's side of (H) (`interpreter/evaluator/types/FType.java:197-297`, no instantiation clause) and has its own rule on the overload side (`interpreter/evaluator/values/OverloadedFunction.java:496-498`). Rung P as briefed — the interpreter's (H) with the compiler's run-time-instantiated specialised overloads — is the one combination that belongs to neither world, and the worker's `probes/ProbeMIEPick.fss` is the program that shows it: links under every placement and dies with `IncompatibleClassChangeError` (`probes/probe-matrix.txt:64-68`, `:86-90`, `:108-112`). That measurement is right and it is the load-bearing fact of this rung.

## 4. Why this is a reserved fork and not a silent specification

Rule 4 of the brief: the specification answers a divergence; a silent specification is the reason to think harder, not to stop. The specification here is not silent. On the divergence "`pick[\X\]` beside `pick`: compiled accepts, `walk` refuses" it sides with `walk` (`overloading.tex:100-105`), which is rule 4's first outcome, repair. The repair is the sentence, and the sentence is what the batch record reserved to Pavol before this rung ran (`CLIMB-BATCH-3.md:18`; `triage.md:113`, "(b) is Pavol's decision about the specification, not a patch: the 35, and the sentence's reach over overrides"). It was reserved because the library the route makes the standard uses shapes the sentence refuses (the triage's 35 sites; `Library/Generator2.fss:52-57` in this ruling), and `POSITIONS.md:39` makes the library's practice the standard. A judge does not lift a reservation the coordinator wrote into the record Pavol launched with "go".

The candidates short of the sentence were derived and each falls on a stop of its own:

- The interpreter's rule ported to the compiled checker (a symbolic pair needs an excluding or subtype-related position everywhere) refuses generic-beside-generic sets with overlapping unrelated domains that the sentence permits (same parameters, different domains), which is a change of semantics against what the specification does say — `explorations/coordinator/PLAN.md:52`'s fork.
- The Return Type Rule quantified over every instantiation that can co-apply — the exact rule — is sound in every model, but its conservative form (hold a static parameter of the less specific declaration rigid when it occurs in the return type and the solution pins it to something other than a bare parameter of the more specific declaration) also refuses spec-valid generic-beside-generic pairs with parameter-dependent return types *unless* the code generator dispatches at the call site's instantiation. That fact is unmeasured (section 3, item 5), and reading codegen for it is a design pass, not a repair round.
- The restricted form that refuses only a non-generic specialisation beside a generic declaration whose return type depends on its parameter (the hole's exact shape; a sub-rule of the sentence, so not against the specification; accepts all the corpus sites of section 3 item 2) is sound only under the call-site model, for the same reason.

So every sound closure is the reserved sentence, or against the specification in a corner, or conditional on a fact about the code generator that no one has measured. That is the second design decision `CLIMB-BATCH-3.md:85` names, found by a probe instead of by the suite. Stop.

## 6. What reaches Pavol

**The fork.** The compiled checker's `checkP` is the 2011–2012 team's multiple instantiation exclusion. The specification refuses it (`types-vals-vars.tex:184-189`; `trait-parameters.tex:359-367`, `:383-397`) and the library route needs it gone (61 of the 93 errors, `CLIMB-BATCH-3.md:73`). Removing it, in any placement, opens one hole: the compiled overloading checker's Return Type Rule trusts the one instantiation that the rule used to guarantee (`OverloadingOracle.scala:88-95`), and a six-line program then type-checks and dies (`probes/ProbeMIEPick.fss`; `probe-matrix.txt:67`). The question is which rule replaces the reliance.

**The candidates and their costs.**

(a) **The specification's sentence** (`overloading.tex:100-108`): overloads of a name share their static parameters; a call site instantiates them once. This is triage decision (b). Closes the hole; makes the run-time choice of instantiation moot on the checker's side. Cost: refuses the corpus sites of section 3 item 2 — `compiler_tests/Compiled12.invariantInference.fss:52-61`, `Compiled6.ak.fss:14-15`, `Library/Generator2.fss:52-57`, four interpreter tests — none of which has the hole; the triage's 35 library sites, already errors today, then need renames rather than a checker accommodation; and the reading of "static parameters" for functional methods of generic traits must be fixed (`triage.md:57-59`), or the sentence refuses overrides. Deleting or rewriting gated tests is itself a reserved stop (`PLAN.md:52`), so this choice carries that cost explicitly.

(b) **The hole's exact shape, as a sub-rule of the sentence**: refuse a pair where one declaration has static parameters the other lacks and the generic one's return type depends on such a parameter, unless the more specific declaration's return type is a subtype with the parameter held rigid. Closes the hole; refuses nothing the specification permits; accepts every corpus site above; leaves decision (b) untouched. Cost: sound only if the code generator dispatches generic overloads at the call site's instantiation, which must be measured first (a generic-beside-generic pair with different bounds on a multiply-instantiated object, static type one instantiation; if the narrower-bounded declaration is chosen at the other instantiation, the rule must widen to the rigid check for generic-beside-generic pairs too, and then it is against the specification in that corner). One edit in `OverloadingOracle.satisfiesReturnTypeRule`.

(c) **The interpreter's rule** (`OverloadedFunction.java:496-498`): the library was written under it and passes it. Cost: against the specification for same-parameter generic overloads with unrelated overlapping domains; which compiled tests it refuses is unmeasured.

(d) **Keep the rule.** Not recommended: against the specification and the library route (section 3 item 1).

**Recommendation.** (b) as the closure that lands with the relaxation, in one rung, preceded by the code-generator measurement it depends on; decision (b) of the triage — the full sentence — stays his, taken on the library evidence, and subsumes (b) here if he takes it. Under either, the rung that lands the relaxation also lands `ProbeMIEPick` as a gated compile-error test, converts `XXXExclusionRelaxRungP` to the positive test the record asked for, and records the row-97 consequence (the overloading half refuses `Tag[\ZZ32\]` beside `Tag[\String\]`, `probe-overload.txt:12-19`) as the specification's Meet Rule taking effect, not a regression.

**Decisions taken in this ruling, and their alternatives.** None of the four candidates was chosen; that is his. Two decisions were taken: to strike "keep the rule" from what is put to him, on the citations of section 3 item 1 (the alternative was to pass the worker's two-way question through unchanged); and to keep the XXX test on the branch rather than remove it with the stop (the alternative, removal, would leave the deferred defect with no gated tripwire, against the brief's home 2).
