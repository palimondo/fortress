## FACTS.md

- **The compiled checker's `checkP` is the Fortress designers' own rule, "multiple instantiation exclusion", not an accident.** Sources: the OOPSLA 2011 paper, `Papers/Types/exclusion.tick:141-152` ("Fortress imposes a rule that forbids multiple instantiation inheritance ... We call this rule multiple instantiation exclusion and adopt it here"), and Karl Naden's writeup of 2012-08-31, `Papers/Types/journal/justificationOfRTR.tex:476-478`, `:496-497` ("specialization of generic functions or multiple instantiation inheritance, but not both"; "Ultimately, Fortress implements blanket multiple instantiation exclusion"). The rule is enforced by `TypeHierarchyChecker.scala:178`, `:187`, and the Return Type Rule check relies on it (`scala_src/overloading/OverloadingOracle.scala:81-105`). The interpreter took the other side of the trade-off: no instantiation clause in `FType.java:197-297`, and generic overloads without an excluding pair are refused (`OverloadedFunction.java:496-498`). The specification sides with the interpreter on both halves (`Specification/basic/types-vals-vars.tex:184-189`; `Specification/basic/overloading.tex:100-105`). The compiler prelude's commented-out `extends` clauses (`CompilerBuiltin.fss:1483`, `:1521`) are the compiled world keeping within the rule. (`explorations/compile-ladder/rung-exclusion-relax/REPORT.md`, "The stop")
- **Relaxing `checkP` alone is unsound on the compiled path, in every placement** (the two hierarchy questions; those plus the overloading check; every analyzer). `explorations/compile-ladder/rung-exclusion-relax/probes/ProbeMIEPick.fss` (`BadList` in six lines) type-checks under all three and dies with `IncompatibleClassChangeError`: a `Tag[\String\]` variable holds an object that is only a `Tag[\ZZ32\]` (`explorations/compile-ladder/rung-exclusion-relax/probes/probe-matrix.txt:67`, `:89`, `:111`).
