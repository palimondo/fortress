The number types of this chapter are the integer types Z , Z64 , Z32 , N64 , and N32 (Section 33.2), the rational type
Q , and the floating-point types R64 (RR64 ) and R32 (RR32 ). They are siblings under the trait Number: each is a
subtype of Number, and none is a subtype of another. Some conversions between them are coercions (Chapter 17),
each declared by the type converted to; since coercions do not chain (Section 17.1), each pair is declared. Those
among the integer types are given in Section 33.2; the others are these:

Q (QQ ) coerces from Z , Z64 , Z32 , N64 , and N32 .
R64 (RR64 ) coerces from R32 , from Z32 , and from integer numerals (Section 13.1).

Every other conversion between number types is written explicitly; in particular, a conversion into R64 from Z ,
Z64 , N64 , N32 , or Q is explicit, written asFloat(x) with the method that the Fortress standard libraries declare on
Number.

I.1.26      The single-precision floating-point type

Affected sections Section 33.1; Section I.1.10.
Change The passage that names the number types names R32 (RR32 ) beside R64 , a sibling of the others under
    Number, and says that R64 coerces from R32 . The effect of Section I.1.10 no longer says that the library’s
    R32 stays below R64 .
Rationale The Fortress standard libraries declared R32 a value object extending R64 , so that a value of R32
     was also a value of R64 , which Section 6.9 excludes: “These types are mutually exclusive; no value
     has more than one of them.” The team’s later Types chapter (Luchangco, 2012) keeps that sentence
     (Documentation/Specification/Prose/Language/types.tick, lines 977–978, in the revival’s
     repository). Route A makes the number types siblings under Number, each with its own algebra, and
     the revival decided on 26 September 2026 that a conversion between number types is a coercion where
     it is exact; every R32 value is exact in R64 . The Fortress standard libraries now declare R32 ex-
     tending Number and the four algebraic traits at its own type, as they declare R64 , and R64 ’s coercion
     from it (ProjectFortress/LibraryBuiltin/FortressBuiltin.fsi, lines 47–48; Library/
     FortressLibrary.fsi, lines 291–296). The team’s own compiler library has declared the two types sib-
     lings, with the same coercion, since its change of 31 August 2009, whose message says “RR32 is NOT a subtype
     of RR64” (ProjectFortress/LibraryBuiltin/CompilerBuiltin.fsi, lines 433–435 and 475).
     The example of Section 17.8 computes an R32 with an R64 by the declaration on R64 after a coercion from
     R32 , which is what the library now does. The full reasoning is in explorations/compile-ladder/
     rung-rr32-sibling/decision-record.md in the revival’s repository.
Effect A value of R32 is not a value of R64 . Two R32 values combine by the operators declared on R32 , into an
      R32 ; an R32 combined with an R64 , a floating-point numeral or a Z32 is converted to R64 , and the result is
      an R64 ; an R32 is converted where an R64 is expected. A conversion from R64 into R32 , which can lose
      precision, stays explicit.
