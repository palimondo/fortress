# The 2012 patents' forest rule for self-typed instantiations

Terms as in `../multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md`. "Self-typed generic" is the patents' term, defined in § 1.

## 1. The rule, verbatim and explained

Sources: US 8,843,887 B2 (Chase, Steele, Naden, Hilburn, Luchangco; filed 2012-08-31, granted 2014-09-23) and US 8,898,632 B2 (Naden, Hilburn, Chase, Steele, Luchangco, Allen; filed 2012-08-31, granted 2014-11-25), from https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8843887 and `…/8898632`. The PDFs are scans; the text below is OCR, with the one unclear line (the invariant clause) checked on a 300 dpi crop. Both patents print the same passages. Citations are to 8,843,887, with 8,898,632 in brackets.

- **The definition** (col. 6 l. 41-55 [col. 6 l. 46-60]): "A particular idiom used in Fortress is the "self-typed generic," where a generic in T also comprises exactly T. This usually corresponds to a property of a binary operator method such as "Comparable" or "AssociativePlus": `trait Comparable[\T\] comprises T  opr < (self, other:T)  end` … Because the only subtype of Comparable[\T\] is T, the two types include exactly the same sets of values, and are in some sense the same type."
- **Meet** (col. 6 l. 58-67): types should "form a lattice, not just a partial order … This requires union and intersection types to ensure that join and meet operations are defined, plus a "bottom" type. … When two types exclude each other, their meet is bottom."
- **The restrictions** open "Restrictions on Fortress types include the following:" (col. 7 l. 40 [col. 7 l. 53]). Three passages matter:
  - "Minimal instance of generic ancestors: If S<:G[\T⃗\], then there exists U⃗ such that for all T⃗ where S<:G[\T⃗\]: if G's ith static parameter is invariant, then Ui=Ti. if G's ith static parameter is covariant, then Ui<:Ti. if G's ith static parameter is contravariant, then Ti<:Ui. G[\U⃗\] is the minimal instance of G that S extends." (col. 7 l. 46-51 [col. 7 l. 60-66]). Its invariant line is the multiple instantiation exclusion.
  - "Self-typed constraints are an exception to this rule; it is permitted to declare that T1<:SomeSelfType[T1]. Because of the different subtyping structure of self-types, this is really more of an equality constraint than an inequality constraint." (col. 8 l. 3-7 [col. 8 l. 18-22]; "this rule" is the order in which constraints are written.)
  - "Self-types meet: if T<:U=S[\U\] and T<:V=S[\V\] then T<:S[\meet(U, V)\] and meet(U, V) must be a declared (not intersection) type. In practice, this means that the instantiations of a particular self-typed generic must form a forest." (col. 8 l. 8-12 [col. 8 l. 23-27]).
- **What uses it.** Dispatch tests the implementations in "most-to-least specific order … until a match is found" (col. 9 l. 8-11). The applicable one provides "bindings … for any static type parameters" from the arguments' dynamic types (col. 9 l. 30-32). Its `match` step looks for the argument type's instance of a generic: "if ∃ M, A <: M, stem(M) = G, M is minimal then …" (col. 10 l. 16-17). Inference then lifts the lower bound: "else if there are self-type constraints t <: Si[\t\] then search for t' above l such that t' <: Si[\t'\] for all Si; if t' exists then l ← t' else dispatch fails" (col. 12 l. 4-7; prose at l. 33-39).

What the words mean:
- **Self-typed.** `S[\T\] comprises T`: the instance at T holds exactly T's values. So `S[\U\] <: S[\V\]` whenever `U <: V`, and a self-typed generic behaves as if covariant although nothing declares it so ("the different subtyping structure"). The covariant line of "Minimal instance" then applies to it, and "Self-types meet" is that line plus the demand that the minimal instance have a name. The patents do not say how the two clauses combine; this reading is ours.
- **Meet.** The greatest common subtype. For two ordered types it is the lower one; for two unrelated types it is an intersection type, or Bottom when they exclude. "Declared (not intersection)" asks for a named type.
- **Forest.** Link each self-typed instantiation to the next wider one a type carries. A forest has no node with two unrelated parents, so each type's instantiations of one self-typed generic form a chain with a least element. That least element is the "minimal instance" the `match` step needs.
- **`comprises T`** is the test for being self-typed. Without it, `S[\ZZ32\]` and `S[\ZZ64\]` are two invariant instances, and the invariant clause forbids having both.

In plain words:
1. Blanket exclusion forbids a type to be two instantiations of one generic: `ZZ32` may not be both `Equality[\ZZ32\]` and `Equality[\ZZ64\]`.
2. The forest rule makes one exception: generics that comprise exactly their parameter (`Equality[\T\] comprises T`), whose instance at a type is that type.
3. For those, a type may carry several instantiations when they line up by subtyping. `ZZ32` may be `Equality` at `ZZ32`, `ZZ64`, `ZZ`, `QQ` and `Number`, because `ZZ32 <: ZZ64 <: ZZ <: QQ <: Number`.
4. The lowest of them is unique. Run-time dispatch binds the parameter to it: `ZZ32` for a `ZZ32` value.
5. Still forbidden: two unrelated instantiations of a self-typed generic, such as one type carrying `Equality[\ZZ64\]` and `Equality[\NN64\]`, unless a declared type below both is also carried.
6. Still forbidden: two instantiations of a generic that is not self-typed. `BadList extends { List[\String\], List[\ZZ\] }` stays illegal, so Naden's `tail[\X\]` beside `tail(x: List[\ZZ\])` keeps its guarantee.
7. `Both extends { Tag[\String\], Tag[\ZZ32\] }` (`ProbeMIEPick`) stays illegal twice over: `Tag` is not self-typed, and `String` and `ZZ32` are unrelated.

## 2. The library's tower under it

Measured with a shadow checker whose `checkP`, asked whether two types exclude, lets two instantiations of a self-typed generic exclude only when their arguments are unordered (§ 4). "Self-typed" there is the library's spelling (the parameter bounded by the generic at itself, `Equality[\T extends Equality[\T\]\]`) or the patents' (`comprises` exactly the parameter). On the interpreter's library the count falls from 103 to 37 (`forest/checker-count.txt:1-6`). By family, with the chains each declaration carries (all follow declared subtyping):
- **Tower, 13 declarations, 31 errors: all lifted.** `Number` is `StandardPartialOrder` and `StandardMinMax` at `Number`, and through them `Equality`, `StandardMin` and `StandardMax` (`Library/FortressLibrary.fsi:276-278`). `QQ` adds `StandardPartialOrder[\QQ\]` (`:373`). `Integral[\I\]` adds `StandardTotalOrder[\I\]` and its parents at `I` (`:412`). So `ZZ32`'s `Equality` chain is `ZZ32 <: ZZ64 <: ZZ <: QQ <: Number` and its `Integral` chain `ZZ32 <: ZZ64 <: ZZ`. `NN32`'s are `NN32 <: NN64 <: ZZ <: QQ <: Number` and, for `Integral`, `NN64 <: ZZ`: `NN32` is `StandardTotalOrder[\NN32\]` but not `Integral[\NN32\]` (`FortressBuiltin.fsi:82`). `Integral`'s own clash, `StandardPartialOrder` at `I` and at `QQ`, is ordered through the bound, `I <: Integral[\I\] <: AnyIntegral <: QQ`. `Int`, `Long`, `UnsignedLong`, `IntLiteral` and `BigNum` inherit a parent's chains.
- **Comparisons, 4 declarations, 7 errors: lifted.** `TotalComparison` is `Equality` and `StandardPartialOrder` at itself and at `Comparison` (`:100-101`, `:120-121`), and `TotalComparison <: Comparison`. The three objects inherit.
- **`Maybe`, 4 declarations, 10 errors: lifted.** `AnyMaybe` is `Equality` at itself and at `AnyUniqueItem` (`:817`, `:886`), and it extends `AnyUniqueItem` in the same clause. `Maybe[\T\]`, `Just` and `Nothing` inherit.
- **Reductions, 2 declarations, 13 errors: kept.** `DistributesOver[\E\]` (`:1775`) has no bound and no methods, so it is not self-typed, and its arguments `MaxReductionN`, `MinReductionN` and `SumReduction` are unrelated objects. The rule does not reach these markers. They need another encoding, one marker trait per fact, or route C's per-trait exemption. Covariance would not help, since the three have no common subtype. The only reader of the marker is inside a comment (`Library/Generator2.fss:59-68`).

So 48 of the 61 errors go and the 13 marker errors stay (`forest/checker-count.txt`, from line 7: the errors that go and the one that appears). The other change in the count, in `RangeInternals`, is in § 4. `sites.tsv`'s line numbers are from an older revision of the `.fsi`; the ones above are today's.

**Under the patents' own test nothing is lifted.** Mode `comprises` counts 103: no trait in the library comprises its parameter. To pass that test, the seven traits need the clause, in the `.fsi` and the `.fss`:

    trait Equality[\T extends Equality[\T\]\] comprises T
        abstract opr =(self, other:T): Boolean
    end
    trait Integral[\I extends Integral[\I\]\] extends { StandardTotalOrder[\I\], AnyIntegral } comprises I

The same goes on `StandardPartialOrder`, `StandardMin`, `StandardMax`, `StandardMinMax` and `StandardTotalOrder` (`.fsi:167-219`). The compiler's own prelude already spells two of them that way: `trait Equality[\T\] comprises T` and `trait StandardTotalOrder[\T\] … comprises T` (`Library/CompilerAlgebra.fsi:16`, `:24`; present in the snapshot lineage by 2012-01-20, `712a2969a`). Not measured: whether `Integral`'s `comprises I` fits beside `AnyIntegral comprises { ZZ }` (`.fsi:409`), which already costs one "Invalid comprises clause" error.

## 3. Soundness under it, and the probe

**What the minimal instantiation does and does not do.** In the patents it binds a generic's parameter inside the implementation that dispatch has already chosen (col. 10 l. 16-17, col. 12 l. 4-7). Which implementation runs is decided before that, by testing them "most-to-least specific" (col. 9 l. 8-11). A specialisation `f(x: Eq[\Mid\]): Mid` is more specific than `f[\X\](x: Eq[\X\]): X`, so it runs for every value that is an `Eq[\Mid\]`, a `Narrow` included.

**In Naden's terms.** `tail(List[\ZZ\])` is safe because no value is a `List[\ZZ\]` and a `List[\String\]` at once. On a forest every `Narrow` is an `Eq[\Mid\]` and an `Eq[\Narrow\]` at once: a legal `BadList`, ordered. Callers typed at `Mid` or above are safe, since the specialisation's `Mid` fits `X = Mid` or `X = Wide`. A caller the checker types at `X = Narrow` is promised a `Narrow` and gets `M`. That caller exists whenever the argument's static type does not show that it is an `Eq[\Mid\]`: a variable of type `Eq[\Narrow\]`, or any generic code over `T extends Eq[\T\]`. The checker's return type rule does not see it. It solves one instantiation (`OverloadingOracle.scala:88`, giving `X = Mid`) and checks that one arrow (`:97`), which is sound only when no value has a second instantiation. So a forest keeps a specialisation sound only if the return type rule checks every self-typed level below it, and (from `ForestTowerC` under `cov` below) only if the checker's static choice and the dispatcher's order agree when a generic's lower instance is more specific than the specialisation. A specialisation at a leaf of the forest, with no self-typed level below, passes. A specialisation at an inner level passes only if every level below has its own, more specific declaration. The patents state no such rule; it is derived here from the probes.

**The probes** (`forest/`, run by `forest-run.sh`; walk on the interpreter, compiled under `-Dprobe.rungP=off`, `hier`, and the forest shadow `-Dprobe.forest=fbound`):
- `ForestInfer`, the generic alone, returning `Marker[\X\]` for an `N` reached through each static type. Walk gives `Marker[Narrow]` for all six, the minimal instance from the run-time value (`ForestInfer.walk.txt:2-7`). Compiled under the forest shadow gives `Marker[Wide]` through `Narrow`, `Mid` and `Wide` variables and the variable's own level through `Eq[·]` variables (`ForestInfer.compiled.txt:40-45`): the call is not dispatched, so the checker's static argument is used, and its inference picks the top of the chain. Under `off` and `hier` the calls through `Eq[\Narrow\]` and `Eq[\Mid\]` do not check at all ("… is not applicable to an argument of type Eq[\Narrow\]"): outside the hierarchy check the blanket rule still applies to `Narrow`'s two instantiations (not traced further).
- `ForestTower`, the pair of `f` called for `N` through `Narrow`, `Mid`, `Eq[\Mid\]`, `Wide`, `Eq[\Wide\]`, and last through `Eq[\Narrow\]`. Walk refuses the pair at load: "with generic type, at least one pair of parameters must have excluding types" (`ForestTower.walk.txt:2-6`), as it refuses `ProbeMIEPick`. Compiled under the forest shadow, the first five calls print `M`: the specialisation runs for `N` through every static type. The last, through `Eq[\Narrow\]`, is checked as the generic at `X = Narrow` and dies: "IncompatibleClassChangeError: Class ForestTower$M does not implement the requested interface ForestTower$Narrow" (`ForestTower.compiled.txt:38-46`).
- `ForestTowerC` is `ForestTower` with the patents' `comprises T`. Compiled, the output is identical (`ForestTowerC.compiled.txt:38-46`): the checker does not use the clause to place `Eq[\Narrow\]` below `Eq[\Mid\]`. Walk overflows the stack (`ForestTowerC.walk.txt:2`); `ForestTowerCNoSpec`, the same program with the specialisation renamed, runs (`ForestTowerCNoSpec.walk.txt:2-9`), so the overflow is in the interpreter's check of the overloaded pair. Under the shadow's `cov` mode (see `ForestGenericLeaf`) the first call, `f(n)` through a `Narrow` variable, already dies the same way (`ForestTowerC.compiled.txt:48-53`). The reading that fits (the checker's choice was not printed): the checker now finds the generic's instance at `Narrow` more specific than the specialisation at `Mid` and promises a `Narrow`, while the dispatcher still tries the specialisation first, since it orders declarations, as the patents' does (col. 9 l. 8-11).
- `ForestGeneric`: `h[\T extends Eq[\T\]\](x: T): T = f(x)`, with `comprises T`. `h[\Wide\](N)` and `h[\Mid\](N)` give `M`; `h[\Narrow\](N)` dies with the same error (`ForestGeneric.compiled.txt:4-7`), and the same under `cov` (`:12-15`). This is the library's shape: generic code over a self-typed bound.
- `ForestGenericLeaf` adds `f(x: Eq[\Narrow\]): Narrow`, the declaration at the level below that would close the hole. The forest shadow refuses it: "Invalid overloading of f … Eq[\Mid\]->Mid … and Eq[\Narrow\]->Narrow" (`ForestGenericLeaf.compiled.txt:2-6`), because the two domains no longer exclude and nothing orders them. A second mode, `cov`, reads a generic that comprises exactly its parameter covariantly, the patents' "different subtyping structure": `Eq[\Narrow\] <: Eq[\Mid\]`. Under it the program links, and `h[\Narrow\](N)` holds `N` (`:10-15`). The declaration at the level below closes the hole.
- **In the library** the shape is real. `ZZ64` declares `opr TIMES(self,b:ZZ64):ZZ64` (`.fsi:519`) beside `Integral`'s `opr TIMES(self,b:I):I` (`:419`), and `ZZ32`, the level below, declares no `TIMES`; generic code at `I = ZZ32` would reach `ZZ64`'s. `QQ`'s and `Number`'s `MIN`, `MAX` and `MINMAX` (`:388-390`, `:289-291`) return their own level beside `StandardMin`'s, `StandardMax`'s and `StandardMinMax`'s generic ones, and no integer level declares them. These are the inner-level specialisations with the parameter in the return type among route C's 69 pairs (`routeC-library.txt`), which leave out the symbolic operators; the rest have a declaration at every level below, or a return type without the parameter.

**The call-site fix** (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md`, landed) is still needed under the forest rule. On a chain one value is exactly an `Eq` at several levels (`N` at `Narrow`, `Mid` and `Wide`), and the call site may hold any of them (`ForestInfer`: the checker takes `Wide`, the interpreter `Narrow`). A dispatched generic arm reads its argument from the value's run-time type, which records one per generic (`dispatch-and-route-c.md` § 1). On this chain that one is the minimal instance: `N`'s run-time type asks `Narrow`'s, which holds the `Eq⟦Narrow⟧` built from its own direct supertype (`forest/javap-rtti.txt`). So for a chain the compiled run time already does minimal-instance dispatch, as the interpreter does, because each level lists its own instantiation directly; undispatched calls take the call site's argument instead. Where the result holds the parameter invariantly, as `Marker[\X\]` does, the arm's `Marker[\Narrow\]` then breaks a call site's `Marker[\Wide\]` (not probed through dispatch on a chain). The comment the scope note quotes, "if each static parameter is mentioned in any invariant occurrence, then the supplied type is exact" (`OverloadSet.java:1981-1990`), does not hold on a chain, where every level is exact. The fix does not touch the specialisation hole: a non-generic declaration has no static parameters to take from the call site.

## 4. The checker change, sized

- **`checkP`, built and measured** (`forest/forest.patch`, the `probe.forest` hunks). In `cP`, when the constructor is self-typed and the question is positive (`!negate`), `pEqv(t1, t2)` becomes "neither `t1 <: t2` nor `t2 <: t1`". A new `forestSelfTyped` tests the bound or the `comprises` clause. That is 39 added lines with the mode switch and comments, about 30 without them. It sits in `TypeAnalyzer` itself, so every analyzer sees it: the placement rung P found necessary. The two hierarchy questions (`TypeHierarchyChecker.scala:178`, `:187`) need no edit, since both call `definitelyExcludes`. The negated direction, which feeds inference's equality constraints, is left unchanged.
- **The covariant reading, built** (mode `cov`, about 3 lines). In the same-constructor case of `pSubInner` (`TypeAnalyzer.scala:236-259`), a parameter of a generic that comprises exactly it is compared like a declared covariant one. The tree has an older attempt at the same effect: a comprises-based rule in the trait-trait case, commented out with "Gets a stack overflow error; maybe we don't need this in practice.  GLS 6/6/12" (`:268-271`). It makes `ForestGenericLeaf` legal and exposes the order mismatch of § 3. The matching dispatcher change is not sized: it would test a generic arm at the value's minimal instance against the specialisations (`OverloadSet.java:1303-1760`).
- **The count** (`forest/checker-count.txt`): stock 103; forest 37, and 37 with the covariant reading; rung P's `hier` 42 and `broad` 24 on today's tree for comparison; the patents' literal test 103. Against stock, the forest removes the 48 hierarchy errors of § 2. It also removes 19 overloading errors in `RangeInternals` (`combine2D`, `combine3D`, `CAP`) and adds one (`IN`, `RangeInternals.fsi:429` against `:505`). The forest's 37 is rung P's broad 24 plus exactly the 13 marker errors.
- **The rung's probe matrix under it** (`forest/rung-probes.forest.txt`): `ProbeMIEPick` is still refused with the same four errors, `ProbeMIEPickCtl` links and runs, `ProbeTypecaseMIE` is still refused (`G` is not self-typed), and `ProbeMIEOverload` links and runs. All four behave as under the stock checker.
- **`OverloadingOracle.satisfiesReturnTypeRule` (`:81-105`) must change, not built.** After `subEDsolution` gives the one instantiation (`:88`), and when that instantiation is a self-typed generic's at a type `M`, the rule must also check the generic's return type at every declared type below `M` that carries the generic at itself, unless a more specific declaration of the same name covers that type. Three things stopped the build. `TypeAnalyzer` has no "declared subtypes of `M`" query; the trait table is keyed by name. The coverage test needs the whole overload set, and the oracle is handed one pair (`OverloadingChecker.scala:421-422`). And `ForestGenericLeaf` shows that the covering declaration cannot be written until the subtyping reads `comprises T` covariantly. Estimated at 40-70 lines across `OverloadingOracle`, `OverloadingChecker` and `TypeAnalyzer`. The symbolic operators (`=`, `<`, `+`) would still escape the check, as they escape the overloading checker today (`dispatch-and-route-c.md` § 2).

## 5. The Welterweight draft and POPL 2019

The draft states the ancestors rule in meet form, not the forest condition. `Papers/Welterweight/static.tick:83-89`: "if a trait extends (directly or indirectly) two different instances T⟦α⟧ and T⟦η⟧ of the same trait T, then it must extend some instance T⟦γ⟧ of T (which could be T⟦α⟧, T⟦η⟧, or a third instance) such that T⟦γ⟧ is a subtype of T⟦α⟧ and also a subtype of T⟦η⟧. A consequence is that if an object value belongs to one or more instances of some trait T, there is a *unique minimal instance* of T to which it belongs." Its rule `[Anc-Same-Trait]` (`fig-meetrule.tick:111-116`) relates instances only through declared variance (`grammar.tick:48-49`), and the draft has no `comprises T` self types. For an invariant trait that forces α = η, the blanket rule. It is the patents' "Minimal instance of generic ancestors" clause, for all three variances. The self-typed part survives only as a commented-out dispatch step, "%\subsection{Self-typed generics}" with "search for $t'$ above $l$ such that $t' <: S_i\OBR{t'}$ for all $S_i$" (`dispatch.tick:195-212`). That is the patents' col. 12 l. 4-7 word for word, without the restriction that makes it well defined. POPL 2019's `[Anc-Same-Trait]` is the same rule and has no self types (`literature.md` § 1).

## 6. Not settled

- The return type rule a forest needs is derived here from the probes. No source states it: the patents describe dispatch, Welterweight dropped the self-typed step, and no paper was found.
- Whether the checker should give `comprises T` the patents' "different subtyping structure" (`Eq[\Narrow\] <: Eq[\Mid\]`). Without it the covering declarations of § 3 cannot be written. With it, as `cov` shows, static choice and dispatch diverge until dispatch orders a specialisation against the generic's minimal instance.
- Which instance a checked call should take on a chain. The compiled checker takes the top (`Wide`), the interpreter and the patents the bottom (`Narrow`).
- `Integral`'s `comprises I` beside `AnyIntegral comprises { ZZ }`; the interpreter's stack overflow on `ForestTowerC`'s pair; the negated direction of `checkP`. None was measured.

**The routes.** Route A keeps the rule and flattens the tower: no checker work, 14 library declaration headers, the dependent sites and the interpreter's coercion that `price-keep-the-rule.md` counts, and specialisation kept; the count goes to 22. Route B drops the rule and instantiates at the call site with no specialised overloads: rung P's 22-line relaxation, the 43-line dispatcher change of `scope-call-site-dispatch.md`, and a refusal of non-generic specialisations, with which the library's specialised pairs over the self-typed traits (69 in route C's count) conflict; the count goes to 24 (§ 4). The forest rule is a third route that keeps all three: the rule for every generic that is not self-typed (`BadList`, `Both` and `G` stay illegal), the nested tower (48 of 61 errors lifted, `SumReduction` and `ProdReduction` re-encoded), and specialisation; the count goes to 37 before the markers are re-encoded. Its cost is five pieces:
1. The measured `checkP` change, about 30 lines.
2. `comprises T` on the seven traits, or the F-bound accepted as the test.
3. `comprises T` read covariantly in subtyping (3 lines in the shadow), and dispatch made to agree with the checker's resulting static choice (not sized).
4. The return type rule over the forest, estimated at 40-70 lines.
5. The library's inner-level specialisations completed at every level below, seven declarations found without them (`ZZ64`'s `TIMES`, and `QQ`'s and `Number`'s `MIN`, `MAX`, `MINMAX`), plus the call-site fix that route B also needs.

That is more checker work than route B and much less library work than route A. It is the direction the type group's own dispatch text points to, in the patents and in Welterweight's commented-out step. The checker never implemented it and no paper published it.
