## Precedent search

The question asked was "has the team solved this here already, and in how many ways", and the answer is in the file being edited.

**Widening `ZZ32` to `ZZ64`: one way.** `coerce(x: ZZ32) = jIntToLong(x)` (`CompilerBuiltin.fss:575`). `widen` uses the same native.

**Narrowing `ZZ64` to `ZZ32`: seven natives bound, one used.** The import block binds `longToInt` (`:33`), `longOverflowingToInt` (`:40`), `longLowHalfToInt` (`:41`), `longHighHalfToInt` (`:42`), `longMaskedToInt` (`:43`), `longWrappingToInt` (`:51`) and `longSaturatingToInt` (`:58`). Past the import block, `longOverflowingToInt` is used once — `getter asZZ32(): ZZ32` at `:576` — and `longLowHalfToInt` sixteen times, every one of them a shift count where truncation modulo the word width is the operation. The other five are used nowhere. So the plainly-named signed narrowing in this world already exists and it throws.

**Bit reinterpretation: four getters.** `ZZ64.bitsAsNN64` (`:578`), `ZZ32.bitsAsNN32` (`:663`), `NN32.bitsAsZZ32` (`:753`), `NN64.bitsAsZZ64` (`:819`). `unsigned` and `signed` are the functional-method spellings of exactly these four, and their bodies are the same four native calls.

**Widening `NN32` to `NN64`: one site, and it is wrong.** `jIntToLong(jMakeZZ32FromNN32(...))` appeared once in the whole file, at what is now `:816`, and `jIntToLong` appeared twice in total, there and at `:575`. That is the count rule 2 asks for: one site, repaired here, and no second one. In the landed tree `jIntToLong` is again at two sites, `coerce(x: ZZ32)` at `:575` and the new `ZZ32.widen` at `:745`, and neither has an `NN32` on the other side of it.

**Narrowing `NN64` to `NN32`: three natives bound, none used.** `longOverflowingToUnsignedInt` (`:86`), `longWrappingToUnsignedInt` (`:94`) and `longSaturatingToUnsignedInt` (`:101`) are all imported and none is called anywhere in the file. The slot was reserved and never filled.

**The convention that decides which of the three to use.** In `trait NN32` and `trait NN64` the plainly-spelled member always takes the Overflowing native and the wrapping and saturating spellings take the other two: `opr +` takes `unsignedIntOverflowingAdd` at `:763` while `opr BOXPLUS` takes the Wrapping one at `:764` and `opr DOTPLUS` the Saturating one at `:765`, and the same triple runs through `-`, `DOT`, `CROSS`, `DIV`, unary `-`, `|self|`, `CHOOSE` and juxtaposition. Thirteen plainly-named members of those two traits take an Overflowing native; nineteen more do in `trait ZZ32` and `trait ZZ64`. `narrow` is a plain name, so by the file's own convention it takes `longOverflowingToUnsignedInt`.

**The interpreter, read in the glue rather than in the api.** `Int$ToLong` is `(long) x` (`interpreter/glue/prim/Int.java:221-224`), sign-extension, and `NN32$ToUnsignedLong` is `Unsigned.toLong(x)` (`NN32.java:237-241`), zero-extension — so `widen` is sign-extending on the signed side and zero-extending on the unsigned side, which is what the two bodies do. `ZZ32$ToNN32` (`ZZ32.java:213-217`), `NN32$ToInt` (`NN32.java:231-235`), `Long$ToUnsignedLong` (`Long.java:231-235`) and `UnsignedLong$ToLong` (`UnsignedLong.java:224-228`) all return their argument unchanged — `unsigned` and `signed` reinterpret bits. `Long$FromLong` is `Int.rc(x)` (`Long.java:237-241`), and `Int.rc` raises `error("Overflow of ZZ32 " + i)` when the value does not fit (`Int.java:239-245`). `UnsignedLong$FromLong` is `(int) x` (`UnsignedLong.java:230-234`), a silent truncation.

**No competing declaration.** No `.fss` under `ProjectFortress/tests/` or any `*_tests/` directory declares any of the four; the only other declarations in the tree are the interpreter prelude's own and `ProjectFortress/not_working_static_tests/BuiltinTest.fss:68,72`, which declares its own `widen` and `unsigned` inside its own trait and is not in a gated corpus. A whole-tree grep of `ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/` for the four names as Fortress identifiers finds nothing: every hit for `widen` is the `widens` reserved word (`parser/Keyword.rats`, `nodes/WhereCoerces.java`, `scala_src/typechecker/impls/Operators.scala`) and every hit for `narrow` is `narrowToRange` or unrelated.

## The specification, and the decision it does not make

The four names are absent from the prose chapters. The grep, re-run 2026-09-20 over `Specification/basic/` and `Specification/basic-lib/`, finds five lines and not one of them is a declaration or a description of these functions: `basic/types-vals-vars.tex:508` and `:514` use "unsigned" as an adjective while listing the fixed-size type names, `basic-lib/objects.tex:63` and `:103` describe a hash code as an unsigned integer, and `basic-lib/basic-integers.tex:802` says "**k** bits suffice to represent this integer in unsigned binary form" about `integerLength`. `widen` appears only as the reserved word `widens` (`basic/conversions-coercions.tex:766`, `:784`), which is the automatic-widening mechanism of `\secref{widening}` — widest-need evaluation of an expression, described at `:771-787` in terms of promoting 32-bit floating-point operands before a product is computed — and is a different thing from a named conversion function. `basic-lib/basic-integers.tex` has one section, `\section{Integers}` at `:13`, and it specifies the methods of `ZZ`; there is no section for `ZZ32`, `ZZ64`, `NN32` or `NN64` anywhere under `basic-lib/`.

What the specification does say about a result that does not fit, read in full rather than grepped: `basic/operators/opr-overview.tex:154-159` and again verbatim at `:195-200`, "The handling of overflow depends on the type of the number produced. For integer results, overflow throws an `IntegerOverflow`." And `:205-209`, "Wraparound addition and subtraction on fixed-size integers are expressed by ∔ and ∸. Saturating addition and subtraction on fixed-size integers are expressed by ⊞ and ⊟. These operations do not overflow." — so the wrapping and the saturating behaviours have their own spellings and the plainly spelled one is the one that may overflow. `basic-lib/basic-integers.tex:368-370` is the same statement from the other side: on `ZZ` the wrapping and saturating operators "do exactly the same thing, because operations on type `ZZ` never need to wrap or saturate".

**The decision, and it is a decision: `narrow` throws `IntegerOverflow` when the value does not fit, on both the signed and the unsigned side.** Four things argue for it. The file's own convention, counted above: thirty-two plainly-named members of the four numeric traits take an Overflowing native, and the wrapping and saturating spellings take the other two. This world's own signed narrowing, `asZZ32` at `:576`, already throws, so a truncating `narrow` would put two plainly-named signed narrowings with different answers in one trait. The team's own import block reserved all three unsigned narrowings and used none, so the plain slot was left open and the convention says which of the three fills it. And the specification's twice-stated rule that an integer result that does not fit throws `IntegerOverflow`, with truncation given its own spelling elsewhere.

**What the alternative costs, since the alternative has real evidence behind it.** Truncating on the unsigned side is what the interpreter does (`UnsignedLong.java:230-234`) and what `ProjectFortress/tests/UnsignedTest.fss:210-211` assert, at the deliberately chosen out-of-range values 2^63 and 2^64-1: `widen(narrow(-x1)) = (x1 LSHIFT 32)-1` and `widen(narrow(x1 LSHIFT 63)) = x0`. Those two lines are gated: `ProjectFortress/tests/` holds 383 `.fss` and no `.test` file at all, and `FileTests.interpreterSuite("tests", true, false)` (`FileTests.java:768`) runs every one of them directly, so an `assert` that fails there turns `ant testSystem` red. The file passes today — measured, `probes/walk-unsignedtest.txt`, `PASS?` and exit 0. So choosing to throw makes those two assertions a walk-against-compiled divergence on the day `UnsignedTest.fss` compiles, and the specification does not settle it because it does not mention `narrow`. That divergence is measured both ways here and carried as provisional ledger row W-2; it is the price of the decision and it is not hidden. The two remaining arguments for truncating are weaker than they look: the interpreter's signed narrow raises an uncatchable Java-level error rather than a Fortress exception (measured, `probes/walk-narrow.txt`), so the glue is not a careful witness on this point; and the asymmetry it would install has no statement anywhere in the tree to support it.

## The measured defects, and where each one lives

Five defects were measured in the course of this rung. One is repaired here and gated; three get provisional ledger rows because the specification is silent or because the repair is in Java outside a library rung; one is an open row already.

**D1, repaired here, home 1 — `NN64.coerce(x: NN32)` sign-extended.** Measured before the edit, `probes/probe-coerce-nn-preedit.txt`: an `NN32` holding 4294967295 coerced to `NN64` became 18446744073709551615, and one holding 2147483648 became 18446744071562067968. The specification settles it: `Specification/basic/conversions-coercions.tex:59-61` says coercion lets one "use a value of one type `T` as if it were a value of another type `U`", and `:73-83` that "given any value of type `T`, an appropriate value of type `U` can be computed to substitute for it" — a substitute that is a different number is not an appropriate one. Repaired by replacing `jIntToLong` with the zero-extending `jUnsignedIntToLong`, and gated by three assertions in `IntConversionsRungW.fss` (the `takesNN64` calls at 4294967295, 2147483648 and 43), each carrying `conversions-coercions.tex:73-75` in its message. The assertions are in place and pass.

**D2, deferred, home 3 — `narrow` out of range diverges between the two paths, and the specification is silent.** Measured both ways, `probes/probe-NarrowUnsigned.txt`: under `walk`, `narrow` of an `NN64` holding 2^64-1 gives 4294967295; on the compiled path it raises `fortress.CompilerBuiltin$IntegerOverflow` from `simpleUnsignedIntArith.toUnsignedIntOverflow(simpleUnsignedIntArith.java:27)` through `NN64.narrow` at `CompilerBuiltin.fss:874`. This is the divergence this rung's decision creates, deliberately and with its reasons above. No prose chapter names `narrow`, so nothing settles it; the home is a committed probe and provisional ledger row **W-2**, which says in as many words that it is here because the specification is silent.

**D3, deferred, home 3 — under `walk`, a signed `narrow` that overflows cannot be caught.** Measured both ways, `probes/probe-NarrowSigned.txt` and `probes/walk-narrow.txt`: the interpreter's `Int.rc` raises `error("Overflow of ZZ32 " + i)` (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/interpreter/glue/prim/Int.java:239-245`), which arrives as a `com.sun.fortress.exceptions.ProgramError` and is **not** caught by `try … catch e IntegerOverflow ⇒ …`, so a program that guards the conversion dies anyway; on the compiled path the same guard prints `true`. Here the specification does settle it, and against the interpreter: `Specification/basic/operators/opr-overview.tex:155` says an integer overflow throws an `IntegerOverflow`; `basic/exceptions.tex:73-74` says every exception is a subtype of `CheckedException` or `UncheckedException`, and `CompilerBuiltin.fsi:749` makes `IntegerOverflow` an `UncheckedException`; and `basic/expressions/try.tex:56-60` says a thrown exception "is matched against the subclauses of the `catch` clause in turn, exactly as in a `typecase` expression". A thrown `IntegerOverflow` is therefore catchable by a `catch e IntegerOverflow` clause, and under `walk` it is not. The repair is `Int.rc` raising a Fortress exception instead of calling `error`, which is Java and outside a library rung — rule 4's fourth case, so the rung lands and opens provisional ledger row **W-3**.

That defect cannot take home 2, and the reason is worth recording because it bounds where expected-failure tests can live. `shouldFail` is set from the file name only in the `.test`-driven suites — `FileTests.java:932`, `boolean shouldFail = s.startsWith("XXX")` inside `suiteFromListOfFiles` (`:897`) — and the interpreter corpus has no `.test` files at all (383 `.fss` and none), so `interpreterSuite` sets `shouldFail` from its own suite-level `expect_failure` argument instead (`FileTests.java:768` passes `false` for `tests`, and `:825-829` hands it to each `InterpreterTest`). So an interpreter-only defect has no gated expected-failure home in this tree, and a ledger row is the instrument rule 4 names for it.

## Decisions, each with the alternative rejected

1. **`narrow` throws `IntegerOverflow` out of range on both sides.** Rejected: truncating on the unsigned side to match the interpreter and `UnsignedTest.fss:210-211`. Argued in full above; the cost of the choice is ledger row W-2 and the cost of the alternative would have been breaking a convention the file being edited follows at thirty-two sites.
2. **`NN64.signed` is declared `: ZZ64`.** Rejected: copying the interpreter api's `: NN64` (`FortressLibrary.fsi:461`). That spelling makes the function an identity on `NN64`, which is not what its own glue computes (`UnsignedLong.java:59-60`) and not what the interpreter's own test relies on (`UnsignedTest.fss:219`); and the compiler path has a type checker that would hold the declaration to its word. Recorded as a deviation and as ledger row W-1.
3. **One native binding is added, `simpleUnsignedIntArith.toLong`.** Rejected: writing the zero-extension in Fortress as a `BITAND` against a 4294967295 mask. The mask would have to arrive as a numeral, and numerals of exactly 32 bits are the subject of rows 317 and 325; paying that risk to avoid one line in an import block of forty-two bindings from the same class is a bad trade. The method is already public and is what every unsigned `ZZ32` operation in that class uses internally (`simpleUnsignedIntArith.java:19-23`).
4. **`NN64.coerce(x: NN32)` is repaired in this rung rather than deferred.** Rejected: leaving it and opening a row. It is a one-token change in a trait body this rung already owns, no other rung of the batch touches the file, and leaving it would have put two spellings of the same conversion in one trait giving different answers — `widen(x)` zero-extending and `coerce` sign-extending — which is the state the rung exists to end.
5. **The eight declarations go at the end of each trait body.** Rejected: putting them beside the `bitsAs*` getters they share a mechanism with. The interpreter's own api puts all six of its equivalents at the end of their traits (`FortressLibrary.fsi:493-495`, `:532`, `FortressBuiltin.fsi:105-107`), and following that keeps the diff a pure append inside each trait.
6. **The gated test renders `NN32` and `NN64` results through `.asZZ.asString`.** Rejected: `.asString`, which would have made the test assert ledger row 326's defect and fail for a reason that is not this rung's; and rejected: comparing values with `=` against constants built from the same four names, which would be circular. `asZZ` grounds each value in `ZZ`, whose rendering is measured correct in the same probe.
