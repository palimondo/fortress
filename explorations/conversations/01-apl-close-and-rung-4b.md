# The conversation around the APL side quest's close and rung 4b

## Why this file exists

Two days of work closed the APL ladder, ran the microGPT program through it, found that its calling convention broke under parallelism, rewrote that convention, and produced a characterization of Fortress from the whole gap ledger. The questions Pavol asked along the way were what moved the work, and they carry his reasons in a way the reports do not. This is the record of those questions.

## What Pavol was after, in his own framing

The toy problem of the whole revival is microGPT, Karpathy's 243-line character-level transformer, and the search is for its most readable expression. Fortress was one candidate: a language claiming a mathematical notation that should bring the code close to the higher-level concepts. APL was Pavol's later discovery, "a different specialized syntax for distilling ideas, in Ken Iverson's tradition", and Aaron Hsu's talks on data design for data-flow algorithms he found "invigorating". The APL side quest began on 2026-09-10 as a question asked while other work ran ("since APL's symbols are Unicode and Fortress is extensible, can APL be a DSL in Fortress?"), became a ladder up Kruger's "Learning APL" on 09-11 because Pavol wanted to learn APL and the book was recommended, was redirected on 09-12 from "an interpreter inside an interpreter" to "convert APL syntax into efficient Fortress code over native arrays", and got its target on 09-13 when Pavol brought the microGPT-in-APL program from another session. The ladder, he said later, was "to keep you busy and expand the probe" while he had not yet read the book. What he hoped for from the Hsu-style flat program was a diamond; what came out, to his eye, was "a few lumps of coal", and the hope for the rewrite was to get closer.

## The questions, in order, and what each revealed

**"Was anything running?"** The iOS client showed a stop button with nothing visible in flight, three times over two days. Each time an inventory (agents, Java processes, file timestamps) showed what was true; twice a worker had finished and its notice was lost to a stop. Revealed: the session's state must be checkable from the tree, not from the UI, which is what the handover file and the commit-as-you-go discipline are for.

**"Explain the loose ends in plain English."** Three: early findings never merged into the ledger (merged the same day as rows 266–283), the ledger's fix list stale by a hundred rows (re-derived later), and two APL chapters skipped by decision. Revealed: an item Pavol has to ask about twice is an item that should have been done or explained the first time.

**"This seems like a serious bug in the Fortress library."** It was not. The frame stack that crashed at pool size 4 is ours, built in rung 4 of the ladder as the calling convention for APL's anonymous functions, and Fortress did what its specification says: the elements of a tuple are evaluated in parallel. Revealed: the distinction between the sealed language and our layer above it has to be stated every time a failure is reported, because the reader reads "Fortress" first.

**"What the fuck is going on? Restate the presumptions, then teach me."** The presumptions: the sub-language translates APL into native Fortress; no interpreter inside the interpreter; and, implied, the expansion should be code a Fortress programmer could have written, keeping Fortress's properties including implicit parallelism. Rung 4 had tried the natural translation of `{⍵…}` to `fn (omega) => …` and hit two walls: the interpreter refuses every nested re-declaration of a name (probes u01–u03), and hygiene renames a binder written by one rule so that a reference written by another never meets it (ledger row 204). It built a global frame stack instead, wrote "FORTRESS_THREADS=1 is assumed" in one line, and no rung ever ran at another pool size. Revealed: the workaround broke the third presumption, it should have been flagged to Pavol when made, and the microGPT program never nests a function, so the universal mechanism was bought for the book and paid for by the program.

**"Is the ledger mixing Fortress gaps with how-to-write-a-DSL knowledge? How much of APL is left, and how hard?"** The 87 APL rows were counted: 46 in a new section on syntax extension, 41 in the language sections; by class 21 implementation gaps, 2 library bugs, 20 design limits, 38 positive facts. Three kinds of row live in one file, and only the first feeds the fix list. On APL: six chapters reached 106 of 214 examples; the other 108 are nested arrays (half the book, a second carrier), characters (tedious), Dyalog's environment (not the language), tacit (a rung), small features (less than a rung, two blocked by the shadowing check). Revealed: Pavol's goal was never the whole of APL; the ladder was a method, and the finish line is the program.

**"How did we get into the side quest? Was microGPT always the goal?"** Answered from the transcript's timestamps: curiosity, then the wish to learn APL, then the redirection to native code, then microGPT on day four. Revealed: the out-of-scope halves of the chapters were never a loss, because the ladder's stated purpose was to find where the extension mechanism gives out.

**"Isn't the universal `Any` boxing everything and cutting off static typing? I assumed the DSL would produce a strongly typed version by inference from the literals outward. Is that ludicrous?"** Not ludicrous, misplaced by one level. The expander is syntactic and infers nothing; the host infers after expansion, and does so for everything the direct rules produce. The one place it breaks is a dfn's parameter, which the APL text does not type; the context does (rows under `⍤1`, planes under `⍤2`), and named functions carry their types on the host declaration. The `Any` boxed functions, never numbers, once per row or plane, which is why the cost was 1.12× and not 10×. Also: rank and depth are different axes; rank we handle in the type, depth (nested arrays) is the wall. Revealed: the design of rung 4b, and Pavol's standard for the backend, which is C4's own typed code.

**"Do the rewrite. Refocus on the readable expression of microGPT. The Hsu-flat Fortress looked suboptimal to my eye."** Decisions taken; the design written the same hour. On "better Fortress through the APL route": the route cannot produce Fortress better than the library entries it expands to, so the vocabulary is where any diamond is, and the rewrite makes the vocabulary the whole backend.

**"Doesn't Fortress have Scala's positional or anonymous parameters? That relates to the ⍺⍵ issue."** Searched the whole in-repo specification draft and the parser: no placeholder, no partial application, no sections; `_` exists only as a discard in a binding position. Exactly the ⍺⍵ issue: with a placeholder there would be no name for hygiene to rename, and the frame stack was a hand-built substitute for the missing feature. APL's tacit spelling is the placeholder-free style that does translate, which is why the rewrite uses it. Revealed: Fortress's model is the mathematician's "x ↦ e", not the combinator tradition, and Sun Labs built a great deal and not this.

**"What is a nat-parameterised type? Where do we need it? Where does it come from?"** A size carried in the type, from the library's own `Vector[\T, nat s0\]` and `Matrix[\T, nat s0, nat s1\]`, so that any function accepting a matrix must quantify over sizes. Our arrays are built at run time from the corpus and the weights and do not unify with sized types statically (row 23); the interpreter matches them at dispatch. Revealed the asymmetry in C4's vocabulary: sizes on parameters, none on results.

**"Why are the views objects? Doesn't Fortress have views? How would Swift, Scala, Kotlin, Rust do this? And did you answer the impedance-mismatch question?"** An array in Fortress is a trait with `get` and `put`, so a view is an object implementing it over other storage: the same idiom as a Swift struct conforming to `Collection` or a Rust struct implementing `Index`, and older than both. The library has row slices; ours cost a fifth as much (measured); rank-3 planes it has not. On the mismatch: the term is right, and the walls are the primitives Fortress lacks: nested arrays, the implicit-argument function, and, at the grammar level, right-to-left glyph reading, which the mechanism handled. The frame stack's failure was not a mismatch between the languages but our workaround meeting Fortress's own primitive.

**"I assumed the hyperparameters would be inlined and the transformer's shape would already use sized types, giving static bounds elimination and faster iteration. You are undermining my core assumption. Explain."** The specification says `nat` parameters "are instantiated at runtime": they check shapes, they were never compile-time constants a compiler folds, and no optimization is built on them, in the specification or in the interpreter, which optimizes nothing. Almost everything in the program is fixed by the hyperparameters and could carry literal sizes; three things are not: the batch size, chosen per call; the corpus, read from a file; and the flat parameter vector Hsu's layout keeps as one buffer. A `nat B` for the batch size is legal in the specification and unprobed in the interpreter. Revealed, and the fact that changed the picture: the static type checker exists, in Scala, and the walk interpreter never runs it; only the compile path does. The compile-time error Pavol was asking for is exactly what the compiled path is for.

**"The vocabulary looks like code after type inference and specialization, Scala in Fortress, no trait flavour. Good reasons, or habit? Launch a Fable worker."** Launched. Its verdict, with 24 probes: 30 of 40 declarations forced in count because the shipped library has nothing to inherit them from (no `×` on arrays by design so that juxtaposition can be the inner product, no algebraic trait at rank 3, no scalar extension, no reshape, gather, outer product or ravel, and the specification's `Ring` and `Field` exist only as commented-out LaTeX), but 19 of the 30 narrower than the language would say (`RR64` where `T extends Number` serves, `nat`s that dispatch nothing) and 10 outright habits; a 28-declaration sketch passes the same smoke; two things that look like habits are not, because they cost 5× and 13–54×. Revealed: the Phase 2 reviewers' verdict again, the habits were the specialisations, and Pavol's untrained eye read the shape right.

**"Isn't matrix multiplication exactly where the nat parameters bind the dimensions that must agree? Why aren't we declaring our operations that way?"** Yes, and the library does: `Matrix[\T,n,m\]` times `Matrix[\T,m,p\]` with the shared `m`. Our own declarations quantified sizes with separate names that shared nothing, which is habit, and the fix is free. The caveat: with run-time arrays the check happens at dispatch, and a failure reads as "no matching overload". Decision: the vocabulary swap states every dimension equality.

**"Explain reshape, planes, ravel, gather, outer product. Which spec are you reading? Did they just sketch the nats and never use them? Where would the class loader generate the checks? How do we get microGPT fast on the JVM? What is the walking skeleton? And an inventory of the ledger, characterized against languages I know."** Definitions given. The in-repo draft with the implementers' notes is what is searched, not the frozen 1.0. The checker is the answer to the nats: it runs on the compile path only, and the code generator has no place for size checks because they are the checker's job ahead of it. The fast path exists and has numbers: the bytecode compiler measured 6.8–8.9× on the shapes microGPT is made of, blocked by a 592-line prelude, not by code generation; the skeleton is C4's program through `fortress compile`, closing prelude gaps in the order the program hits them, the interpreter kept for debugging. The inventory was drafted by a Fable worker over all 286 rows (`explorations/reviews/fortress-characterized.md`): area by area against Scala, Swift, Kotlin and Rust; nine concepts with no counterpart, ten the others lack; 88 defects, 74 design limits, 20 never built, 103 capabilities; five options with evidence for and against.

**"I don't understand what my options are on the two decisions."** Fair. The vocabulary swap needed no decision and was launched with the shared nats. The ledger split was mine to decide: not to split the numbered store (287 numbers cited from thirty reports), but to derive two views, the fix list re-derived and a lessons document for the next DSL writer. On the JVM plan: class-load-time instantiation exists in the compiler, so baked-in sizes are a plausible shape of their intent, but two unbuilt layers sit below it, unboxed `RR64` in the code generator and primitive array storage; the first step is the prelude either way.

## What was built while this was discussed

Run C4 as the target form; the microGPT rung on the universal base (19 of 25 lines APL, 40 of 40 at both pool sizes once the tuple each was sequenced); rung 4b, the focused base (no frame stack, the expansion C4's program bit for bit, 439 s and 254 s against C4's 528/444 s and 263 s); the ledger from 174 to 287 rows with a new section on syntax extension and every reproducer re-run; the vocabulary review; the characterization. Process records 12, 13, 14.

## Decisions on record

The APL ladder is closed; rungs 7 and 8 stay unbuilt; the goal is the readable expression of microGPT. The focused base replaces the universal one for that program. The vocabulary is swapped with the dimension equalities stated. The ledger is not split; two views are derived. The compiled path for microGPT is the option with a measured multiplier and unwritten first probes; Pavol has not chosen it yet.

## What the dialogue revealed about how to work

Pavol's questions were the right ones each time and the answers had to be made plain enough to act on; when they were not, that was the coordinator's failure, not his. A decision made in a worker's report and recorded in one line (the frame stack's thread assumption) is a decision not made. "A week of work" is not an estimate this project accepts; sizes are given in the units it has measured. And the frustration he named, at concepts undermined and edges he could not see, is what the characterization document is for.
