## 9. The fork

**What the probe finds.** Under walk the switch is not a rung that edits the library's numeral sites: with the four devices beyond rung Q's files, 25 tests still fail, for eight causes. Most follow from two properties of walk and one of the decided rule:
- walk instantiates a generic from run-time values and has no expected type (row 510), so every aggregate or instance built from numerals now takes `IntLiteral`, or `Number` beside a `ZZ32` (kind F), where the checker takes the type a binding or a parameter expects;
- a native's arguments are not converted at binding (kind G), nor is a `typecase` subject (kind P);
- the conversion rule runs a declaration that fits a numeral as is before one that needs a conversion, so the library's `Any` and `Number` catch-alls take numerals (kind K) and three team tests that assert the old model fail (kind E).

**The options, the library's own way first.**
1. The compiler library's model under walk as decided, with rung Q widened: the devices of section 2 (A, B, C, F, G) and one for each remaining kind, the library's catch-alls respelled (`AnyList`, `Map.member`, `Contains.MATCH`, the 16 shift and exponent sites or device A), and the three team tests (`typecaseTest`, `ReflectTest`, `disp2`) restated to the decided model. What it touches: `Evaluator.java`, `Coercions.java` or `OverloadedFunction.java`, `EvaluatorBase.java`, the glue natives, `BaseEnv.java` for row 486, `List.fss`, `Map.fsi`, and team test lines, which the record's stops forbid without Pavol's yes. What it costs: a rung several times rung K's, and aggregates of numerals still differ from the checker's typing wherever the checker uses an expected type (kind F has no device here).
2. Q1's default widened under walk: a type parameter that only numerals fix takes `ZZ32` (`ZZ64`, `ZZ` by magnitude) whatever its bound, so that walk's inference from values gives the type the checker gets from a typical expected type (`<|1, 2, 3|>` a `List[\ZZ32\]`, `idt(5)` a `ZZ32`). A numeral stays an `IntLiteral` for `typecase`, dispatch and conversion. What it touches: rung K's `narrowest`, one method. What it costs: walk departs from the checker where no expected type fixes the parameter (`idt(5)` is an `IntLiteral` on the checker), and kinds E, G, K, L, M, N, O and P remain. Measured on the 41 changed tests: pending (`captures/tests-r2.txt`).
3. Split the switch: land the one library's `IntLiteral` model and the walk devices that stand on their own (rows 395 and 511, `asif`, the natives, the comparisons on each type, the numeral's value-dependent conversion) in a first rung with walk's numeral unchanged, and make walk's numeral an `IntLiteral` in a second, once the team-test question is answered.

**What a yes commits to, and a no's cost.** Option 1 is Pavol's decision as recorded, carried through; its yes lets team tests change. A no to all three keeps rung Q as recorded, which meets the record's own stops (team tests failing) and cannot land.

**Recommendation** (one line; the coordinator's to frame): option 3, since each first-rung device fixes a walk gap on its own and the numeral's run-time switch then meets only the kinds that are the model's, E and K, which Pavol decides.
