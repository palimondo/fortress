## 6. Findings

- **A. The exclusion paragraph refuses what the checker accepts.** `advanced/overloading.tex:583-586` says: "Two parameter types are not taken to exclude each other only because the static arguments at which they could meet are ones that a bound rules out; declarations in that case are told apart by an `excludes` clause or by their names". `future.tex:279-280` says the same. So by the text, `m[\T extends Fig2\](x: Tag[\T\])` beside `m(x: Tag[\Other\])` is not a valid overloading: they do not exclude, neither is more specific, and no third declaration exists. The compiled checker accepts all three of my `SkExclBound` programs and runs them right. `SkExclBound2` shows that it does not treat the plain declaration as more specific: had it done so, the Return Type Rule would have refused `ZZ32` against `Tag[\String\]`. So the checker concludes that the two domains do not meet when the forced static argument is a named type that fails the bound. It does not reach that conclusion through the ancestors of the parameter types (`DifferMIE`, the `CAP` shape, `explorations/reviews/overload-static-params-ways/captures/DifferMIE.txt`), and rung C does not touch exclusion (its section: "Not the checker step of the judgement's option 2"). Answer 9 has the text state "the model the checker runs"; this sentence states a narrower one. Walk refuses the three programs, but only by its own load check that some pair of parameters excludes (rows 159 and 475), which rung W may lift. What the decisions do not settle is whether the language takes the bound step. The worker read answer 9's decline of option 2 as settling it; that decline was about adding a checker step for `CAP`, and the base checker already takes the step in the ground case. Required correction 2.
- **B. The Return Type Rule's repair is called allowed, and neither path runs it.** `advanced/overloading.tex:602-609`, the pair `ident[\T extends Shape\]` and `ident[\T extends Round\]`.
