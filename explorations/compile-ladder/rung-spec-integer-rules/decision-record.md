## 2. The rules, their decisions, and what runs them

Measured: the gated tests `ProjectFortress/tests/IntSemanticsRungI.fss` (walk) and `ProjectFortress/compiler_tests/IntSemanticsRungB.fss`, `ProjectFortress/library_tests/IntegralOpsRungN.fss`, `ProjectFortress/library_tests/IntConversionsRungW.fss` (compiled) assert most of the table; the rest is measured by this rung's probes, `probes/rules/RulesWalk.walk.txt` (19 cases, all as the text says: `ZZ`'s `narrow`, `NN32` at and beyond the width, `LSHIFT`/`RSHIFT` on `ZZ` as `shift`, their overflow, `ZZ` and `NN32` counts on a `ZZ32`) and `probes/rules/RulesCompiled.compiled.txt` (the operator overview's two examples, `(2^31-1) LCM 2` and `-2^31 GCD 0`, both `IntegerOverflow`; `0 GCD -4 = 4`, `1 LCM -5 = 5`).

The reasons behind the rules, from the decisions: the design principle of 2026-09-22, "we should use Java or JVM defaults where it makes sense, but we shouldn't be shy to correct the mistakes of JVM when seen through the lens of high performance and mathematical precision language", with hardware behaviour given its own spelling; on the shifts, that every peer language lets shifted-out bits fall because a shift is a bit operation, with the arithmetic `shift` kept exact on `ZZ`; for a count at or beyond the width, the rule the team's interpreter has followed since `b451f5506` (2007-06-26) and that `ProjectFortress/tests/BitTwiddle.fss:33`, `:36`, `:39`, `ProjectFortress/tests/UnsignedTest.fss:30`, `:32`, `:135`, `:137` and `ProjectFortress/tests/QuickCheckTest.fss:97-103` assert (`explorations/compile-ladder/rung-integral-ops/shift-history.md`, section 2); for `narrow`, Steele's boundary test of 2008-07-21, `ProjectFortress/tests/UnsignedTest.fss:210-211`; for `GCD` and `LCM`, the chapter's own "The result is always nonnegative" and the `NN` of its suppressed declarations (`Specification-1.0-frozen/basic-lib/basic-integers.tex:518-519`).

## 4. Decisions taken inside the rung

1. **The integer rules go in the integer chapter's opening, not in the `ZZ` listing and not in api comments.** The opening introduces the fixed-size types and ends on the explicit conversions; the rules concern all five types, where the listing and its method entries present `ZZ` alone. Rejected: (a) listing entries for `LSHIFT`, `RSHIFT` and `narrow` on `ZZ`, which would state the rules for `ZZ` only and leave the fixed widths, the types they matter for, undescribed; (b) comments on `Integral`'s shift pair and the `narrow` declarations in `Library/FortressLibrary.fsi`, which batch 3.5 and 4's review offered, which Part IV renders, and which this rung may not edit (a library line other than the scalar comments is a stop); the prose chapters are the standard (FACTS.md, "Citing Specification/library/apis/*.tex as an independent standard is circular"); (c) a new section on the fixed-size types, heavier than two paragraphs for the same content.
2. **The operator overview gets two sentences on `GCD`/`LCM` and one on the shifts.** It is the passage that describes the integer operators for every integer type, and its `GCD` subsection is in the list; without the shift sentence its list of bitwise operators omits two the library declares. Rejected: leaving the overview unchanged, which keeps the overview silent on the one question of the fixed widths the revised integer chapter cannot answer for `GCD` and `LCM` (the chapter's entry is on `ZZ`, which never overflows).
3. **`GCD`/`LCM`: "the absolute value of the other argument", and the property `(m GCD n)(m LCM n) = abs(m n)`.** Row 334's decision reads "equals the other argument" as its magnitude, both paths compute it (`0 GCD -4 = 4`, `1 LCM -5 = 5`), and with nonnegative results the property holds only up to sign, which rung N's gated test already checks against `abs(a b)` (`ProjectFortress/library_tests/IntegralOpsRungN.fss:176-185`). The misspelling "argment" is corrected in the two sentences revised, and nowhere else. Rejected: leaving the property, which then contradicts the sentence two paragraphs above it. The typeset line is the team's with `m \cdot n` made `\left|m \cdot n\right|`, because today's `Fortify/fortify.el` mangles a closing bar at the end of a line (`\left|m \cdot n\lfloor`); the `%%` source line is written in the source's own style.
4. **`narrow` is stated for `ZZ` as well as for `ZZ64` and `NN64`.** The library declares `narrow(self): ZZ32` on `ZZ` (`Library/FortressLibrary.fsi:608`), walk truncates it the same way (`BigNum.java:286-290`, measured in `probes/rules/RulesWalk.walk.txt`), and the decision's rule is that `narrow` truncates. Rejected: `ZZ64` and `NN64` only, the cases the decisions measured, which would leave one of the library's three `narrow`s undescribed. The compiled prelude declares no `narrow` on `ZZ`; the specification's appendix entry says so (row 442's note in `record.md`). `ZZ`'s `widen`, which walk also truncates to 64 bits (`BigNum.java:292-296`), is not stated: no decision covers it and its name promises no truncation; it is reported here and not chosen.
5. **The shift paragraph states the rule by arithmetic** (`floor(x/2^k)`, and `x 2^k` reduced modulo `2^n`), which gives the saturation at or beyond the width as a consequence, instead of listing the saturation cases alone; one sentence then states that consequence, because the team's tests pin it.
6. **The coercion example's clauses are `ZZ32 excludes { ZZ64, ZZ128 }` and `ZZ64 excludes { ZZ128 }`.** The definition of "no less specific" (`Specification/basic/conversions-coercions.tex:494-501`) needs `ZZ64` to exclude `ZZ128` and to reject it, and rejection needs `ZZ32`, the one type that coerces to `ZZ64`, to exclude `ZZ128`; the Meet Rule (`Specification/advanced/overloading.tex:224-282`) needs `ZZ32` to exclude `ZZ64` for the two coercions to `ZZ128`, and `ZZ64` to exclude `ZZ128` for the two declarations of `f`. This is the library's spelling: the narrower type's `excludes` clause names the wider ones, in braces (`Library/FortressLibrary.fsi:510-511`, `:552-553`). Rejected: (a) `ZZ64 excludes ZZ128` alone, which both paths refuse (`probes/examples/SpecZZOnly64.walk.txt`, `SpecZZOnly64.compiled.txt`: the two coercions to `S128` are an invalid overloading); (b) each trait listing both others, which the library does not do. Measured: the revised example runs on both paths as printed, `f(S32Of) = 1` (`probes/examples/SpecZZExcl.walk.txt`, `SpecZZExcl.compiled.txt`), and the Working Draft's is refused by both (`SpecZZOrig.walk.txt`, `SpecZZOrig.compiled.txt`), as row 394 recorded. The later Types chapter is cited beside, for what an `excludes` clause declares (`Documentation/Specification/Prose/Language/types.tick:344-351`; POSITIONS 2026-09-26, the lineage note); it has no coercion resolution to cite.
7. **The rational listing states the library's four traits, and this undoes part of rung T's decision 2.** Rung T took the field and order clauses out and put nothing in their place (`explorations/compile-ladder/rung-spec-numbers/decision-record.md`, section 5, decision 2). The batch record reads Pavol's answer 6 and the rule that the number chapters describe the library as requiring the library's traits (`CLIMB-BATCH-6.5.md` section 1, "The rational type's listing states the library's algebra"), and this rung follows it. The traits are copied from `Library/FortressLibrary.fsi:387-388` in the api's order; the typeset header is today's `fortify` output over the new `%%` source (`tmp/fy/drive.el`, not committed), which reproduces the base's rendering of the rest of the listing byte for byte. T's own sentences stay true: "`Q` is neither totally ordered nor a field" (`numbers.tex:67-69`), and its callout's "no longer gives it the field and order traits of the original design" (`:88-90`).
8. **The sentence on the laws says that those of the same traits on `RR64` "need not hold where a NaN takes part", not that they "hold away from NaN".** (The rung wrote "do not hold", for the rationals too; the gather made both "need not hold" on the skeptic's RC2, since the order and min/max laws do hold with the two infinities.) The brief's analogy is kept in the form that is true: floating-point laws hold only approximately even away from NaN, as the team's own chapter says ("algebraic properties can be expected to be obeyed only approximately", `Specification/advanced-lib/algebraic-constraints.tex:569-576`), and the appendix entry cites that passage. The sentence sits after rung T's callout on the listing, so that T's box stays beside the listing it documents.
9. **Four Appendix I entries, one per change**: shifts and `narrow` together (one passage of the opening states both, and both are the integer rules of 2026-09-22 and 24), `GCD` and `LCM`, the coercion example, the rational algebra; seven callouts, each pointing at its entry. Rejected: one entry per passage (seven entries repeating one rationale) and one entry for everything (four decisions of different dates and rows). The first three entries say that they do not follow from route A, as rung X's entry does (`Specification/appendices/changes.tex:1359`, `:1423`, `:1469`); the fourth, the rational algebra, rests on route A's own entry "The number types" and says nothing of route A (`:1514-1515`; corrected at the second merged-diff review of climb batch 6.5, where this sentence had said each of the four does). The section's opening sentence, "Every change below follows from one decision of the revival ... route A" (`Specification/appendices/changes.tex:48-51`), which the entries on `tabulate` and `comprises` already contradict, is left as it is (`record.md`, for the gather).
10. **The appendix entries do not name walk's `NN32`/`NN64` `LCM` defect.** Rung E of climb batch 6.5b repairs it and opens its rows, and E edits no specification file; a sentence here would go stale when E lands. The gather of 6.5b checks this text against E's natives (`CLIMB-BATCH-6.5.md` section 4).
11. **Row 443's test asserts a numeral `RR64` holds exactly** (3000000000 is below `2^53`), so it does not touch the residue row 443 records for numerals above `2^53`.
12. **The two new tests have no `.test` file**: `ProjectFortress/tests/` has none, and its harness takes an expected failure from the `XXX` of the file name (FACTS.md, "An XXX*.fss in the interpreter corpus IS a gated expected-failure test").
