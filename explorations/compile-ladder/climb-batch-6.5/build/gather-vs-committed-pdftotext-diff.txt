> The operators LSHIFT and RSHIFT shift the bits of an integer of any of the five types by a count of any integer
> type, and return a value of the type of the integer shifted. On Z , x LSHIFT k is shift(x, k) and x RSHIFT k is
> shift(x, −k) . On a fixed-size type of width n , for a nonnegative count k , x RSHIFT k is ⌊x/2k ⌋ , and x LSHIFT k

> Change Two paragraphs after the coercions of Section 33.2 state the method narrow and the operators LSHIFT and

> on a fixed-size type the two operators lose the bits shifted out, give 0 , or −1 for a negative value shifted right,

> bitwise operators.
> Rationale This change does not follow from route A; the revival decided it on 22 September 2026, with two further decisions on 24 September: that the signed narrow truncates too, and that Z32 ’s shifts take a count
> of any integer type. The Fortress standard libraries declare LSHIFT and RSHIFT on every integer type
> with a count of any integer type (Library/FortressLibrary.fsi, lines 459–460), and narrow on
> Z64 , N64 and Z (lines 506, 594 and 608), and this working draft said nothing of them. In the languages
> the revival compared a shift is an operation on bits whose bits shifted out are lost, and the revival kept it
> one on the fixed-size types, with an exact arithmetic shift on Z under the name shift , which this working draft already defines. For a count at or beyond the width the rule is the one the team’s interpreter has
> followed since 2007 and three of its gated tests assert (ProjectFortress/tests/BitTwiddle.fss,
> lines 33, 36 and 39; ProjectFortress/tests/UnsignedTest.fss, lines 30, 32, 135 and 137;
> ProjectFortress/tests/QuickCheckTest.fss, lines 97–103). Nothing pinned a negative count;
> it shifts the other way, and the count is read by its value, so that a large unsigned count is not read as a
> negative one. narrow keeps the low 32 bits as the team’s own test asserts at the values 264 − 1 and 263
> (ProjectFortress/tests/UnsignedTest.fss, lines 210–211), on the signed types as on the unsigned. The full reasoning is in explorations/compile-ladder/rung-spec-integer-rules/
> decision-record.md in the revival’s repository.
