> If a trait declaration of T includes a comprises clause then every type listed in its comprises clause extends T ,
> and every value of type T is a value of one of the listed types. A listed type may be an instantiation of a parameterized
> trait, provided that every static parameter that occurs in its static arguments is a static parameter of T . A trait or
> object that explicitly extends T must be a subtype of a listed type, unless it is a trait with a comprises clause of
> its own each of whose listed types meets this requirement, or a trait with static parameters such that at least one trait
> or object extends it and every trait or object that extends it is a subtype of a listed type. If T has static parameters,
> these statements hold of each instantiation of T , with the static parameters of T in the listed types replaced by the
> corresponding static arguments.

> The traits that extend a closed trait
> Affected sections Section 10.1.
> Change The first sentence of a draft note in Section 10.1, which said that the traits listed in a comprises clause are
> exactly the traits that immediately extend the declared trait and that they must explicitly extend it, is replaced
> by normative text: every listed type extends the declared trait T , and every value of type T is a value of one
> of the listed types; a listed type may be an instantiation of a parameterized trait whose static arguments contain
> no static parameter other than those of T ; and a trait or object that explicitly extends T is a subtype of a listed
> type, or a trait with a comprises clause of its own each of whose listed types meets the same requirement,
> or a trait with static parameters that at least one trait or object extends and every trait or object extending it is
> a subtype of a listed type. The note’s second sentence, on a clause that includes “. . . ”, is kept in its note, and
> the example of Molecule is unchanged. The new text answers Victor Luchangco’s draft note on trait identifiers
> earlier in the section: an instantiation of a parameterized trait may be listed.
> Rationale This change does not follow from route A; the revival decided it on 28 September 2026. The team’s
> later Types chapter (Luchangco, 2012) reads a comprises clause as coverage: the clause “specifies a set of
> types and generic types determined by the generic type of the declaration”, and an instantiation of the declared generic type “is covered by the union of the types in this set and the corresponding instantiations of
> the generic type in this set”, one generic type G determining another G′ “if every parameter of G′ is a parameter of G” (Documentation/Specification/Prose/Language/types.tick, lines 277–283
> and 384–390, in the revival’s repository). Welterweight Fortress, the calculus of the type group’s paper of
> 2012 (Chase, Hilburn, Luchangco, Naden, Ryu, Steele, and Tristan, “Dynamic Dispatch and Type Inference
> Semipredicates”), reads it at the level of values: “no value can belong to the trait unless it also belongs
> to one of the comprised types”; its rule for a trait declaration asks nothing of the traits that extend a trait
> with such a clause (Papers/Welterweight/grammar.tick, lines 21–22; Papers/Welterweight/
> fig-wellformeddecls.tick, lines 38–62). The draft note restated the rule of the compiled type checker
> of 2009, under which the Fortress standard libraries could not close their marker of the integer types as the
> team meant to: AnyIntegral lists the five integer types, and the parameterized trait IntegralJIK , which each
> of them extends at itself, stands between them unlisted, as the team’s comment on the declaration records (“not
> yet: comprises IntegralJIK where JIK ”, Library/FortressLibrary.fsi, line 434). Integral has a
> static parameter that AnyIntegral does not have, so it cannot be listed, and every value of it is a value of one
> of the five. The full reasoning is in explorations/compile-ladder/rung-spec-comprises/
> decision-record.md in the revival’s repository.
> Effect The compiled type checker accepts the declaration of AnyIntegral in the Fortress standard libraries as
> written (Library/FortressLibrary.fsi, lines 433–436). No example of this specification changes:
> ExclusiveMolecule, which has no static parameters, is still not allowed. The compiled type checker enforces
> the requirement on a trait with static parameters over the declarations it sees: a trait or object declared in another component, below such a trait and a subtype of no listed type, is not refused (row 487 of the revival’s gap
> ledger). The interpreter does not check comprises clauses. The passages that read a comprises clause at the
> level of types, the intersection of two traits with such clauses (Section 6.10, Section 24.4) and the definitions an
> abstract function declaration requires (Section 9.3), are not revised; whether they are to be restated at the level
> of values is not settled.
