# Brief: the report of the record post-mortem, 2026-10-20 to 22

You are the writer of a report for the curator, the person in charge of this restoration. From 2026-10-20 to 22 batches were held while the project's record was examined and rebuilt. An archaeology scout mapped that work, with sources: `explorations/coordinator/postmortem-2026-10-20-record/MAP.md`. Read it whole. Then write `explorations/coordinator/postmortem-2026-10-20-record/REPORT.md`.

## What the report does

It tells the curator what happened, why, what was done, what it cost, how the record is kept now, and what is still open, so that he can lift the hold on batches knowing what changed. He lived through it but forgets detail after a gap: restate, do not refer back.

Sections, in this order:

1. **What started it.** The incident, in a few lines: the decision in the record that he did not recognize, the coordinator's three failed explanations, the three fresh workers' contradictory accounts. Say what the decision said.
2. **Why it happened.** The root causes that the analyses on file name, each in a line or two, with what each one did to the record. Where the analyses disagree, say so.
3. **What was done.** The steps, in order, each with its outcome: the audit of where each decision came from, the rewrite of the decisions and the facts so that each reads on its own, the removal of status and plan words, the follow-up, the worker skill's revival part rebuilt, the gap ledger's check and repair, the skill corrections. Give the sizes before and after.
4. **The reusable tools.** Each one: what it does, in one or two lines, and when to run it again.
5. **How the record is kept now.** The rules as the skills now state them, in plain words, few: what each file holds, which way pointers go, who writes what and when. Check each against `.claude/skills/coordinator/references/record.md` and `.claude/skills/fortress-repo/references/records.md` as they stand on `main`, not against the map alone.
6. **What it cost.** Tokens written, by the main runs, and the total, with which figures are measured and which are estimates. Give no cost in money.
7. **What is still open.** Each open item, with where it waits.
8. **What to watch for.** The few failures that the work showed and that could come back, each with the rule or tool that now guards against it, or the note that nothing does yet.

## How to write it

- About 10K to 12K characters. Plain, short sentences, halfway to ASD-STE100: active voice, one word for one thing, contractions allowed. No metaphor, no flourish, no closing summary.
- Lists, not tables: the curator reads on a phone, where a table is one line that scrolls sideways.
- Numbers as K and M (5K, 1.2M), each with what it counts.
- Times in UTC with his time beside them, which is UTC+2 in October 2026 (22:39 UTC is 00:39 his time).
- Cite a decision by what it says, never by its number alone. Define every term that is the project's own where you first use it. No shorthand of past conversations.
- Put each section's sources at its bottom, numbered, never inside the sentences: he listens to the text read aloud, and citations must not be read.
- Where the map says a claim is a guess, or that two sources disagree, keep that in the report. Check any claim that the report rests on and that you doubt, in its cited source.

## Rules

- Edit no file other than `REPORT.md`. Commit nothing.
- End with a short final message: the path, its size, and anything you could not settle.
