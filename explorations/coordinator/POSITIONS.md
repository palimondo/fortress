# Pavol's positions, decisions and known ground

## The goal

- **The measuring stick.** Finish what the designers intended, judged by the specification in `Specification/`, the Working Draft of 2010-12 as the revival revises it, with the 2012 Types chapter cited beside it; not redesign the language. The measure is one program, microGPT, compiled to bytecode and running fast. A pure-Java microGPT is written later as the floor to measure against. The specification is a living document that reflects the changes we make. The tree is tagged `sealed-tree` at the map's last commit; commits after it edit the original sources, test-driven.
- **How he weighs his calls where the designers left no word.** "I don't care about type theoretical purity and I'm accepting all unfinished corners of the language because I want to get to a compiled version of micro GPT. And I think this is the right pragmatic approach so that we do not get bogged down in weeds. Theory. We want to get this one program running fast. That's our guiding star. But along the way, I want to stay true to the spirit of Fortress." He has not read the specification, the papers on its type system or the library, and relies on the record: "I assume those people knew much more than I do right now." The check he asked for is a thorough top-tier review of every decision of his against the designers' intent, for errors of commission and of omission (`reviews/decisions-review/evidence.md`, `judgement.md`, `popl-recheck.md`); its items are decisions below.
- **The readable expression.** The toy problem is microGPT and the search is for its most readable expression: "we are searching for the optimal expression of the microGPT algorithm for readability." Fortress's mathematical notation and Iverson's APL notation (with Hsu's data design for data-flow algorithms) are the two sources. The Hsu-style flat program gave "a few lumps of coal", not a diamond; the vocabulary is where any polishing happens. C4 is the APL target form; C2's tuple hyperparameter form is what a strand assignment expands to; the semicolon form is set aside.
- **The APL side quest is closed.** It was a curiosity, then a ladder to learn APL, then native code (not an interpreter in an interpreter) with microGPT as its target; its remaining ground (tacit, characters, nested arrays) is not pursued.
- **The 2D notation is a gimmick.** Fortify's mathematical rendering is "a neat gimmick, not a good idea": it encodes one perspective on a computation into the syntax, while the same mathematics can be written many ways; APL's glyph-per-transformation is the purer realisation. Presentation only, not on the path, not an argument for Fortress; no work on it unless he asks.
- **The type checker on interpreter programs.** He wants the checker, which runs only on the compile path, usable on interpreter programs, and considers it low-hanging fruit. It comes after the switch-over, when walk also takes the static coercions the checker inserts (the entry on walk's choice of coercions).

## History he knows (do not re-explain)

- **The project's end.** DARPA's HPCS did not take Sun into phase three (2006) and the project lived on at Sun Labs until 2012. His reading: they did no work on performance, "mucked around with syntax", and the promise that a sufficiently advanced compiler would later make mathematical types fast was never true; the funding was pulled for good reason.
- **Boxed numerics.** The compiled path is only about ten times the interpreter because numerics are boxed (`FRR64`, no `DADD`); "without unboxed doubles and arrays of doubles, what are we even doing here". The first measurement's scalar loop was an edge case microGPT never hits (literals in the loop, a `:=` accumulator); the fairer test he asked for agreed with him (FACTS § Execution model). The storage is his decision below; unboxed arithmetic is phase 6 of the plan.
- **Hsu's critique** of language paradigms as accidental complexity; he is "coming around to his point of view".
- **The third-party Java 9 port** gets no credit and no mention: "I don't wanna see them mentioned anywhere." CLAUDE.md no longer describes the tree as the port's migration grafted on the history; the other files that repeat that sentence wait for the lineage cleanup (`PLAN.md`). His plan to rename the graft-free `clean-ladder` branch to `main` was not carried out.

## Decisions on record

### The standard and the sources

- **The specification stays the standard, the Types chapter beside it.** Where the Types chapter of the team's unfinished restart (`Documentation/Specification/Prose/Language/types.tick`) covers a topic, it is cited beside `Specification/` as the designers' later word: the exclusion rule as "instantiation exclusion" in its two-part form, the `covariant` modifier, `Bottom` inexpressible. "On the rule S revises, it is the designers' latest intent, and the goal is to finish what they intended."
- **The type group's late positions outweigh the early text.** The specification "was written in a vacuum early on", "as a wish"; "then the type group went in and tried to make it run. So they knew much more about what's feasible and what's not. The goal was performance. The specialization is a must"; "when that wish met reality, then the spec must change"; "whatever the spec was dreaming about in 2008 is irrelevant because it met hard reality". So the late, implementation-informed positions of the type group (the exclusion rule, specialization kept, the compiled tower flattened by Steele in 2011, Naden's 2012 write-up, the POPL 2019 paper) outweigh the specification's earlier text where they conflict, and the implementers' later word outweighs unfinished text. "We are not Naden. We know nothing here."
- **The library's own practice is the standard.** "all the extensions that [microGPT] invented need to go directly to the standard library in the spirit of the standard library." Exclusion "is a practice that is all over the standard library"; whoever extends the library "should study the existing parts of the Fortress library meticulously, and use those patterns for extending the standard library in the native way". No fix is designed from a reading of the specification alone or from a user-side workaround. A fork put to him names the library's own way first, and a fork resting on a rule that refuses something says what the library does instead (protocol principle 2). The lesson behind the rule: he had to find the diagonal's override shape himself ("why the fuck did I have to invent this solution?"), because the step skipped was what the library already does in the same family.
- **Fleshing out the standard library** is a very important task: first the `RR64`/`AdditiveGroup` mismatch (done by the flattening), then the algebraic layer (Ring, Field and what ML's matrices and tensors need) so the library's trait flavour reaches them; into the standard library itself, not a tree of our own.
- **Interpreter performance is irrelevant.** "interpreter of Fortress, which is hundreds times slower than the Python interpreter, is irrelevant for any performance discussions"; "we cannot judge anything performance related in Fortress while we do not have a compiler". No edit to C4 for speed, and no performance decision of any kind until microGPT runs compiled. The two microGPT programs never run in the gate ("those are too long running to be tests"); the ladder-regression stage compiles them and records the phase; when they one day run, the run goes to a separate non-gating stage.
- **Development-loop performance is a first-class concern.** When an iteration waits too long on something, find the cause and fix it.
- **The territory map** (`coordinator/map/`) is the map down to where the design intent is written, papers as well as the specification; he wanted it before unsealing. The maps exist so that nothing on record is rediscovered; the boot order is followed as written, `INDEX.md` included.
- **The plan** (`coordinator/PLAN.md`) is six phases, approved with its workers: 1, the wrapping operators, the specification rung without the number chapters and the run-time size rung; 2, the flattening, the number chapters and the overflow natives; 3, the checker at a true zero; 4, the switch-over, natives then names; 5, microGPT compiles: its static types, the array design and the code-generation holes; 6, microGPT fast. Phases 1 and 2 are landed. His answers come in the plan's order; the re-approval rows and the other off-path items are parked until relevant; row 41 joins the switch-over's design.

### Numbers

- **The integer principle.** "we should use Java or JVM defaults where it makes sense, but we shouldn't be shy to correct the mistakes of JVM when seen through the lens of high performance and mathematical precision language", as the designers did with rounding and the sign of `GCD`; "they were running on JVM is basically an accident of history, a bootstrap"; "high performance languages should give you tools to stay close to the metal … Swift and Rust type of reasoning": where hardware behaviour is expressible in bytecode it gets its own explicit spelling, in the family the specification already has for wrapping and saturating; "if the JVM itself is precluding such implementation then I guess at this point in time we should just give up fighting with the JVM". Julia and X10 are the counterparts to compare, beside Swift and Rust.
- **`round` on an exact half goes to the even integer** on both paths, the specification's rule for rationals, IEEE's default and the team's own rational body. Built.
- **`floor` and `ceiling` on the float types keep the float** (row 330): "So floor returns float." The brackets ⌊x⌋ and ⌈x⌉, `round` and `truncate` return the unbounded ℤ, and the library's spellings of `truncate` and `round` are tidied to the same. A library rung after the switch-over, gated, with the count of interpreter tests whose output changes measured first.
- **`LSHIFT`/`RSHIFT` are bit operators; `shift` is exact on ℤ** (row 335). On the fixed widths `LSHIFT`/`RSHIFT` are bit operators with the smart-shift rule, since every peer language lets shifted-out bits fall silently ("if every other language does something else, that's the clearest red flag to stop and pause and reconsider"), and the specification's arithmetic `shift` is exact on ℤ. His reservation for the code generator: on the fixed widths the compiled code shifts the machine word, never through ℤ. Built on both paths; `shift` reaches the compiled path at the switch-over. A `ZZ32` is shifted by a count of any integral width (rows 380, 381): `ZZ32`'s shift pair is declared with any integral count, as `Integral[\I\]` promises and as `ZZ`, `NN64` and `ZZ64`'s implementation do, `ZZ64`'s api lines matching its implementation; built under walk, the compiled side at the switch-over.
- **`GCD`/`LCM`** (row 334): nonnegative results as the specification says, and an overflow error when the multiple does not fit, on both paths. Built.
- **`|0|`, `-0` and `0 DIV -1`** (row 333): the overflow guards test for the most negative value itself instead of `a == -a`, in the compiled natives and their siblings with the same guard. Built.
- **`narrow` out of range truncates** (row 346): the low 32 bits kept, as Steele's boundary test (`tests/UnsignedTest.fss:210-211`) asserts; the signed `narrow` too. Built.
- **Fixed-width overflow raises `IntegerOverflow` under `walk`** (row 379), as the specification says (`Specification/basic/operators/opr-overview.tex:154-155`, `:195-196`) and as the compiled path does: an overflow check in the natives of the interpreter's `Int` and `Long` glue classes raising the catchable `IntegerOverflow`, the unsigned `NN32` and `NN64` under the same rule. The code that means to wrap uses the specification's own wrapping operators ∔ `DOTPLUS`, ∸ `DOTMINUS` (unary and binary) and ⨰ `DOTTIMES` (`opr-overview.tex:172-176`, `:205-209`), declared on `Integral[\I\]` and on `ZZ32`, `ZZ64`, `NN32`, `NN64` and `ZZ`, bound to wrapping natives of their own beside the checked ones, every expression fully parenthesised since their precedence is stated only among themselves; the strided distance that must not overflow is reordered and keeps the checked operators. The saturating family waits for a step of its own.

### How we work

- **Test first, the test kept.** Every sealed-tree edit starts from a failing test added to the corpus: the worker writes the test first, verifies that it actually fails, makes the fix, proves it passes, and the test stays in the corpus from then on; the full suite stays green. The unacceptable process is the one-off validation script, proving once by hand that the interpreter and the compiler agree and leaving no permanent check. The batches fix the issues the explorations captured as ledger rows, each with how to reproduce it, "Those are like our bug reports": the worker first commits the reproduction into the test suite, "this is how I have reproduced what the previous probe has discovered", then makes the fix, and the test passing demonstrates that the issue is fixed; "There isn't much more to it." The test captures the essence of the problem, not the shape the probe met it in, "do not blindly transpose the issue from the probe like think about it what's the core problem and capture that", written as a clean minimal version. Whether the test was seen failing first is read from the worker's transcript, not shown by a committed capture. The skeptic checks it there and runs no build to repeat it: "It should just check that the procedure of test first was followed. Not that it should be repeating the whole process, that is a fucking waste." Whether the suite runs per edit or per landed batch is an engineering choice, and per batch is in line with the rule. Every measured and repaired defect gets a gated assertion, a deferred spec-settled one an `XXX` test.
- **The suite's verdict is the check.** A test passes or fails, as the team designed the suite, and a value that matters is asserted in the test with the mechanisms the suite already has (an `assert` in an interpreter test, `run_out_equals` in a compiled one): "what is the purpose of a test suit that you just cannot run it and get a green? If everything that's encoded in it is, is okay and it turns red if it's not, why would you have to compare some values that it outputs? Those values must be part of the test." What a test prints beyond its checks does not matter: "When the designers of the test suite decided that it passes without any additional check, it is not needed to control its outputs." So no batch compares the corpus's printed outputs, no baseline is run to compare against, and no expected-output files are added to the interpreter suite, which has none; none of it is offered to him again.
- **Nothing is built or run twice on the same code.** "We don't want to re be rebuilding stuff that doesn't need to be rebuilt." The design, agreed with him and built into the batch script: the old code, the batch's base, is built once per batch, by the coordinator, before the launch, in a worktree nobody compiles or runs in (the script's `args.baseBuild`); "We just mechanically take the latest build and share it between each rung from the same baseline": every rung worktree is seeded from that one build in seconds (`coordinator/tools/seed-worktree.sh`), never built clean (his "Duh!" to "seeding a worktree by copying a built tree rather than rebuilding it"); the worker builds only what its own edit changed; the skeptic builds nothing, "very smart skeptics that just like reuse everything that was built during the rank phase as much as possible and just do their independent checks": it reads test-first from the worker's transcript, runs its own small programs on the new code with the rung's build and on the old code against the batch's one base build, through the rung's private seeded copy of it so that no two agents compile into one cache; the second skeptic checks only the repair against its own refusal; no stage re-runs a build, a suite or a measurement on a code state another stage has already run, and no agent reads again a log of output that has not changed: "I want to eliminate all waste of this type. Where we are re-executing stuff for no reason." How a worktree is seeded and what a worker clears after an edit or a failed build is engineering, measured before it is built into the batch script (`coordinator/build-cache-exploration.md`; the blinded engineers' variants in `coordinator/process-engineering/cache-sharing.md`).
- **The coordinator watches what it launches.** A worker the coordinator launches outside a batch, a probe among them, gets an estimate of its cost in its brief and the instructions that keep it efficient (a wait polled in steps under the prompt cache's life, among them); the coordinator watches its spend, and when it passes its estimate the coordinator reacts, by an instruction to the worker or a stop. No new ask is added before a probe: "I don't want you to become paranoid about running probes, but it is the coordinator's job to monitor the probe that it launched, and if you have an estimate before it runs, when it goes over it, then yes, it is your job to react to it." The cause of probe P1's 7.0M (`coordinator/process-engineering/p1-cost.md`) was a waste the worker knew how to avoid: "an instruction to that worker would have corrected it."
- **A deeper pass, not a halt.** When something goes wrong or turns out undecided, the answer is a deeper pass, not a halt or a rollback. On a red gate over a merged batch, no bisection of commits: "we should identify the source of the conflict holistically and rework that part in the merged batch", because the goal is one tree with everything running rather than the largest subset that happens to be green. On a divergence the specification does not settle, no stop: "it is not a reason to stop the climb, it is the reason to think harder, review the architecture and holistically decide on the best path forward and execute that one." What reaches him are the forks already reserved in `PLAN.md`, not new ones invented at the point of difficulty.
- **Forks are probed before a batch is briefed.** "if there are known decision forks, which we can evaluate using probes, we should be doing these during this phase, during this planning phase": a fork a probe can settle is probed first, so that the decision is his and the worker gets a clean task, never a decision left to a worker and reviewed after the fact. Rows are taken one per message with refreshers (other languages, what makes sense in a high-performance language), not in groups.
- **A brief describes the problem, not the solution.** "How do we prevent the myopic view? … I want to have full creativity of solutions": a worker that has not read our notes lists every way the language and the library offer before a design choice reaches him; a rule that blocks an option is answered with how the library gets around it (protocol principle 2). A worker that records timings records the CPU too, so that comparisons can be guessed at.
- **The mission briefing.** "this whole exercise is about in-context learning. You were not pre-trained on Fortress. And the language was not finished." - **Asks and decisions.** Decisions are presented one at a time as: what we do, what it changes, with a default he can accept without reading the argument; the argument lives in the committed review, not in chat. An ask for a yes states, in the message itself, what the worker would do, what it produces, what it touches in the tree, what it costs, what the yes commits him to afterwards and what a no costs ("What are the consequences of the yeses? What does that work even want to do? ... this is just pressuring me to give approval without understanding"); one ask per message; no label stands in for an explanation, and a new name is defined in the sentence that uses it; the recommendation comes after the explanation, in one line, never instead of it.
- **Ownership.** "I need to maintain some ownership of this work, even if we are doing agentic coding … I did not know what you did. I need to know what you did. And I need to be aware of those choices and decisions because I am the one carrying the responsibility for the consequences as a director of this revival": every re-approval row is gone through in detail, grouped by theme, and the count of reversals is not the measure. A decision made inside a worker's report and recorded in one line is a decision not made; flag it to him at the time.
- **The coordinator's standing goal** is to model what Pavol already knows and where his context has gaps, and to fill those gaps with the minimal set of relevant facts at the level he needs to decide; machine tokens are spent on thinking so that human context is protected. This came out of a compaction that left him "out of control, unaware of crucial details" while the coordinator was "taking unauthorized initiative which compounded my sense of helplessness to steer the Fortress revival efforts". The coordinator's two hats: "executive assistant here in this Fortress Revival project, helping me, the director, find a way forward, and the analyst that finds out what the fuck happened, why, and how do we move forward".
- **While a batch runs**, no progress reports on it until it lands. He is on Central European time; check-in times are given to him in UTC and in his local time. The harness's stop-hook reminders about uncommitted or unpushed work are processed silently, declined without a word while work is in flight, and never mentioned to him. He reads on an iOS client that sometimes shows stale state; "is anything in flight" is answered from the tree, not from the UI.
- **Format.** "table formatted in this way is hard to read on mobile … we just need a list": no tables in chat, lists. "write the numbers just as K or M. I'm fine as a programmer understanding what a kilo is": never spelled-out thousands and millions, and a numbers message carries few numbers, each with its meaning. Time is spoken from the clock or the record (when a batch will probably land, when a check-in fires), never from feel, "yesterday", "this morning", "last night", when the events were minutes or hours apart (protocol § 3).
