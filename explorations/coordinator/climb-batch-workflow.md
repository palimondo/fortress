# The batch workflow script, stage by stage

The script runs *a* batch, not batch 1. Everything a batch changes lies between the `MANIFEST` and `END MANIFEST` markers: the batch number, its decision record, its intro and overlap sentences, the first free ledger row, and one entry per rung — `id`, `slug`, `path`, `branch`, `tail`, `blurb`, `expectedMinutes`, `writesState`, `expectedMoves`, the three optional fields of the checker-count stage, `testIsStage`, `expectedCheckerCount` and `expectedCheckerCrash`, the optional `landsOnlyWith` (below), and the optional `briefing` and `checks`, the rung's briefing and the sub-list of it that the checking roles read ("Shared prefix"). Batch 1's manifest is left in place as the example.

## Manifest and scatter (item 8)

`expectedMinutes` sorts the scatter, longest-expected worker first: 0–12 minutes when the guess is right, free when it is not. k stays 4. The two-agent cap and the FIFO handing of a freed slot to the next queued agent are a comment on the manifest block with their `FACTS.md` sources; rung chains (decision 2(a)) are not implemented and the comment says why — replayed on batch 1's walls they save 0.3 minutes. Ledger numbering follows manifest order, not scatter order, and the script maps the results back.

`landsOnlyWith`, added 2026-09-26 for batch 6 (`CLIMB-BATCH-6.md` section 8, item 4), is a list of rung ids a rung lands only with: batch 6's T, the number chapters, names F, the flat library they describe. After the scatter, and before anything is handed on, `applyLandsOnlyWith` withholds every approved rung any of whose named rungs is not approved (stopped, dropped, its worker dead, withheld itself, or absent from the run), and repeats until nothing moves. A withheld rung keeps its approval in `approvedAs`, takes the state `withheld` and a `withheldReason` naming the rung it waited for and that rung's state, and reaches the gather on the not-landed list, so its source is never applied and its findings are folded as for any rung that did not land; its `expectedMoves` and checker declarations no longer count at the gate. Until then such a rule lived in the batch intro alone, which only the gather's reading enforced (batch 5's rule for rung Z's `cP` piece; `climb-batch-6-review.md`, change 10). A rung that is not in the run cannot be approved, so an entry that names one is withheld: batch 6's O names F only when it runs beside F (`FOLLOWUP ? [] : ['F']`).


## Preparing a batch record

An Opus planning worker drafts `CLIMB-BATCH-<n>.md` and commits it. On Pavol's yes each time, one Fable worker then reviews it in place: it edits the record directly, writes one reason per change in a short review note (`climb-batch-6-review.md` is the example), and commits, so that the diff between the two versions shows exactly what the review changed. The coordinator reads that diff, fixes what is left, and brings the questions in section 1 to Pavol one per message; the batch launches on his go (batches 5 and 6; his words in the two entries of 2026-09-26 on the review of those manifests, in `POSITIONS-history.md`).

The planner builds each rung's briefing, writes it into the rung's section of the record, and the manifest carries it as the rung's `briefing` ("Shared prefix"). Its purpose is in-context learning (Pavol, 2026-09-27): the agents were not trained on Fortress, the language was never finished, and what they write from other languages' habits contradicts the specification and the library unless they have absorbed what the project has gathered. So a briefing carries, as keys of `explorations/coordinator/tools/facts-extract.sh`, decisions first: the `POSITIONS.md` entries, ledger rows and earlier judges' rulings the rung rests on; the specification's sections its subject touches, whole sections by their `\section` or `\subsection` heading and never grep hits; the probe and review notes already written on the subject, found through `INDEX.md`, by section; the library code that is the precedent for the same kind of problem, by the declarations it starts at; and the `FACTS.md` entries and map rows and sections of its area. Relevance decides what goes in, not size. The planner marks the sub-list the skeptic, the repair round and the judges read as `checks`: the decisions and ledger rows their checks need, and the specification's sections and the precedent code those checks compare against, for O the overflow subsections of `opr-overview.tex` and the glue natives it edits. Each list goes through `facts-extract.sh --check` on the tree the batch is cut from, every key matching exactly one place, and the tool's size line gives the reading it costs. Batch 6's four manifest entries are the worked examples, O's being the one batch 6b runs.

## Shared prefix

New: a `run_bg`/`wait_for` pair at five-second granularity (item 7); a committed capture is `.txt`, never `.out` or `.log` (`.gitignore:42,46`, item h); `ant` is never piped through `tail` (item i). Rule 2 asks for the site count when a precedent repaired a defect (item 6); rule 3 asks for the passage rather than the grep hit, ten lines either side (item 2). Rule 4's closing sentence, and the judge's `stop` question with it, named batch 3.5's two stops, a change to a declared type the prelude already has and a renamed or removed declaration a gated test uses, as if they applied to every batch; since 2026-09-26 both point to the stops the batch record's intro reserves for Pavol, at the head of the prefix (`open-items-2026-09-26.md`, item 27; batch 6's rung F changes declared types by Pavol's decisions). The register paragraph limits a test file to one comment line pointing at its `REPORT.md` (item e, `protocol.md`, principle 1).

"Your briefing", added 2026-09-27 before the territory map, says what a briefing holds and who reads which part of it, and names no rung, so the prefix stays byte-identical across the batch's agents. Each role on a rung runs `explorations/coordinator/tools/facts-extract.sh` once, as its first step, with keys from the rung's manifest entry: the rung worker, and a worker resumed after a stop, read the whole `briefing`; the skeptic, the repair round after a refusal and the judge read its `checks` sub-list, and the judges and the repair on the merged tree read every rung's `checks`. The tool prints each key's entry whole and in the order given: a `POSITIONS.md` entry by `positions:DATE WORDS` (the entry of that date whose head, its text up to the first colon and space, holds the words; from `POSITIONS-history.md`, saying so, when `POSITIONS.md` has none), a ledger row by `ledger:ROW`, a judge's ruling, a note's section or a specification section by `doc:PATH#HEADING` (in a `.tex` or `.tick` file the headings are its sectioning commands, macros dropped; a heading equal to the key wins over the ones that hold it, and `A > B` names B's section inside A's), a map section by `map:FILE#HEADING`, one table row, list item or bold-lead paragraph of a section by `@WORDS`, code by `code:PATH#FROM..TO` (from the declaration on the one line holding FROM through the end of the one at the next line holding TO, braces counted in Java and Scala, indentation and `end` in Fortress, with line numbers), a `FACTS.md` entry by its title and `INDEX.md` lines by `index:`. It prints in parts of at most 28 KB, under the 30,000 characters one shell command shows an agent, with `--part N` to continue, and opens with the size of the whole output in bytes and estimated tokens (0.41 a byte and 110 a part, the rate `explorations/reviews/worker-context-cost.md` fitted); `--check` prints where each key matches and its size, and `--root DIR` reads another checkout's record. The script refuses a key holding a double quote, a backtick, a dollar sign or a backslash, since each is rendered in double quotes, and a `checks` key missing from `briefing`. Until this change every such role also ran `--common`, the map README's three sections, `PLAN.md`'s phases and `repo-internals.md` whole, the same for every agent; no role runs it now. Pavol's reasons, 2026-09-27: the early climbs failed on agents' local decisions, and the record exists so that an agent works from the whole territory; and the briefing is in-context learning, since the agents were never trained on Fortress ("Preparing a batch record"). The reason for its shape is two reviews of that day: the misses of the later batches sat in ledger rows, `POSITIONS.md` entries, earlier judges' rulings and the library's own precedents, which neither `FACTS.md` nor the maps carry (`explorations/reviews/worker-global-decisions.md`); and a 29K-token read first, half of it `--common`, priced over the 78 agents measured, would have cost more than the gathering it could remove: on rung workers the cost fell inside the range it could save, and on skeptics and repairs it was a cost in every case (`explorations/reviews/worker-context-cost.md`).

"What a measured defect is worth" is the rule that closes the 22-and-2 gap. Every defect anyone measures has one of three homes and the record says which:

1. **repaired in the rung**, whoever measured it → an assertion in the rung's own gated test, passing before the second skeptic runs (item a);
2. **deferred, specification settles it** → a gated expected-failure test named `XXX…` asserting the specification's answer. The mechanism is cited so it can be checked: `FileTests.java:932` sets `shouldFail` from the name and `:587`/`:654` make `shouldFail != failed` the failure condition, so the suite goes red the day the test starts passing; `:851` names the case and the author's warning at `:853` is why a rung's first `XXX` file is shown to go red on a deliberate local fix (item b);
3. **deferred, specification silent** → a probe with a committed `.txt` capture and a ledger row, the record saying the silence is the reason (item c).

A home-2 test is owed in the batch that measures the defect, even when a later rung or batch is planned to repair it; the argument that it can wait for the repairing rung was ruled against three times (batches 6b, 7R and 7C; `reviews/batch-7C-review.md`, the repeat; `reviews/process-review-6b-7-7R.md`, measure 3). The rule text the agents read carries the same sentence.

The tracked-path check is here too: every `explorations/` path a record cites is `git ls-files --error-unmatch`'d before the rung reports (item g, gap 4).

## Rung worker

Step 1 is the rung's whole briefing ("Shared prefix"), put to the worker as a mission briefing: it works in a language it was not trained on, learns from the briefing how Fortress does things, and then does the rung in the library's own way with the ledger and the decisions on record in mind, looking in the same places where the rung goes past the briefing; and the steps after it moved down by one on 2026-09-27; a worker resumed after the judge rules that its stop is not one reads the whole briefing too, and a repair round after a refusal reads the `checks` sub-list. Step 2 fixes the check line at `run_out_contains=PASS` and notes that `run_out_WIcontains` is now implemented rather than silently inert (item d). Step 9 is the three-homes obligation. The provenance block gains a fifth line, `historical:`, naming every 2012-tree file the rung edits (item 8, the protocol's hard rule on the gate). Step 12 is the tracked-path check. `RUNG_SCHEMA` gains `historicalFiles`, `defectHomes`, `trackedPaths`.

Since 2026-09-26 it also carries `reportText` and `recordText`, the full text of `REPORT.md` and `record.md` word for word, and `stopsMet`, which is required. In batch 5 the harness refused the rung workers' writes of `REPORT.md`, and the gather composed each report from the structured result, whose `summary` is at most 15 lines (`CLIMB-BATCH-6.md` section 8, item 1). `stopsMet` lists every stop the batch record's intro reserves for Pavol that the rung met, including one met on part of the work while the rest lands. Each entry has the stop, its evidence and `liftedBy`, which is the `POSITIONS.md` line of the decision of Pavol's that lifts it, or empty. The skeptic and the merged-diff review return the same field (`STOPS_MET`), and the commit stage reads all three (below).

Each rung's worker commits and pushes to its own `wip/<slug>` branch as it works, so a dead container loses nothing; the gather composes one commit per rung on `main`.

`RUNG_SCHEMA` also gains `forPavol`, every point for Pavol the rung does not settle, one entry each with its evidence: a decision taken inside the rung on a question that was his, a fork, a defect or divergence that lands unrepaired and needs his word (the reports the tail's "What comes back to Pavol" names reach him with the landing and are not such points). A repair round carries in `forPavol` every point the rung still has, the earlier pass's included, since the script reads the last worker's list.

## Skeptic

Check 1 is the `checks` sub-list of the rung's briefing ("Shared prefix"); the checks after it, numbered from 0 until 2026-09-27, moved down by two. Check 9 is the three homes — the skeptic runs the test itself to see a home-1 assertion pass. Check 2 covers the fifth provenance line. A rung whose manifest entry sets `writesState` has every differential run at `FORTRESS_THREADS=1` **and** `=4`, both columns reported (item f, gap 2); a rung without it is told to do the same if its reading of the diff finds a mutable variable, a field, an `atomic` block or a write into `CompilerLibrary`'s state. `SKEPTIC_SCHEMA` gains `recommendedRows` (item 1), `threadCounts`, `defectHomes`, and since 2026-09-26 `skepticText` (the text of `SKEPTIC.md`) and a required `stopsMet`, in which the skeptic names every reserved stop the rung meets as it stands, whether or not the worker named it; its approval lifts none.

Check 10, added 2026-09-23 as check 8, compares the rung's own count table with its report; it is a file read and runs nothing. The skeptic takes `#total` from the committed post-edit table the brief names (`probes/checker-count-postedit.txt` for a `testIsStage` rung) and compares it with the total the report declares. A mismatch goes into `requiredCorrections` with both numbers, so the report is corrected and the rung does not stop over it. A rung that declares a count, in its report or as `expectedCheckerCount`, with no table path named gets the finding "no count table"; a rung that declares none has nothing to compare. The manifest's `expectedCheckerCount` is written beside the two as a prediction, not a value the table must meet. This is f1 of `climb-batch-3-redesign.md` § 4, which `checker-gate-review.md` § 4 recommends taking with the report-only gate: in batch 3 nothing set L's committed table (103) beside the declared 102 between 19:14 and the gate at 00:43.

Checks 11 and 12 search the ledger and the tree for rows and sibling sites of the defect with the skeptic's own terms, and hold the landed text to the decisions on record it rests on; check 5 has the skeptic look for the library's way itself when the worker followed none. They answer the misses of `explorations/reviews/worker-global-decisions.md` by checking the result. The worker is not asked to prove its own search, since its briefing carries the library's way, the ledger rows and the decisions (POSITIONS 2026-09-27, the three questions).

`SKEPTIC_SCHEMA` gains `forPavol`, as the worker's.

A judge's first ruling on a rung or on the merged tree runs on Opus; a second ruling on the same rung or tree runs on Fable (`judgeTier`; POSITIONS 2026-09-26). Its first item to read is the `checks` sub-list of the rung's briefing, or on the merged tree every rung's.

## Gather

Rungs are applied in ascending order of lowest edited line in the shared files, and a cited `file:line` that an earlier applied rung shifted is re-anchored by symbol (item 5). Each `recommendedRows` entry is opened or refused in one sentence (item 1). After a rung's patch is applied, each of its `REPORT.md`, `record.md` and `SKEPTIC.md` that the branch does not carry is written from `reportText`, `recordText` and `skepticText` verbatim (with a first skeptic round's text under "## First round" where there were two); a file the branch carries stands. The same holds for a rung that did not land, whose files are taken out of its branch. Files are staged by an explicit list and `git diff --cached --stat` is read before the commit, never `git add` of a directory (the protocol's hard rule on worker commits, 2026-09-19). A commit touching any path outside `explorations/` carries a `historical:` line (item 8, 7c). A rung that stopped, was dropped or was withheld by `landsOnlyWith` has its `SKEPTIC.md` findings, its recommended rows and the probes those findings cite — taken out of its branch path by path — folded under a "Not landed" heading with no source applied (7a, gap 3). After the last commit the tracked-path check runs over every landed record (7b).

Step 5, added 2026-09-27, is measure 4 of `explorations/reviews/worker-global-decisions.md`: a judge's "For Pavol" section or a rung's list for him stopped in the rung's files and no stage moved it into `PLAN.md`'s queue (batch 5's judge on a size's range, rung F's `=` on `Number`, rows 417, 419 and 420). The script numbers every item a rung's worker, skeptic or judge returns in `forPavol` (`O.worker.1`, `O.skeptic2.1`, `O.judge-stop.1`; `JUDGE_SCHEMA`'s `forPavol` is a list since then) and hands the gather the list. Each item becomes an entry of `PLAN.md`, under "Pavol's answers, in the order they are needed" when it asks for his decision or answer before work on the path can go on, in the group of the phase it must come before, else under "Off the path, parked", in the commit of its rung (a rung that did not land: the commit that carries its "Not landed" section). The gather's result lists each id with its section and entry in `pavolItems`, and each it could not put in, with the reason, in `pavolUnrouted`. The merged-diff review's check 11 checks those entries and puts in any the gather missed, and its own items (`review.1`); the repair on the merged tree puts in its judge's (`judge-review.1`, `judge-gate.1`). Nothing waits on them (Pavol, 2026-09-27): before the commit stage the script logs every id no stage put into `PLAN.md` under one of the two sections, with the gather's reasons, and every return of the script carries the items with `inPlan`, as `forPavol`, for the coordinator.

## Gate

Disk, `compileAll`, the library rebuild **plus a copy of the fresh library cache** for the ladder stage, `testFast`, `testSystem`, the summary and its comparison, the four-thread `atomic` runs, the ladder regression, since 2026-09-22 the checker count, and since 2026-09-27 the distance stage, started in the background right after `compileAll` and read last, as step 9; the last two have their own sections below. Logs go to `tmp/gate-batch-<N>/`, which `.gitignore:64` now ignores (appended at the end of the file so that no existing `.gitignore` citation moves), never committed. The `"Tests expected to pass are failing"` grep is gone (item 4): it is ant's own `<fail message=…>` and reads the same dial as `BUILD SUCCESSFUL`.

`summary.txt` comes from a fixed shell snippet in the script, not agent prose (item 3): one row per suite — `track/suite`, tests, failures, errors, skipped — read from the plain-formatter files under `ProjectFortress/TEST-RESULTS/fast-*/` and `system-*/`, because the four parallel `testFast` tracks interleave their `Running` and `Tests run:` lines in the ant log and cannot be paired there; the `BUILD SUCCESSFUL` and `Total time` lines come from the logs. `gate_compare` diffs it against the newest landed summary, found with `git log --name-only`; a suite whose count fell, or that is gone, is red and named (gap 1). The `system-<i>` rows are compared by their sum, not one by one: `testSystem` is one suite sharded by sorted index (`FileTests.java:790-806`), so a file added to `ProjectFortress/tests/` moves every later file along the shards; climb batch 2's gate went red on exactly this after `02d09a39f` added two interpreter tests between the baseline and the batch base (`compile-ladder/climb-batch-2/JUDGE-gate.md`). `run_bg` runs its command string in a subshell so a `cd` inside it cannot escape the log, and `atomic_runs` resolves its out-file before its own `cd` (both measured by that batch's gate stage). The first landed summary is `explorations/compile-ladder/gate-baseline/summary.txt`.

The four-thread stage runs the thirteen `atomic` programs the repair batch measured — the ten of `other_compiler_tests/atomicTest.test` plus `AtomicTopLevelObjectVar`, `AtomicTopLevelVar` and `MutableTopLevelVarInLoop` — three times each at `FORTRESS_THREADS=4`. Any `FAIL`, `NO-PASS` or compile failure is red; a timeout is re-run once and then red. The 39 lines go into the summary.

The ladder stage re-runs the 85 files of `baseline-2026-09-19/pass-list.txt` and the eighteen microGPT components of `microgpt-phase.md` with the baseline's own drivers, copying the gate's fresh library cache instead of rebuilding one. The 85 are compared on phase **and** stdout, with a filter masking the `Operation took …ms` line: those three `nestedTransactions` captures are the only ones of the 85 whose output varies, and masking the line rather than exempting the file keeps the rest of their output checked. The eighteen are **compiled only** — `fortress compile`, the phase reached — never linked and never run in this stage (Pavol, 2026-09-19); when they one day compile, running them is a separate non-gating stage, not the gate. A `DOWN`, `STDOUT` or `MISSING` line is red unless `expectedMoves` declared it; a move up is the batch's result and is reported. The judge's `gate` question now names the ways the gate can be red — five since the checker count below — and where each one's evidence is.

## The checker count, and a rung whose test is that stage (2026-09-22)

The gate's eighth step runs the compiler's static checker over the **interpreter's** library and compares its error total with the last landed one. It answers Pavol's decision of 2026-09-21 (`POSITIONS.md`, "the library route"): one library, the interpreter's `FortressLibrary` and `FortressBuiltin`, becomes the library the compiler checks, and the compiler's own prelude is deleted at the switch-over. Step 1 of `library-route-judgement.md` is what makes the distance a stage rather than a script — it was measured twice by hand (92 on 2026-09-16, 93 on 2026-09-19) and nothing kept it measured, which is the one-off validation these rules forbid everywhere else. Pavol's own objection is on record and this stage does not answer it: a count is a metric, not a measure of the work behind it (`postmortem-2026-09-19/positions-checker-gate.md`, 15:18).

The stage is `explorations/coordinator/tools/checker-count/run.sh`, with `WorldFlip.java` and the instrumented `StaticChecker` copy beside it — both copied from `perf-probes/prelude/`, so that the gate does not depend on a probe directory. It compiles the two against `bin/fortress_classpath`, puts the shadow checker ahead of `ProjectFortress/build`, and runs the compiler phase order over `Library/FortressLibrary.fss` with `Shell.useInterpreterLibraries()` under its own `-Dfortress.caches` in the gate's log directory: no library rebuild, nothing written into `default_repository/`, 20 s. Its table is one row per api with that api's error count, then `#total`, `#locations` (the distinct `file:line` the errors were reported at), `#crash` (the crash the checker meets after the apis — today the `nat` gap, `Not yet implemented` at `STypesUtil.scala:557`) and `#shadow`, which says whether the copy still matches the tracked checker, because a copy of a tracked source goes stale silently and then the number is not this tree's. `checker_compare` is red on a total that rose, on a changed crash line and on a stale shadow, each unless a rung of the batch declared it in `expectedCheckerCount` or `expectedCheckerCrash`; a total that fell is the batch's result and is reported, and the per-api rows and `#locations` are printed either way and are not themselves red. `last_landed_checker_count` finds the comparand the way `last_landed_summary` finds the summary, over `gate-baseline/checker-count.txt` and `climb-batch-*/gate/checker-count.txt`; the gate writes the table and commits nothing, and the commit stage lands it beside `summary.txt`, which is what keeps the chain of comparands unbroken. The first comparand is `explorations/compile-ladder/gate-baseline/checker-count.txt`, this change's own dry run on the tree at `6a6b36531`.

Until 2026-09-23 that comparison was a gate: a total above the last landed one was red unless it equalled a rung's `expectedCheckerCount`, and in climb batch 3 it went red on L's 103 against a declared 102. Since then the total is reported and is never red on its own, for the reasons in `checker-gate-review.md` (§ 2, and § 4, which recommends option (b) with (f)'s stale-copy check): a fix can raise the count, because the checker stops checking an api at its first errors (`StaticChecker.java:268-272`), and two rungs' changes do not add, so neither a rise nor a missed declaration tells progress from harm. `checker_compare` prints `COUNT UP`, `COUNT DOWN` or `COUNT SAME` with the last landed total, the measured one and the declared totals, as in `COUNT UP   93 -> 103, declared L: 102`, and exits 1 only on a crash line no rung declared, a stale shadow or a table with no total. Dry-run over the landed baseline and batch-3 tables, the rise exits 0 with or without a declaration, and a stale shadow or an undeclared crash change exits 1. `expectedCheckerCount` stays in the manifest as a prediction the gate prints. Explaining the change is now the merged-diff review's job: its check 9 waits for the gate's table (the `#shadow` row is written last), diffs it against the last landed one, and ties every new or risen row to a named rung edit, one line each in a new `checkerRows` field; a row it cannot tie to a rung edit is blocking and goes to the judge and a repair. The judge's `gate` question and the rung and skeptic briefs no longer say an undeclared rise is red. Two sentences still say the count may only fall and are left for their own edits: the header of `run.sh` and the manifest comment on `expectedCheckerCount`, which the next manifest replaces.

A rung whose manifest entry sets `testIsStage` has that stage as its test. Step 2 of the order of work then asks for the stage's table captured before the edit and step 3 for the table after it, in place of the `.fss` component and its `.test` file; the skeptic's check 3 asks for both captures and re-runs the stage itself to see the second one come out again, and check 6 checks the two tables differ in the way the report says. Nothing else about such a rung changes — the ladder subset, the three homes, the provenance block and the tracked-path check all stand. This is the one place the test-first rule is met by a permanent stage instead of a test file, and the reason is that no program can yet be compiled against the interpreter's prelude (`library-route-judgement.md` § 2 step 1, which also sets the stop condition: two runs printing the same table). The first rung of the library route is the rung this flag exists for.

The overloading checker's memo is off in the stage since 2026-09-27 (`CLIMB-BATCH-7.md` section 8, item 2): `run.sh` passes `-Dfortress.analyzer.overload.cache=false`, read at `OverloadingChecker.scala:77`, and its table gains a `#cache` row before `#shadow`, which stays last. The memo is keyed on declaration pairs and hides 27 to 35 errors depending on the build order once an api reaches its overloading check (`perf-probes/nat/triage.md` section 1; `FACTS.md`, "The hidden layer, classified"). Until batch 7 the `FortressLibrary` api stopped before that check and the stage read the same either way; batch 7's rung H clears the api's early errors, and from then on a count taken with the memo on would depend on the build order.
