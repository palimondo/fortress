## The ruling

The skeptic is right on the refusal ground. At Coercions.java:200-206 (the skeptic cites :212-217, but the method is at :200-206), CoercedCall applies the overload chosen before the conversion to the converted arguments. OverloadedMethod.java:58-62 does the same for methods. So g(NarrowOf(2)), with g(Wide) and g(WideOf) declared, gives g(Wide) under walk (probes/skeptic/diff-1.txt:5, :12), while the compiled run gives g(WideOf) (:21, :28). Walk's own bound value also gives g(WideOf) (:6), and so does walk's functional-method refinement inside the same CoercedCall (:8). The method twin behaves the same way (diff-3.txt:4, :9 against :16, :21).

The worker reads 'the declaration with parameter type T is applied to the call' (conversions-coercions.tex:535-536) as fixing the declaration that runs. That reading is wrong. The passage describes a static call, rewritten 'for pedagogical purposes; implementation techniques may vary' (:512-514); the rewriting inserts explicit coerce calls into the expression (:253-265). What :567-570 fixes statically is the coercion, not the declaration that runs. The overloading chapter says what runs: the statically most specific declaration is the anchor, and run-time dispatch considers 'that declaration plus declarations that are more specific than that declaration' (advanced/overloading.tex:73-78). 'The most specific declaration that is applicable to a dynamic call is more specific than' the static one (:466-468). Every call dispatches among the declarations applicable at run time (basic/overloading.tex:263-276). The specification's own example (:572-604) cannot tell the two readings apart, because B excludes A, so a coerced A can only reach f(A). The skeptic's citations are correct; the advanced chapter's two passages are the ones that settle it, and the repair's messages cite them. The value-dependent coerce in instruction 2 also proves the repair's cache reasoning: the coercions depend only on the argument types, which are the cache key (OverloadedFunction.java:760-776), but the type of a coerce result may depend on the value. So the declaration must be chosen per call on the converted arguments, never cached per the original types.
