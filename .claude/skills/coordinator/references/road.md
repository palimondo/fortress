# Planning the road

`explorations/coordinator/PLAN.md` holds the phases, the questions for the curator in the order they are needed, and the parked items. Keep every open question there, in the order it needs deciding, and none only in your context.

**nine steps**
: Order in which a fork is answered from the evidence before it reaches the curator: (1) what the mathematics says; (2) what each path does today, measured; (3) what the specification says under every spelling; (4) what the library does in the same family; (5) where the designers departed from Java; (6) what peer languages and their implementations do; (7) what the commits say; (8) the derivation from the curator's principles in POSITIONS; (9) the decision, in the curator's words.

**re-approval rows**
: Earlier decisions put to the curator again, parked in PLAN, "Off the path, parked".

## Procedures

- Ask for the curator's answers in PLAN's order. Raise the re-approval rows and other parked items only when the work reaches them.
- If a probe can settle a known fork, run it while you plan, before the batch is briefed. Put its result to the curator as an ask, so that the worker gets a task with the decision made.
- Answer a fork by the nine steps before you put it to the curator. Seek one global rule from type theory and the peers' implementations, not a rule on a rule.
- In the ask, name the library's own way first. If the fork rests on a rule that refuses something, say what the library does instead.
- Put rows to the curator one theme per message, each row in detail, with a refresher: other languages, and what makes sense in a high-performance language. The count of reversals is not the measure.
- Give idle time to the parked research in PLAN. It needs no go, but a new deliverable does: discuss it with the curator before you make it.
- Park what needs the curator's machine; do not simulate it.
