# Climb batch 28: orientation admission

Two entries are admitted. Together they make one edit:

1. **An integer converts to another integer type by a checked getter or by a low-bits name (P-161, F-331).** This is a new bold rule in "Numbers". F-172, F-132 and F-307 changed with it, and are folded into it.

The orientation has never stated how one integer type converts into another. Three things make a worker's default wrong here. Pretraining offers Java's `(int)` cast and Scala's `.toInt`, which wrap silently. The team's walk made `narrow` raise. The skill's core says that "Overflow raises `IntegerOverflow`". So a worker either picks `narrow` as "the conversion", without knowing that it wraps, or calls `narrow` or `widen` on a `ZZ`, which no longer has either. Both mistakes are on record (the table below).

No other entry changes what a worker brings to a task, or an area part already owns it. Most of the landing moves native bindings and the I/O objects, arrays, threads and `args` onto compiled helpers. Walk's values do not change. The area parts own the natives, the class loader and the checker's tie order. Several of their sentences are now false, listed under "Elsewhere" below. Twenty-one changed entries changed only their line citations, which the batch's library hunks moved. Nothing in the orientation itself is made false by this landing (`grep` of `revival-changes.md` for glue, `args`, threads, files, `catch` and rows 349, 466, 527, 543, 733, 895, 929 and 935 finds only the power rule's unrelated "no `catch` sees").

  - the five getters keep the value or raise `IntegerOverflow`: they are the way to convert a value;
  - the five low-bits names keep the bits and never raise, each on every type where it applies;
