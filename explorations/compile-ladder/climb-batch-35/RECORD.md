### Rung R, `rung-radix-point-leading-zeros`: a numeral with a radix point keeps its leading zeros in its digit count, on both paths

**The change.** The team's parser kept a numeral with a radix point in its syntax tree with all but one of its leading zeros deleted from the numeral's text: `ExprFactory.makeFloatLiteralExpr` trimmed `00.5` to `0.5` (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/nodes_util/ExprFactory.java:1640-1643` at `a874948ac`), while its integer numeral kept them (`makeIntLiteralExpr`, `:609-632`), so on both of the team's implementations the tree of `00.5` was that of `0.5`. The tree now keeps the digits as written, leading zeros among them, so a numeral's digit count `n`, which a program's coercion over `RadixPointNumeral[\n, m, r, v\]` reads, is 3 for `00.5` on walk and on the compiled path, and a compiled-path message that quotes the numeral shows `00.5`; the numeral's value is unchanged. Tests: `ProjectFortress/tests/RadixPointLeadingZerosWalk.fss`, `ProjectFortress/compiler_tests/RadixPointLeadingZerosCompiled.test`. The reason: the Working Draft defines `n` as "the total number of digits" of a numeral with a radix point (`Specification-1.0-frozen/basic/expressions/literals.tex:110-111`), and the specification as revised says "the number $n$ of its digits, leading zeros among them" (`Specification/basic/expressions/literals.tex:101`); with the trim, a coercion that read `n` failed 'FAIL: a ZZ32: 2 =/= a ZZ32: 3; literals.tex, section "Literals": 00.5 has three digits' under walk, and 'FAIL:  2 =/= 3' compiled.

**Provenance.** Rung R of climb batch 35: row 960; P-174 (a numeral's four numbers, `n` the count of its digits); fork R1 of `explorations/coordinator/CLIMB-BATCH-35.md`, "Forks the batch takes" (the node keeps its digits as written; way not taken, a count kept beside a trimmed text).

### Rung W, `rung-walk-subscript-tuple-binding`: walk applies a subscript before a superscript with all its indices, and runs a tuple binding's right side once

**The change.**

- The team's walk applied a subscript that is a math item of a primary, as in `m[3,7]^2`, `x[5]^2`, `g[3][7]` or `x[5]k`, without its first index: its helper evaluated the indices by the function that drops the first element of a call's list (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/interpreter/evaluator/Evaluator.java:935` at `a874948ac`). So `m[3,7]^2` gave `(m[7])^2`, 11449, and the other forms stopped, 'Failed to find any matching overload, args = ()'. The tree now passes every index (`Evaluator.java:1069-1071`): `m[3,7]^2` is 1075369, `x[5]^2` is `(x[5])^2`, `g[3][7]` is `(g[3])[7]`; test `ProjectFortress/tests/SubscriptThenSuperscriptWalk.fss`. The reason: the specification's reassociation replaces a math item and the element before it by "a single element that does the appropriate operator application" (`Specification/basic/operators/juxtameaning.tex:164-168`), the team's own `forSubscriptExpr` passes every index (`Evaluator.java:1037`), and the compiled path gives `(x[5])^2` (`ProjectFortress/compiler_tests/SubscriptThenSuperscriptCompiled.test`).
- The team's walk bound a tuple binding's right side to a temporary and then initialized each name from a field of the right side itself (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/interpreter/rewrite/DesugarerVisitor.java:872-874` at `a874948ac`), so `(first, second) = pairOnce()` ran `pairOnce` three times and its names held elements of different runs, at the top level and as a field of an object or object expression. The tree now binds each name of a tuple expression to its own element, and otherwise has each name read the temporary, declared at the names' types (`DesugarerVisitor.java:854`, `:868-877`, `:878-893`, `:897`): the right side runs once, and a numeral converts to its name's declared type; tests `ProjectFortress/tests/TopLevelTupleBindingOnceWalk.fss`, `ProjectFortress/tests/FieldTupleBindingOnceWalk.fss` and `ProjectFortress/tests/TupleBindingNumeralTypesWalk.fss`. The reason: tuple notation declares several variables from one value, "especially helpful when a function application returns a tuple of values" (`Specification/basic/variables.tex:99-120`), a field declaration has the same meanings (`Specification/basic/objects.tex:295-297`), the team's comment at the site says "Introduce a temporary, then initialize elements piece-by-piece" (`DesugarerVisitor.java:859-860`), the compiled path evaluates the right side once (`ProjectFortress/compiler_tests/TopLevelTupleBindingCompiled.test`), at the tuple type of the names' types (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/scala_src/typechecker/impls/Decls.scala:272-277`), and the team's code generator binds each field of a tuple expression to its element (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/compiler/codegen/CodeGen.java:5230-5236`).

**Provenance.** Gap ledger rows 1 and 1026; the forks W1 ("A subscript that follows a primary's front evaluates all its indices, as `forSubscriptExpr` does") and W2 ("Each name of a top-level tuple binding reads its element from the temporary"), both taken at their defaults; climb batch 35, run 1, rung W. The field form follows W2 by the same member and the objects chapter's sentence; no decision on record names it.

### Rung S, `rung-self-not-first-dispatch`: the dispatch method of a functional method reads each argument at its own local

Revival change: none. The change brings the compiled path to what the specification says and walk does: a functional method's parameter type holds self at its place (`Specification/basic/overloading.tex:177-180`), and a call runs the most specific declaration applicable to it at run time (`Specification/basic/overloading.tex:298-311`); walk runs each of the four test programs to 'PASS' on the base. The team's code generator read the receiver for the argument before self, a defect, not a rule of the language.

### Rung O, `rung-override-compiled`: the modifier `override` compiled, and the checker's refusal of one that overrides nothing

**The change.** The team's code generator refused every method declaration with the modifier `override`: `fnDeclCompilableModifiers` held the getter, setter, `io`, `private` and `abstract` modifiers only (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/compiler/codegen/CodeGen.java:2866-2868` at `a874948ac`), and `forFnDecl` stopped, 'Don't know how to compile this kind of FnDecl'. The team's checker read no `override` (`OverloadingChecker.scala` at `a874948ac` holds no `isOverride`), so it accepted one that overrides nothing. The tree now compiles the modifier: a type's class holds the override and, at each declaration that the override hides, a method at that declaration's signature that runs the override (`compiler_tests/OverrideModifierCodeGeneration`, `OverrideBridgesCompiled`); and the checker refuses an `override` that overrides no inherited declaration, where the parameter types are equal too, 'Invalid override of f in object B: ... has the modifier override and does not override any inherited declaration.' (`compiler_tests/XXXOverrideNothingCompiled`). Two forms stay refused by the code generator: an override of or by a method with static parameters of its own, 'Can't compile an override of or by a method with static parameters of its own yet.', and one in a generic trait or object whose parameter types name a static parameter, 'Can't compile an override in a trait or object with static parameters, ...' (`XXXOverrideGenericMethodCompiled`, `XXXOverrideOverStaticParameterCompiled`). The checker refuses an `override` under a conditional extension that walk runs (`XXXOverrideConditionalExtensionChecker`), and checks none in an object expression. The reason: the traits chapter gives the modifier its meaning and makes an `override` that overrides nothing a static error (`Specification/basic/traits.tex:673-683`), and walk built both (`tests/disp0.fss`, `tests/OverrideNothingWalk.fss`).

**Provenance.** Rows 650 and 653 of the gap ledger; climb batch 35's forks O1 (the class holds what the checker reads as provided, by its table) and O2 (the checker refuses an override that overrides nothing by the chapter's rule, with walk's words), and review-queue item 73's default, "strict"; climb batch 35, rung O.

### Rung A, `rung-exit-out-of-atomic`: an exit out of an atomic block keeps the block's writes, on both paths

The change: the team's walk runs an atomic block in `FortressTaskRunner.doItOnce`, which commits the block's transaction only after the body ends normally and gives it up uncommitted in its `finally` when anything leaves the body (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/interpreter/evaluator/tasks/FortressTaskRunner.java:121-138` at 13f4fdea75), so an `exit` (a `LabelException`) out of the block lost the block's writes. The compiled path's handler, `BaseTask.leaveTransaction`, did the same: it ended the transaction without a commit for anything but an abort (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/runtimeSystem/BaseTask.java:293-314` at 13f4fdea75). The tree now commits the block's transaction when an exit leaves it, on both paths, and an exit that leaves a nested block and its outer one commits both; a commit that fails runs the block again; an exception other than an exit still discards the writes. Tests: `ProjectFortress/tests/ExitOutOfAtomicWalk.fss` and `ProjectFortress/compiler_tests/ExitOutOfAtomicCompiled.test`. The reason: the specification, "If it completes abruptly by exiting to an enclosing \KWD{label} expression, writes within the block are retained and become visible to other threads." (`Specification/basic/expressions/atomic.tex:60-63`), and the measured failure on the base: 'FAIL: a ZZ32: 0 =/= a ZZ32: 7; writes within a block that an exit leaves are retained' (walk), 'FAIL:  0 =/= 7; ...' (compiled). An exit that runs after its label block has completed commits the blocks it passes as well, since neither path tells it from an exit to an enclosing label. Under walk an exit out of a `tryatomic` block that no `atomic` block encloses still dies before its label.

Provenance: the question was review-queue item 561, who builds the exit out of an atomic block (row 1039); the answer followed is climb batch 35's fork A1, "An exit out of an atomic block commits the block's transaction, then leaves; a failed commit runs the block again, as at a normal end; an exception other than an exit discards the writes as today" (default); built by climb batch 35, rung A.
