# Planning the road

`explorations/coordinator/PLAN.md` holds the phases, the questions for the curator in the order they are needed, and the parked items. Keep every open question there, in the order it needs deciding, and none only in your context.

**nine steps**
: Order in which a fork is answered from the evidence: (1) what the mathematics says; (2) what each path does today, measured; (3) what the specification says under every spelling; (4) what the library does in the same family; (5) where the designers departed from Java; (6) what peer languages and their implementations do; (7) what the commits say; (8) the derivation from the curator's principles in POSITIONS; (9) the decision, yours or the curator's (`SKILL.md`, "The curator's role and decisions"). Steps 3, 5 and 7 give the team's word, step 4 the library, and step 6 the peers; the order sets no precedence. `SKILL.md` gives the peers their place.

**re-approval rows**
: Earlier decisions put to the curator again, parked in PLAN, "Off the path, parked".

## Procedures

- Ask for the curator's answers in PLAN's order, after you check each question against the three principles. Raise the re-approval rows and other parked items only when the work reaches them.
- If a probe can answer a known fork, run it while you plan, before the batch is briefed, so that the worker gets a task with the decision made. If the fork is consequential, put the probe's result to the curator as an ask.
- Answer each fork by the nine steps. Seek one global rule from type theory, and from the peers' implementations where the peers weigh (`SKILL.md`, "The curator's role and decisions"), not a rule on a rule.
- Put re-approval rows to the curator one theme per message, each row in detail, with the ask's short primer (`SKILL.md`, "An ask", item 2) and what makes sense in a high-performance language. The count of reversals is not the measure.
- Give idle time to the parked research in PLAN. It needs no go, but a new deliverable does: discuss it with the curator before you make it.
- Park what needs the curator's machine; do not simulate it.
