# The passages that read `comprises` at the level of types: the judgement

The question. Climb batch 7C rewrote the traits chapter to the type group's 2012 reading of a `comprises` clause: the clause says to which types a trait's values belong, and a trait with static parameters may stand unlisted between a closed trait and its listed types when every trait or object extending it is below a listed type (`Specification/basic/traits.tex:235-252`). Four passages elsewhere still argue from a clause at the level of types: the intersection example (P1, `basic/types-vals-vars.tex:583-604`), the Meet Rule's example (P2, `advanced/overloading.tex:275-307`), the rule on abstract function declarations (P3, `basic/functions.tex:370-415`) and a team draft note (P4, `basic/components/source-code.tex:428-434`). Row 491 asks whether they still hold and what the specification and the two implementations should do. The clean worker's note (`reviews/comprises-type-level-ways.md`, "the ways note" below) lists six ways and marks its own reading. This note checks the deciding claims at their sources, adds one measurement, weighs the ways and recommends one.

## 1. The type question, in plain words

Terms first.

- `trait S comprises { U, V }` closes `S`. The type reading: every type under `S` is under `U` or `V`. This was the Working Draft's note and it is Java's `sealed`. The value reading, "coverage": every value of `S` is a value of `U` or `V`. This is the 2012 Types chapter and Welterweight, and batch 7C's text. A trait has no values of its own; only objects make values. So under the value reading a trait may sit between `S` and its listed types, and it is a type below `S` and below no listed type.
- P1 and P2 conclude "any subtype of both S and T must be a subtype of V. Thus, V = S ∩ T" from the clauses of `S` and `T` and `U excludes W`. That first step needs the type reading. Under the value reading the same steps show that every value of both `S` and `T` is a value of `V`: `V` covers `S ∩ T`.
- P3 and P4 speak of "every immediate subtype" of a closed trait. Under the type reading those are the listed types. Under 7C's text they can differ: `AnyIntegral`'s immediate subtype is `Integral[\I\]`, its listed types the five integer types.

What each source says, checked at its line:

1. The specification's four passages: the type reading (P1 `:583-604`; P2 `:303-307`; P3 `:377-382`, `:413-415`; P4 `:429`). All are 2009 text, Sukyoung Ryu's drafts of 2009-11-02 to 11-06 (`cec470a34`, `a8e403b50`, `0f49d8698`), and the 1.0 release has no such example (ways note section 7; the dates verified with `git log`).
2. The team's own list of future work, in the same drafts and in the Working Draft: "Identifying the intersection of any two types with comprises clauses with the union of their common subtypes … S&T ~~ V. We agreed to revise the Meet rule to address this with the coverage check" (`Specification/appendices/future.tex:269-285`; `Specification-1.0-frozen/appendices/future.tex:269-285`). So in 2009 the team knew the example needed a coverage check and named that as the fix.
3. The later Types chapter (Luchangco, 2012): it names covering as a relation beside subtyping and exclusion (`Documentation/Specification/Prose/Language/types.tick:36-38`), reads a clause as coverage, "covered by the union" of the listed types (`:384-390`), never defines covering ("% What about "covering"?", `:934`), and repeats P1's example word for word (`:673-688`). Both readings side by side, unreconciled.
4. Welterweight (2012): the value reading in its grammar (`Papers/Welterweight/grammar.tick:21-22`); rule Sub-Comprises, a closed trait is below whatever all its listed types are below (`fig-subtyping.tick:207-211`); the Covered judgement for abstract declarations, "considered to be covered if it is implemented for all the types comprised" (`static.tick:27-47`, `fig-covered.tick`); the Meet rule still asks for a declaration equivalent to the intersection (Meet-Third, `fig-meetrule.tick:56-60`); and its intersection rule, one side below the target or the two sides excluding (`fig-subtyping.tick:48-51`), cannot derive `S ∩ T ≤ V` for the example. So the calculus put coverage into abstract declarations and not into the Meet rule; on the example it is silent.
5. Guy Steele's last code on it (2012-06-06, `59fdeff62`, its diff opened): a closed trait is below a union when every listed type is, "This should improve the behavior of 'covers' checks in AbstractMethodChecker" (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/scala_src/types/TypeAnalyzer.scala:152-157`); the same for trait below trait, commented out, "Gets a stack overflow error; maybe we don't need this in practice. GLS 6/6/12" (`:269-273`).
6. The checker's normalizer already shrinks a closed conjunct by its clause when another conjunct excludes one of its listed types (`TypeAnalyzer.scala:645-655`, `normConjunct`; team code, present at the July 2012 import). Measured now (section 2): that is enough for the checker to accept `f(S)`, `f(T)`, `f(V)` in one shape, so the compiled path already runs a coverage meet in part.

Which is the designers' latest word. By the decisions of 2026-09-23 and 2026-09-26 the type group's later, implementation-informed texts outweigh the 2009 text. They read a clause at the level of values everywhere they use it; the type-level example survives in the Types chapter as an unrevised copy of 2009 text, beside the coverage relation it names and does not define. The team's stated plan for the Meet rule, from 2009 to the Working Draft's future-work list, is the coverage check. That is the word to finish.

Does the compiled checker already find a meet through a `comprises` clause?

- `MeetViaExclusion`: `trait S comprises { U, V }`, `trait T comprises { V } excludes U`, `V extends { S, T }`, with `f(S)`, `f(T)`, `f(V)`. `T` excludes `U`, so the normalizer's existing rule shrinks `S`'s clause to `{ V }` inside `S ∩ T`, and `V ≤ T` gives the meet `V`. - `MeetViaExclusionNoV`, the control, the same program without `f(V)`: the checker refuses it, "Invalid overloading of f"; walk as above. So the acceptance goes through the meet the clause gives, not through an exclusion between `S` and `T`.
- What it settles. The compiled checker already computes a coverage meet in the one shape its normalizer reaches, and the two paths already disagree on such a program. Row 492's example is the shape it does not reach: no exclusion prunes either clause, so `S ∩ T` is never expanded to `(U ∪ V) ∩ (V ∪ W)`, whose distribution with `U excludes W` gives `(U ∩ V) ∪ V`, that is `V`. Rung C's work under way 2 is that distribution, an extension of team code that runs today, not a new mechanism. Walk has neither the expansion nor a lattice meet beyond equality and subtyping (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/interpreter/evaluator/types/FType.java:338-347`; `FTypeTuple.java:197-258`), and its load check reports "unrelated" before it looks for a meet (`interpreter/evaluator/values/OverloadedFunction.java:439-462`, the message at `:545`; `meetExistsIn` at `:645`, consulted only when each declaration is better in some parameter, `:470-472`). Row 492's sentence, "neither path uses the comprises clauses to find a meet at all", is right for the example and a little too strong for the checker: a note on the row.

One interplay the note did not have. Batch N's rung I builds the ambiguity check at `Functionals.scala:463` (POSITIONS 2026-09-28, the conversion judgement's decision 1). Under the Meet rule in its equivalence form, two declarations both applicable to a static call always have a third, more specific, applicable to it, so a tie among unconverted candidates cannot arise. The coverage case is the one place it can: an argument whose static type sits between, `g: G[\ZZ32\]`, with `f(S)` and `f(T)` applicable and `f(V)` not. Whether rung I's check errors on such a tie depends on how it is written; after batch 7b it must not. So the "one sentence" the note asks for has a code site and an order: it lands in 7b's rung C, after N.

## 3. The ways, weighed

### Way 2. Restate the four passages at the level of values, "covers" defined once

- What it does. P1: "any value of both S and T is a value of V; thus V covers S ∩ T", the intersection definitions at `:583-588` unchanged and one definition of covering beside them: a type covers another when every value of the second is a value of the first; a closed trait is covered by the union of its listed types. - What it finishes. The reading 7C adopted on his option 1; the Types chapter's relation, defined; Welterweight's Covered, carried to the Meet rule as the team agreed in 2009; the checker's own partial coverage meet (section 2).
- The two paths. Exclusion, below-a-union and the abstract-method check stay as they are. Row 492's repair becomes a requirement: the checker's meet search distributes closed conjuncts by their clauses with excluded pairs dropped (`normConjunct`; `validOverloading` at `scala_src/typechecker/OverloadingChecker.scala:418`), and walk's load check consults a clause-reading meet before it reports "unrelated". By the Return Type Rule the declaration that runs is more specific than each of them, so its return type is below that intersection: sound, deterministic, and the most precise type. The head of a sort, the checker today, is sound and order-dependent.

## 8. Taken as defaults, listed for his review

- The type of an in-between call is the intersection of the minimal candidates' return types (section 3). The alternative, the head of a sort, is sound and order-dependent, the failing rung W cures elsewhere.
