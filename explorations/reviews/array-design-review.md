# Review of the array design for the compiled world

## 0. What was checked and how

The design's own citations were opened: every line number in §1, §2, §4c, §5, §6a-§6e and §8 was read in the tree, and the two rulings were read in `POSITIONS.md:40`. Where the design says "unprobed" or "not measured", or where a claim depends on what the checker does rather than on what the interpreter does, a probe was run: five Fortress programs through `fortress compile` under a private cache (one also through the interpreter), and two Java models beside the kernel models of `perf-probes/kernels/java`. Nothing under `default_repository/`, `Library/` or `ProjectFortress/` was touched.

The standard applied is the one in the brief: finish what the designers intended, judged by the July 2012 draft and the breadcrumbs in all three sources of truth (specification, interpreter and its library, compiler and its prelude); storage is `double[]`, unboxed; sizes are `nat` parameters and the two forks are taken together; extensions go into the standard library in the library's own patterns; and the measuring stick is one program, microGPT compiled and running fast.

## 1. Findings

### Decisive 1. The design keeps the model's unsized static types and never checks the model's applications against a static checker; as written, nothing in microGPT and none of the three kernels passes the type checker, whatever the storage is

What the design says. §3: "every constructor takes a run-time size and returns the unsized `Array[\RR64,ZZ32\]` or `Array[\RR64,(ZZ32,ZZ32)\]`"; §6d, default: "the run-time-sized factory returns the unsized `Array[\RR64,ZZ32\]` as it already does in the model … and only literal and symbol sizes get sized classes"; §6c: the minimal checker "covers all 81 and all of microGPT's" declarations; §8: the second rung times "`kdot` compiled over a native `dot`".

What the model is. `rmsn(x: Array[\RR64,ZZ32\])` computes `x DOT x` (`run-c4/src/MicroGptFlat.fss:27`); `DOT` exists only on `Vector[\T,n\]` (`Library/FortressLibrary.fss:2264-2265`, and the api `.fsi:1508`); `FlatArrays` declares no `DOT`. `view(p, i)` returns `Array[\RR64,(ZZ32,ZZ32)\]` (`:45`), `wq^T` needs `Matrix[\RR64,r,c\]` (`:23`), and `x1 wq^T` (`:58`) is the library's juxtaposition on two `Matrix` (`.fss:2625-2627`); `x1` comes from `rows`, which returns `Array` (`FlatArrays.fss:139`). `diag(vm / nv)` (`:63`) passes an `Array` where `diag` takes `Vector[\RR64,s\]` (`FlatArrays.fss:41`); `gather(wte, ids)` (`:57`) passes the `Array` from `view` where `gather` takes `Matrix`; `rows(rmsn, x)` (`:57`) passes the `Array` from `gather + gather` where `rows` takes `Matrix`. The kernels are the same: `kdot.fss` writes `x DOT y` on two `vec(…)` results typed `Array[\RR64,ZZ32\]` (`FlatArrays.fss:14`); `kmat.fss` writes `a b` on two `mat(…)` results; `krows.fss` writes `rows(rmsn, m)` on a `mat(…)` result.

Why it runs on the interpreter. `array[\E\](n)` reflects `n` into a nat and calls `array1[\E,n\]()` (`.fss:1927-1934`), which selects `vector[\T,s0\]()` for any `Number` by `typecase` (`:2240-2244`), so the run-time object behind every `Array[\RR64,ZZ32\]` in the model is a `__DefaultVector[\RR64,n\]`, a `Vector`, and the interpreter dispatches on the run-time object; the static type is never consulted (FACTS, execution model: the checker "runs only on the compile path"). The same holds for the views: `view` calls `viewN(p, off, reflect(nr), reflect(nc))` (`FlatArrays.fss:60-62`), where `reflect` returns `NatParam` (`LibraryBuiltin/NatReflect.fsi`) and `viewN` takes `N[\r\]`; only the run-time object is an `N[\16\]`.

What the checker does with it. Probe `p3_static_dispatch.fss`, no `nat` in it so nothing else can be what fails: a trait `Vec[\T\] extends Arr[\T\]`, an object under `Vec[\RR64\]`, a function on two `Vec[\RR64\]`, and a call on a value whose static type is `Arr[\RR64\]`. Compile path: `(Vec[\RR64\], Vec[\RR64\])->RR64 is not applicable to an argument of type (Arr[\RR64\], Arr[\RR64\])`, exit 255 (`p3_static_dispatch.compile.out`); interpreter, same file: `p3 9.0` (`p3_static_dispatch.interp.out`). As an operator the refusal is identical and lists the prelude's scalar `DOT` overloads beside the user's (`p3b_static_dispatch_opr.compile.out`).

Where the first compiled microGPT fails, then. Before the model: `FlatArrays.fss:62`, `viewN(p, off, reflect(nr), reflect(nc))`, because `NatParam` is not `N[\r\]` statically and the checker cannot infer `r` from a run-time object. Then `MicroGptFlat.fss:27` on `x DOT x`, and after that nearly every line of `step` (`:57-70`). The design's own second-rung measurement, `kdot` through a native `dot`, cannot happen as written: `kdot.fss:15` is `x DOT y` on two unsized `Array`s.

Why this is decisive. The two rulings of 2026-09-19 (`POSITIONS.md:38, 40`) are "the model's text is the notation" and "nat sizes are central, not sidestepped"; on the compile path these two collide on every operator application in the model, because the model's text carries sizes only in its run-time objects. The design says "the checker sees the library's own types; the model's `Array[\RR64,ZZ32\]`, `Vector[\RR64,s\]`, `Matrix[\RR64,r,c\]` resolve as written" (§4c); the types resolve, the applications do not. That collision is not named anywhere in the 203 lines, and it is the first decision Pavol has to take (§2, decision D), not a repair a rung can make.

### Decisive 2. The default for "what implements the sized traits over the store" has no checked mechanism on this path; the checker refuses it, including through the library's own selection idiom

What the design says. §4c and §7 question 2, default: `__DefaultVector[\T, nat s0\]` "keeps its shape and its `mem` field is the `RR64` store for `RR64`, `FZZ32Vector` for `ZZ32`, the boxed store otherwise, chosen by the factory from the run-time type tag … it needs one probe and no loader change, and it is the library's own structure"; "a native helper behind `array1[\T, nat s0\]()` can return the `double[]` store when `T`'s tag is `FRR64` and the boxed store otherwise".

What the probes say. `p1_monostore.fss`: a generic `Arr[\T\]`, a monomorphic `DStore extends Arr[\RR64\]`, and `mk[\T\](x: T): Arr[\T\] = DStore()`. Refused: `Function body has type DStore, but declared return type is Arr[\T\]` (`p1_monostore.compile.out`). `p1b_typecase.fss`, the library's own idiom from `array1` (`FortressLibrary.fss:2240-2244`), `typecase w of () -> RR64 => DStore() else => BStore[\T\](x) end`: refused, `Function body has type OR(DStore,BStore[\T\]), but declared return type is Arr[\T\]` (`p1b_typecase.compile.out`). The library's `array1` passes the interpreter because nothing checks it, and would pass the checker only because both of its arms (`vector[\T,s0\]()`, `PrimitiveArray[\T,s0\]()`) stay generic in `T`; a `double[]` store is not generic in `T`, which is the whole point of it.

What "the library's own structure" would have to become. A generic object cannot have a field whose type changes with `T`; `mem: PrimitiveArray[\T,s0\]` is one type. So the shape is one monomorphic storing object per native element type (`__RR64Vector[\nat s0\]() extends Vector[\RR64,s0\]` with `mem: RR64Store`) beside the generic boxed one, and the problem moves to the factory `vector[\T extends Number, nat s0\]()`, which must return the monomorphic object for `T = RR64` inside a body the checker types with `T` abstract; the probes show it cannot. A foreign helper cannot be generic (`import java` binds static methods with fixed types, `CompilerBuiltin.fss:300-306`); a second factory name per element type is the library's pattern for `array1`/`vector` (`.fss:2251`) but does not make `vector[\RR64,16\]()` produce the store; a code-generator special case in the manner of `NamingCzar`'s hand-implemented types (`NamingCzar.java:538-553`) is a `.java` change. Every route is a `.java` rung; "no loader change" is not one of them.

The branch the design dismissed is the team's. §7's second branch, "the loader picks a `double[]` template per instantiation", is set aside as "nothing of it exists, and the instantiater's only tool is name substitution". The team's breadcrumbs point there: `compiler/optimization/Unbox.java:12-19` lists as case 1 "primitive. ZZ{8,16,32,64}, RR{32,64}, Boolean" and as case 3 "unboxed fields"; the expando stub `if (false) { // Here will go all the magic expando-stuff. }` is in the loader (`InstantiatingClassloader.java:168-172`); the JVM paper's stamping by upper bound (`MethodMapping.tex:134`) is the mechanism that would be specialised. `PLAN.md` step 5 says of this exact item "the probe on generic instantiation in the class loader comes first"; the design's probe 1 shows what the loader does today, not whether it can select, and the plan's probe was not run. Our own roadmap's A2 ("selected from the element type carried at run time. Scala's `Array[Double]` via class tags", `performance-roadmap.md:40`) is the design's default; the team's direction is the other branch. Which precedent wins is Pavol's (§2, decision A).

### Decisive 3. The second reserved fork, the library route, is closed by default in §7, against the ruling as it was applied the same evening

What the design says. §7, last paragraph: "Where microGPT's 26 operations go is not a question after the third ruling: into `CompilerLibrary` in the library's own style"; §4c and §8: the interpreter's trait layer "written in Fortress as they are in `FortressLibrary.fss`" into the compiler world.

What the ruling said and did. `POSITIONS.md:39`: "all the extensions that [microGPT] invented need to go directly to the standard library in the spirit of the standard library." The commit that applied it, `02d09a39f` (`postmortem-2026-09-19/array-work-brief.md` §1), edited `Library/FortressLibrary.fss` and `.fsi`, the interpreter's library. `PLAN.md`, stop conditions: what stops the climb includes "the library route, the interpreter's library as prelude against growing the compiler library beyond what one rung needs". `next-climb.md` §3a: "the second reserved fork — the library route … is entangled with it".

What building in the compiler world means, which the design does not say. The compiler world has no `AdditiveGroup` and no `MultiplicativeRing` (`Library/CompilerAlgebra.fss:15-25` holds `Equality` and `StandardTotalOrder` only; the scalar-extension review says the same at `:162`), no `Indexed`, no `ReadableArray`, no `Array`; `Vector[\T extends Number, nat s0\] extends { AnyVector, Array1[\T,0,s0\], AdditiveGroup[\Vector[\T,s0\]\] } excludes { AnyMultiplicativeRing }` (`.fss:2191-2193`) cannot be declared "as the team designed" without the algebra above `Number` being written a second time in that world. From then on the standard library exists in two texts: the interpreter's, which the checker never sees, and the compiler's, which it does; the map's warning "the same library written twice" (`map/README.md:127`) was about boxed and unboxed, and it applies here unchanged. The design never mentions `comprises`, `excludes` validation, `AdditiveGroup`, `CompilerAlgebra`, or the 92 tower errors the checker raises on the interpreter's library (FACTS, the interpreter's library fed to the compiler), which are what the library route costs and what the compiler-world route avoids by copying.

Severity. A reserved fork decided in one line of a worker's document, which `POSITIONS.md:48` says is a decision not made. It goes to Pavol as decision C.

### Serious 4. "Natives first" contradicts the ruling's letter, has no team precedent, and is measured to deliver nothing on the shapes the model is made of

What the design says. §7 question 3, default: "natives first, with the kernel measurement in the first array rung; generated-code unboxing as PLAN's step 4"; §4b admits "Without it, an unboxed store with boxed traffic is what `ZZ32Vector` is today; whether that alone is faster than boxed storage is not measured".

Measured now (`compiler-probes/array-design-review/java-medians.tsv`, the kernels' harness, medians of 3). Unboxed `double[]` storage read and written through a boxing accessor, arithmetic boxed as the runtime boxes it: `kdot` 0.007559 s, 8.9× primitive, against boxed storage 0.005495 s (6.4×) and primitive 0.000852 s; `kmat` 0.005552 s, 6.6×, against boxed 0.005255 s (6.2×) and primitive 0.000844 s. The store buys nothing until the traffic is unboxed; the design's §2 already showed the traffic on `ZZ32Vector` is four calls and an allocation per element (probe 2).

Where the model's traffic is. The model's data design is views: nine `PView`s over the flat vector, `RowView`, `Row3View`, `heads`, `unheads`, `plane`, `Diag`, `Transposed3` (`FlatArrays.fss:40-120`), each a `get` that indexes another array. A native product over a view reads its operand through `get`, boxed; a native `dot` over the store helps `p DOT p` and Adam's three lines (`MicroGptFlat.fss:76-79`) and nothing in `step`. So the first rung's number (`kdot` over a store) would not transfer to the program the number is for.

Precedent. The team's compiler-world operators over `ZZ32Vector` are Fortress `for` loops over boxed subscripts (`CompilerLibrary.fss:484-535`), and the team's unboxing note is a code-generator taxonomy (`Unbox.java`); the design says of natives "Not the team's precedent" (§4b) and defaults to them anyway. The route with the precedent, and the only one under which "it cannot be boxed" is true of the model, is unboxing by static type in generated code (roadmap A1, `Unbox.java` case 1), which is the largest code-generator work in the plan. Decision B.

### Serious 5. The census "no arithmetic occurs inside white brackets anywhere in the library" is false for the three storing objects the array rungs need

The claim (§6c): "Every one of these is a symbol or a literal; no arithmetic occurs inside white brackets anywhere in the library or in the two microGPT programs … So the minimal checker, symbols and literals unified by equality, covers all 81". The nat plan's census (`nat-checking-plan.md` §d) counted "an operator inside white brackets" and found 0.

The tree. `object __DefaultArray2 … mem:PrimitiveArray[\T, (s0 s1) \]` (`FortressLibrary.fss:2420`), `__DefaultMatrix` the same (`:2568`), `__DefaultArray3 … mem:PrimitiveArray[\T,(s0 (s1 s2))\]` (`:2785`). Juxtaposition is multiplication in a static argument: the interpreter evaluates it as `IntBinaryOp` with the operator `" "` (`EvalType.java:461`), and the compiler's mangler spells it `IntBinaryOp` too (`NamingCzar.java:1899-1904`). The plan's `TypeWellFormedChecker` rule rejects any `IntArg` containing `IntBinaryOp` (`nat-checking-plan.md` §c, "What it touches"), so the minimal checker refuses the library's own rank-2 and rank-3 storage. `NatReflect.fss:42-46` (`__refl'[\r+b, b+b\]`) is the fourth site and the design admits that one (§6d).

What follows. Option (c)'s storing objects are rewritten, not copied: a store trait that carries its size as a run-time field instead of `PrimitiveArray[\T,(s0 s1)\]` in the type is what §6d's "the field first" amounts to. That is a departure from the library's text and it is not named as one. The alternative, static arithmetic in the checker, is what the specification marks "not yet supported" (`basic/expressions/constant.tex:15`) and what the team's meeting refused for overloading (`internal-document.tex:167-182`); it is a decision, not a default.

### Serious 6. The array rung of §8 hides three pieces of `.java` work, so the count of gated batches is at least three, not two

The design's count: "First, the checker rung (`.scala`, full gate)… Second, the array rung (`.java`, full gate)" (§8), with a batch holding at most one such rung (§0).

Hidden in the second rung. (a) `CodeGen.java:5784-5792`: an `IntArg` in an `extends` clause throws `CompilerError("Only emitting RTTI for types right now")`; §6a says "every `object … extends Vector[\RR64,s\]` will hit" it, §8's list of the rung's contents omits it. (b) A run-time representation for a nat argument: the loader's generic RTTI class holds one `RTTI` field per static parameter, named `T0…Tn` and typed `RTTI_CONTAINER_DESC` (`InstantiatingClassloader.java:1600-1622`), and the only kind the runtime consults is `opr` (`Naming.java:95-101`, `isOpr`); nothing reads `intnat` (`Naming.java:213` is its only runtime mention). §6d says the stamped class per size is "which the loader already produces from the mangled name (probe 6)"; probe 6 stamped a generic function, which has no RTTI class; a generic object with a nat argument was never stamped (probe 4 died in the checker). (c) The store-selection mechanism of finding 2.

Hidden in the first rung. The plan's "no Java" (`nat-checking-plan.md` §d) is unproven: the object crash's frames are Java (`StaticTypeReplacer.replaceIn:102`, the replacer composition at `:95-99`), six Java files in `compiler/` handle `IntArg` (`TypeDisambiguator.java:402-433`, `TypeNormalizer.java:57`, which synthesises an `IntArg` `"0"` for array types, `StaticTypeReplacer.java`, `NamingCzar.java`, `CodeGen.java`, and the replacer's own test), and the plan's bug (i) says of the source "not identified by reading" (`:492-497`). The design's shadow session (§6e) is the right instrument; its outcome decides whether the checker rung is Scala-only.

Consequence for cost. Checker (`.scala`), then code generator and runtime for nat arguments (`.java`), then store class, trait and factory (`.java`), each in its own batch under the one-Java-rung rule, before `library_tests/Vector1.fss` can pass. Three gated batches at the least; the design says two.

### Serious 7. The unconstrained-nat default is unsafe and does not address what the export checker will actually meet

The design (§7 question 5, default): "default an unconstrained nat to `0`", keeping the library's text at `.fsi:1496-1523`.

What those six declarations are. The api declares `opr +[\T extends Number, nat n, nat m\](me: Vector[\T,n\], other: Vector[\T,n\])` and `-` with a dead `m` (`.fsi:1496-1503`), and the vector products with dead `m, p` (`.fsi:1508-1523`). The component's `+` and `-` are methods of `trait Vector[\T extends Number, nat s0\]` with one nat (`.fss:2195-2197`), and its products carry one nat `n` (`.fss:2264-2276`). So the api and the component disagree in the number of static parameters, not merely in whether one is constrained. `ExportChecker.equalIntExprs` is `false` with the comment "Not implemented!" (`ExportChecker.scala:645-648`); once it compares, what it compares is a shape mismatch that default-0 at call sites does not touch. The interpreter never runs the export check, which is why the mismatch has lived since 2008.

Why default-0 is the wrong side. A nat left unconstrained by inference and then used as a value in the body (`n.asZZ32`, the spec's "any context a variable of type N32 can appear", `trait-parameters.tex:82-86`) would compute with 0 and no error; the interpreter's analogue, `BottomType` for an unbound parameter (`EvaluatorBase.java:238-240`), errors at first use. The safe rule is the interpreter's: an unsolved nat is an error when it reaches a type, a name or a value, and nothing when it reaches nothing. Whether the six api lines are then edited (a sealed-tree library edit) or the export checker is taught to tolerate a dead parameter is decision E.

### Serious 8. The design gives no stop condition, and the ones `PLAN.md` has do not cover the failures this plan can meet

`PLAN.md`'s stop conditions are a design fork, "a rung's gate red twice after one repair", disk, and a permission wall. None of them fires when the shadow session finds the nat confusion in Java rather than Scala, when the runtime needs a new RTTI kind, when the checker refuses the store selection (finding 2), or when the model cannot be typed without a diff to its text (finding 1). Each of those is a fork returning to Pavol, and the design should say so before the first rung, not after; §7 of this review names five.

### Minor 9. The "unprobed link" of §4c is already proven by the tree, and the rung's first experiment is misdirected

§4c: "Unprobed: that a non-generic trait extending an instantiated generic supertype with a hand-written implementation class compiles and links; it is the first thing the array rung of §8 finds out." `FZZ32 extends fortress.CompilerBuiltin.ZZ32.DefaultTraitMethods implements fortress.CompilerBuiltin.ZZ32` (`FZZ32.java:14-15`) under `trait ZZ32 extends { Number, Equality[\ZZ32\], StandardTotalOrder[\ZZ32\] }` (`CompilerBuiltin.fsi:210`), and `FJavaString` under `trait JavaString extends String extends StandardTotalOrder[\String\]` (`:25, :52`), are that exact shape and pass the gate every day. Probe `p2_nongeneric.fss` adds the Fortress-object half, reached through the stamped generic `get`: compiles, runs, `p2 5.0`. The first thing the rung should find out is finding 2's question, not this one.

### Minor 10. "The one recorded implementation stance" is a conditional inside a question

§6b reads `internal-document.tex:199-208` as recording "'don't instantiate'" as the team's stance. The text asks whether `f(x: ZZ32)` beside `f(x: T[\0\])` is allowed "if our implementation strategy for nat parameters is 'don't instantiate'?" and answers "Allowed"; two items earlier it allows `T[\1\]` beside `T[\0\]` "now that nat parameters are all instantiated" (`:183-198`). The record is a question under a hypothesis, and the compiled encoding the team then built carries nats by name into instantiations (`MethodMapping.tex:142`, `NamingCzar.java:1891-1911`), which is instantiation. Nothing in the design turns on it, but it is cited as intent.

### Minor 11. What the specification's own breadcrumb on unboxed arrays says, which the design does not cite

Jan's note #82 on unboxed types (`appendices/future.tex:42`): "Do we want to permit arrays of fixed bounds to be unboxed? No. Unboxed arrays are a different other thing. Among other things, an array of fixed bounds may be a segment of another array." That is the team saying that a sized array is a structure (possibly a view) over a linear store and that the store is the thing to unbox, which is the shape of option (c) and of `HeapSequence` (`arrays-distributed.tex:18-22`). It supports the design's separation of sized traits from a native store; it should be in §5.

### Minor 12. Two numbers in §3 are the review's, not the tree's

§3 counts "38 declarations, of which 5 are aliases … 26 are new operations … and 9 are new structures" at `a92569f83`, and says the working copy is "under uncommitted repair"; the brief records that the repair landed as `8590d7a9e`, dropping seven declarations to the library (`array-work-brief.md` §3). The design's counts are of the file before that commit; the current `FlatArrays.fsi` (56 lines) has 31 declarations. Nothing turns on it; the inventory should be re-dated when the design is revised.

## 1b. The design's nat rungs against `reviews/nat-checking-plan.md`

The two documents are the same shape. The plan (2026-09-16) states "nothing was run beyond the probes that already exist" (`nat-checking-plan.md:22-23`); the design (2026-09-19) ran eight probe programs that reproduce symptoms (§9) and compiled no line of any fix. The plan's §f names the directory its prototype would live in, `explorations/perf-probes/nat/`; it does not exist. Its sibling, `template-checking-plan.md` (2026-09-16, "Nothing was run for this document", `:4`), was prototyped the same day under a classpath shadow, 31 changed lines, and gated green with the shadow overlaid (FACTS, the template-checking fix; `perf-probes/template-check/33-testfast.out`, `34-testsystem.out`). So the project has the instrument and the precedent for proving a checker plan before a rung, and the nat plan has not been put through it in four days; the design's §6e re-specifies the same shadow session rather than reporting its result.

Where the design's nat rungs agree with the plan, and simply restate it: the minimal design, symbols and literals unified by equality, no arithmetic (plan §c, design §6b-§6c); the reserved-name `IntRef` for the prototype and the AST node for the real edit (plan §c "Representation", design §6e); the unconstrained nat defaulted to 0 with deletion posed as Pavol's question (plan §c, design §7 question 5); the five nat compiler tests kept green (plan §d, design §6e); shadow first, then the tree (plan §f, design §6e); and the plan's two named bugs outside the checker, the object crash and the `IntArg` in `extends` (plan §c "Two known bugs", design §6a).

Where the design adds to the plan. Probe 4 reduces the object crash to a four-line object with no `extends` and no use of the nat, so it sits on the path of the very first sized declaration, where the plan had it one step later (`pNat2.fss`). Probe 6 finds a defect the plan does not know: a nat in value position types as an integer literal, which the plan lists under "Not touched" as already right (`KindEnv.scala:64-70`), and then compiles to `getstatic NatVal$n.ONLY` and dies at run time with `NoClassDefFoundError`; the plan's claim is true of the checker and false of the run. Probe 6 also shows the mangler carrying nat literals into instantiation names, which the plan's §g asserts from `NamingCzar.java` and does not run.

Where the two contradict each other, or share one blind spot. (1) Code generation: the plan's §g closes with "if the compiled path ever wants `Vector[\T, n+1\]` the question is codegen's, not the checker's" and its §d says the change "does not by itself flip any catalogued test"; the design folds the code-generator work into the array rung without listing it (finding 6). Neither document owns the `.java` half that the first sized object needs. (2) Applications: the plan's gate step 5 expects `FlatArrays.fss` and `FlatArrays2.fss` to "pass the type checker with no nat-related error" once the minimal design lands (`:586-589`); finding 1 says `FlatArrays.fss:62` cannot, because `reflect(nr)` is statically a `NatParam` and `viewN` takes `N[\r\]`. The plan's §d "covers all 81 and all 30" and the design's §6c "covers all 81 and all of microGPT's" both count declarations; the plan's one worked call site (§b) is `unbox(Box[\3\](7))`, a literal argument, the easy case; neither document types an application whose argument was built at run time, which is every array in the model. (3) The census: the plan's §d table gives 0 for arithmetic inside white brackets in `FortressLibrary.fss` and the design repeats it; `:2420`, `:2568` and `:2785` are arithmetic (finding 5), so the plan's `TypeWellFormedChecker` rule refuses three library objects the design's option (c) needs. (4) The export checker: the plan changes `equalIntExprs` to structural equality (§c "What it touches"); the design says every exported signature mentioning `Vector[\T,n\]` fails today and passes after; neither notices that the six api declarations at `.fsi:1496-1523` do not match the component's shape (finding 7), so the first run of the fixed export checker over the library reports them.

What has been proven by a probe, by either. The symptoms, three times over: probe 11 of the prelude report (`pNat1.fss`, `pNat2.fss`, 2026-09-16), the design's probes 3 to 6 (2026-09-19), and this review's probes for the questions beside the checker (2026-09-20). ## 2. The seven questions, answered by finding

**Q1, finish or redesign.** It finishes the specification's shape: the sized family `Array k[T, 0, n0, …]` over a generic array (`aggregate.tex:130-136`), both factories (`arrays-distributed.tex:42-48`), a linear store under a structure (`:18-22`, `future.tex:42`), sizes in types (`nutshell.tex:85-86`), and the checker doing what the interpreter's `unifyStaticArg` does (`IntNat.java:127-147`). It departs in six places: the storing objects are rewritten, not copied (finding 5, unnamed); the run-time-sized factory's dynamic half, that an `Array[\RR64,ZZ32\]` is a `Vector` underneath, is dropped (§6d, named as "the one library pattern the compiled world does not reproduce", but its consequence for the model is not, finding 1); natives in place of the team's generated-code unboxing (finding 4, half named); store selection by run-time tag, our roadmap's A2, in place of the team's `Unbox`/expando direction (finding 2, unnamed); a third library text in the compiler world in place of the spec's Core/Standard split and the ruling's "the standard library" (finding 3, unnamed); and the compiler world's own precedent, the unsized rank-polymorphic `ZZ32Vector`, set aside (§4b, named).

**Q2, the defaults.** Finding 2 (store selection: refuted by probe, alternatives all `.java`, precedent points the other way), finding 4 (loops: measured to buy nothing on the model's shapes, no precedent), finding 7 (unconstrained nats: unsafe side, and the real problem is an api/component mismatch). The smaller defaults: the nat also as a run-time field is right and forced (finding 5); the run-time-sized factory returning the unsized `Array` is what breaks the model (finding 1); one shadow session before the checker edit is right and should widen to five probes (§7 below). Which are Pavol's: all three large ones and the run-time-size one; the shadow session is the worker's.

**Q3, the nat plan.** The Scala side is consistent with itself: `staticInstantiation` already substitutes `IntRef` (`STypesUtil.scala:670`), `staticArgsMatchStaticParams` already accepts an `IntArg` for `KindNat` (`:742-747, 767-772`), self-types are built with `IntArg(IntRef)` for nat kinds (`:338-339, 352-353`), and the equality hole is one line (`TypeAnalyzer.scala:332`). Unsound or unproven: the crash is in Java replacer composition (finding 6); "no arithmetic" refuses three library objects (finding 5); the export checker meets an arity mismatch (finding 7); nothing in the plan or the design touches code generation or the runtime for a nat argument, which the first sized object needs (finding 6). Smallest rung that proves or refutes it: the shadow session with `NatInfer1`, `NatObject1`, `NatValue1`, `XXXNatMismatch1` as the design lists them, plus `NatExtends1` (`object V[\nat n\]() extends S[\n\] end` instantiated as `V[\3\]()`, through code generation and a run) and `NatStore1` (a generic factory returning a monomorphic object, the probe `p1_monostore.fss` as it stands). Dead ends: the `TypeArg`/`IntArg` confusion pervasive in the Java front end; a nat needing a runtime RTTI kind; the checker refusing the library's own storing objects with no arithmetic rule the spec permits.

**Q4, `double[]` under the generic trait.** Stamping cannot produce a `[D` field; the design's probe 1 settles that and the map already said it (`map/README.md:43`). The `ZZ32Vector` precedent proves that a hand-written class can implement a trait's `DefaultTraitMethods`, be a `NamingCzar` special type, and reach Java natives through `import java`, and `FZZ32` proves it under instantiated generic supertraits (finding 9). It does not prove unboxed traffic (probe 2, and `KDotStore`), per-instantiation representation, or anything about nats. The route the design proposes for selecting the store is refused by the checker (finding 2); the route that could give unboxed storage per element type under one generic trait is the loader/code-generator one, which `PLAN.md` step 5 says to probe first and which the design dismissed unprobed.

**Q5, the two preludes.** The compiler world, said in §4c and §7's last paragraph, and no more than said: what it costs (a second algebra, ~1,200 lines of array text, two library texts from then on, the interpreter's copy never checked) and what it forecloses (the library route, a reserved fork) are absent (finding 3).

**Q6, the 26 operations and the three kernels.** As written, none is reachable from the model's text and none of the kernels compiles, for the reason of finding 1; the first failure is `FlatArrays.fss:62`, the second `MicroGptFlat.fss:27`. On values that are statically sized (literal sizes, `vector[\RR64,16\]()`), the design serves: the 14 elementwise maps (declared over `Array[\RR64,I\]`, bodies boxed per element under the question-3 default), the library's products and `Diag`, `transpose` of a `Matrix`. It cannot serve as written: `view`, `row`, `row3`, `heads`, `unheads`, `plane` (run-time shapes through `reflect`), `rows` in all four shapes (takes `Matrix`, returns `Array`, so one `rows` cannot feed the next), `gather`, `pick`, `onehot` where the argument is a view's result, and the batched product on `heads`' results. The kernels: `kdot` and `kmat` need `vec`/`mat` to return sized types or `DOT`/juxtaposition to be declared over `Array`; `krows` needs both and `rows`.

**Q7, order and stop.** The first gated rung should not be the checker rung until a shadow rung with no gate has answered five questions (§7 below); the checker rung is right as the first gated rung only if the shadow says the crash is in Scala. The stop conditions are in §7.

## 3. Decisions that are Pavol's

Each as what we do, what it changes, the alternatives, and a labelled default that can be accepted without reading the argument; the argument is in §1.

### Decision A. What puts `double[]` under the library's sized traits

What we do: choose the mechanism by which `Vector[\RR64,16\]` gets a `double[]` store while `Vector[\T,16\]` for other `T` gets a boxed one.

What it changes: which `.java` rung the array work needs first, and whether the library's generic factories keep their text.

Alternatives. (A1) The design's default, a factory selecting by run-time type tag under one generic storing object: refused by the checker (finding 2); to keep it, a code-generator special case for the factory, a `.java` rung with no team precedent. (A2) The team's direction: the code generator and loader give a stamped `PrimitiveArray⟦FRR64,n⟧` a `double[]` field, `Unbox.java` case 1 plus the expando stub; the library's text stays as it is; the largest change, in the loader and `CodeGen`, and `PLAN.md` step 5's own first probe. (A3) Monomorphic storing objects per native element type (`__RR64Vector[\nat s0\]`) beside the generic boxed one, with a monomorphic factory name per element type in the manner of `array1`/`vector` (`.fss:2251`); typed cleanly, no code-generator change, but the model then names `rr64vector`, not `vector`, and the generic `Vector[\T,s0\]` is boxed for every `T` including `RR64` unless the user calls the right factory.

Default: probe A2 first (one shadow experiment, `next-climb.md` §3b's first item), because it is the team's breadcrumb and `PLAN.md`'s order; A3 is the fallback that needs no `.java`; A1 is closed by the probe unless Pavol wants the special case.

### Decision B. Where loops run unboxed

What we do: choose between Java natives per whole-array operation and unboxing `RR64` by static type in generated code.

What it changes: whether the ruling "it cannot be boxed" holds for the model (views, lambdas, `rows`) or only for whole-array operations on stores; and the size of the code-generator work.

Alternatives. (B1) Natives first, the design's default: measured at 8.9×/6.6× primitive on the model's shapes (finding 4), the same as boxed storage; helps `p DOT p` and Adam only; no precedent. (B2) Unboxing by static type first (roadmap A1, `Unbox.java` case 1): `D` locals, `daload`/`dastore`, `dadd`/`dmul` wherever the checker's type is `RR64`; the library's own Fortress loops (`fill`, `map`, `Vector.dot`) become unboxed without natives; every rung `.java` under the full gate; the team's precedent. (B3) Both, in the order B2 for element access and arithmetic, natives only where a whole-array kernel beats the JIT (a measured residue, not a default).

Default: B2, because it is the only alternative under which the ruling is true of the model, and the kernel measurement in the array rung is then `kdot` in Fortress against `KDotPrim`, not against a native.

### Decision C. Which library the array layer and the model's operations go into

What we do: choose between growing the compiler world (`CompilerLibrary`, `CompilerBuiltin`, `CompilerAlgebra`) with a copy of the array layer and the algebra above `Number`, and making the interpreter's library (`FortressLibrary`) the prelude the compiler checks.

What it changes: whether the standard library exists once or twice from here on; whether yesterday's commit to `FortressLibrary.fss` and this design's rungs land in the same file; the 92 tower errors and 108 native bindings of the library route (FACTS) against the second copy of about 1,200 lines plus the algebra.

Alternatives. (C1) The compiler world, the design's default. (C2) The library route, `PLAN.md`'s reserved fork: the interpreter's library becomes the checked prelude; the 92 errors are classified first (`next-climb.md` §3b's last item, "one probe"). (C3) One text, two bindings: the array layer and algebra written once in `Library/` in Fortress, with the native bindings the only per-world file (`NativeArray.fss` with `builtinPrimitive` for the interpreter, a compiler-world twin with `import java`); whether the two worlds can share a prelude file is unprobed.

Default: none can be labelled; this is the reserved fork and it is Pavol's. The cheapest fact before deciding is the classification of the 92 errors.

### Decision D. The model's static types

What we do: decide how `MicroGptFlat.fss` and `FlatArrays.fss` are typed for a static checker, given that today their values are statically unsized and their operations are declared on sized types (finding 1).

What it changes: the model's text, or the vocabulary's declarations, or the compile path.

Alternatives. (D1) The model carries sizes in its types as the specification intends (`makeVector`, `trait-parameters.tex:94-97`): `step` and its helpers become generic in `nat` parameters, the hyperparameters become static arguments, and every `Array[\RR64,…\]` in the model becomes a `Vector`/`Matrix`/`Array3` with nats; the ruling of `POSITIONS.md:38` requires the diff to be shown before it is built. (D2) The vocabulary is declared over the unsized `Array[\RR64,I\]` and the products the model uses (`DOT`, juxtaposition, `rows`, `gather`, `diag`) are added over `Array` beside the library's sized ones; the model's text is unchanged; the sized products and the shape check are then not what the model exercises, which is the sidestep the second ruling refuses. (D3) A compile-path `reflect`: a native that asks the loader for `N⟦n⟧` by name at run time, which the team's own comment asks for ("we ought to build it in", `NatReflect.fss:39`); it keeps the model's dynamic dispatch but does not make the static types sized, so it does not by itself pass the checker; it is what D1 needs for the run-time-sized parameter vector.

Default: D1 with D3, because both rulings are honoured only there; D2 is the honest alternative if the model's text is to stay byte-identical. The first thing to show Pavol is the D1 diff of `rmsn` and `step`'s signature, not a rung.

### Decision E. The six api declarations with dead nats

What we do: decide what the checker does with `nat m` and `nat p` in `.fsi:1496-1523`, which the component does not have.

What it changes: whether the checker rung touches `Library/` (six api lines) and what an unsolved nat means anywhere.

Alternatives. (E1) Default to 0, the design's: unsafe when the parameter is later used as a value (finding 7), and blind to the api/component shape mismatch. (E2) Unsolved is an error when the nat reaches a type, a name or a value, nothing otherwise (the interpreter's `BottomType` rule); the six declarations pass because their dead parameters reach nothing; the export checker compares by name and reports the extra parameter. (E3) Delete the dead parameters from the api, a sealed-tree library edit flagged at commit, and the export checker then agrees.

Default: E2 in the checker, E3 as the library edit shown to Pavol as a six-line diff; not E1.

### Decision F. What the first gated rung is, and what stops the climb

What we do: run a shadow rung with no gate before the checker rung, answering: where the `VarType`/`IntExpr` crash comes from; what the export checker reports on the six declarations; whether a generic object with a nat argument in `extends` can be stamped and run (`NatExtends1`); whether the store can be selected under the generic factory in any typed way (`NatStore1`, decision A); and whether the loader can be made to select a representation (decision A2). Then the checker rung.

Stop conditions to adopt, each returning to Pavol with the capture: the crash's fix is in a Java front-end class, so the plan's "no Java" and its size are void; a nat argument needs a new RTTI kind in `runtimeSystem/`; the checker refuses every typed route to the store (A1 and A3 both fail); the model cannot be typed without a diff to its lines (decision D not taken); the kernel compiled over the store lands at the `KDotStore` number rather than the `KDotPrim` number, which means the traffic is still boxed.

## 4. Verdict

Unsound as written. The two rulings stand and the shape the design draws from them, sized traits over a native linear store with the checker doing what `unifyStaticArg` does, is the specification's own shape and has the team's breadcrumbs behind it (`future.tex:42`, `arrays-distributed.tex:18-22`, `MethodMapping.tex:142`). But three load-bearing claims fail: the plan cannot compile the program it is measured by, because it keeps the model's unsized static types and the checker resolves statically (finding 1); its default mechanism for putting `double[]` under the generic trait is refused by the checker, and the branch it dismissed is the team's (finding 2); and it closes the second reserved fork in one line (finding 3). It becomes sound only under conditions Pavol sets: decision D taken and its diff shown; decision A taken after the loader probe `PLAN.md` step 5 already orders; decision C taken as the fork it is; decision B taken with the `KDotStore` measurement in view; and the shadow rung and stop conditions of decision F adopted before the first gated rung.
