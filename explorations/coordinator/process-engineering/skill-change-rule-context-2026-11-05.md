# Draft 1: who decides changes to the coordinator skill

Times are UTC. The coordinator is the session that plans the work, briefs the workers and talks to you; its skill is the instructions it loads at every start. The drafts page shows you proposed skill wording for comment.

## What the draft changes

Two rules of 17 October stand. A change to how you and the coordinator work together is proposed to you in words and made after your yes. That covers what it says to you, the asks (questions to you, one decision each), the held list (your rules from chat that wait for the skill) and the review list (decisions taken without you, kept for your later look). The coordinator makes every other change itself and tells you when it lands: what changed, why, and what risk to watch.

Draft 1 puts every added, changed or removed rule of the coordinator skill under your yes to its exact words, on the drafts page, with no default (no option that stands on your silence). The other skills keep the old way.

## What triggered it

On 19 October a post-mortem reviewed three days in which the session had run on a smaller model unnoticed. It found the rule of 17 October incomplete: the first kind left out who decides what, and the second kind did not require the draft and the cold read, an independent agent's fresh reading of the new text. Its question to you was never answered, and stayed open as item 4 of the closing docket of 3 November. Meanwhile the coordinator promised on 23 October, 00:32, to change nothing in its skill without asking you, and on 1 November, 23:54, called this "your rule"; no message of yours says so. The draft followed your message of 3 November, 18:28, which rejected "defaults" for the eight working rules on the docket. So yes, it reverses your rule of 17 October.

## The burden, measured

From 17 October to 3 November, 28 commits changed the coordinator skill. Three went through the drafts page, on 1 November at 23:42 and on 2 November at 08:21 and 22:03. They carried 33 of the 84 changes that the skill's list of sources gives for the period. The other 25 commits, with 51 changes, landed outside it. Most followed a yes or an instruction of yours in chat; the rest were the coordinator's own fixes, reported on landing.

Even the page did not hold every change to your yes: the first landing took the items that you had not commented on as accepted. In the third, the coordinator settled 16 of 20 items, after you asked at 22:0x on 2 November that the review finish without you.

Version 6 of the page held about 91,000 characters in 33 items, about 2,800 an item: an hour of reading. Version 8 grew to 120,000; version 9 holds about 30,000 for eight drafts. You commented from 22:19 to 23:34 on 1 November, then from 05:59 to 07:38 (17,500 characters dictated) and 20:50 to 21:34 on 2 November, then handed the review over.

## Three ways

(a) The draft as written. For each change, you read an item of about 3,000 characters and give your yes. At the period's rate of five changes a day, that is some 15,000 characters a day. The risk: on 1 to 3 November this load ended in a review handed back, and so in changes nobody read.

(b) The rule of 17 October. Only a change to how you two work together waits for you. Every other change lands after a cold read, and you read its exact words in a report. The risk: the coordinator judges which kind a change is, and the post-mortem found that list too narrow, since it leaves out who decides what.

(c) The middle way. Every change lands after a cold read; the coordinator's next message gives its old and new words, a few hundred characters, and you can reverse it. Your yes comes first only for a change to your role, the asks, the held list, the review list or the register, which is the way the coordinator writes to you. When in doubt, the change waits. The risk: the coordinator still sorts the changes, and you must read the reports to catch drift.

## Where your words point

Your words of 17 October keep the coordinator's initiative and ask for detail as changes happen: that is (b) or (c). Your words of 31 October ask to be told, not asked: the same. Read alone, your words of 3 November point to (a), since they name the whole skill. But their reason is your cooperation and the exact wording. Under (c), what governs your cooperation waits for your yes, and you see every other change word for word. Together, the three point to (c), close to the post-mortem's own proposal of 19 October.

Recommendation: take (c), and keep your yes before landing for the rules on your role, the asks, the held list, the review list and the register.
