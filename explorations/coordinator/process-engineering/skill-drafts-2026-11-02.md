## What happened

A review of the decisions on record under the rule of the team's later word (`explorations/reviews/walk-leaning-decisions.md`) found that P-156's reason held a clause that the landing of climb batch 24 made false, and that its text did not name the later team text that it departs from. The coordinator put these two findings to him as an ask, with options to keep or to replace the decision. The decision stood, and the record already said why: its history holds his words and the earlier corrections. The right handling was a cut of the stale clause, committed at 18:04 (`4ee3eb38c`), and one line to him.

The coordinator's account, which he accepted: the skill does not contradict itself; it has a gap. It says to search the record and act where the record settles the question, and to put a decision already taken as an item for review, never as a hold. It says nothing on a review's findings before they reach him. Inside that gap, the coordinator made two errors of its own:

- Its brief to the review worker asked for every reopened item "ask-ready", with options and a default. So the worker shaped a reason correction as a decision.
- It told him that the position did not say why, from the entry's text alone, without reading the entry's history.

The three drafts below answer the gap and the two errors, one each.

**What he meant.** The judgement between a worker's findings and what reaches him is the coordinator's own step. A worker asked for issues returns every kind of finding at one weight. Each kind already has its handling in his rules: a change of his decision is his to make; a reason that the tree made false is brought in line without him (10-31); a correction of an entry's ground that changes no decision goes to the history (10-29); a confirmation needs no more than a line. Only the first needs him. Without the sort, a correction that needs nobody's approval reaches him as a choice, and he decides again what he already decided.

**New text** (`SKILL.md`, "The curator's role and decisions", a new bullet after the bullet "If a decision that a worker wrote in one line of its report is consequential, ..."):

> - A worker asked for issues returns its findings at one weight, so your own judgement sorts them by what each needs before any reaches the curator or becomes the next brief. Only a finding that would change a decision of the curator's becomes an ask. A reason that is no longer true is cut, since bringing the record in line with reality needs no approval. A correction of an entry's ground that changes no decision goes to the entry's history, since it changes nothing that a reader does. A finding that confirms a decision takes one line.

**The old text at that place** (`SKILL.md`, lines 111 and 112; the new bullet replaces nothing):

> - Before you ask the curator anything, or measure anything, search the record (below). If it settles the question, act on it.
> - If a decision that a worker wrote in one line of its report is consequential, it is not made until the curator sees it. Bring it by one of the three ways.

Version 5 of the drafts page redrafted rule 12 for "The standing goal": "A worker's result becomes the brief of the next step only after your own judgement, against the goal and the record. A worker answers what it was asked, to the letter, and returns trivial points beside those that matter." This draft takes its place. It keeps that text's point, the judgement before a result becomes the next brief, and adds what each kind of finding needs. It sits beside the search rule and the rule on a worker's decisions, since all three govern what reaches him.

**What it alters.** Today a review's findings reach him, or the next brief, as the worker returned them. With this bullet, the coordinator sorts them first. A change of his decision comes to him as an ask. A stale reason is cut, a correction of an entry's ground goes to its history, and a confirmation is one line, all without an ask. The bullet also brings into the skill his rules of 10-31 and 10-29. The skill does not state them today: the 10-31 rule stands only in the record-landing workflow's briefs (`explorations/coordinator/tools/record-landing/judge.md` and `entries.md`), and `references/record.md`'s third question for each sentence of an entry ("Does it tell how the fact came about: a date, a batch, a correction? ## Draft 3: the search takes in an entry's history

**What happened.** The coordinator said that the position did not say why it departs from the later texts. It had read the entry's text only. The history holds his "Option one is OK" of 10-23, his answers to two questions of climb batch 21, and the correction of 10-31 that named the departure (the coordinator's answer of 18:07:02).

**What he meant.** An entry's reasons are in its text and its history together. The record keeps an entry short on purpose: his dictated words and the corrections of its grounds go to the history, by `references/record.md` and by his rule of 10-29. So a claim about an entry's reasons that rests on its text alone misreads the record.

**New text** (`SKILL.md`, "The curator's role and decisions", one sentence added at the end of the search bullet):

> - Before you ask the curator anything, or measure anything, search the record (below). If it settles the question, act on it. An entry's history is part of that search, and is read before any claim about the entry's reasons, since the history keeps the ground and the curator's words that the entry's text leaves out.

**The old text at that place** (`SKILL.md`, line 111):

> - Before you ask the curator anything, or measure anything, search the record (below). If it settles the question, act on it.

**What it alters.** Before the coordinator says why a decision was taken, or that it lacks a reason, it prints the entry's history (`facts-extract.sh 'history:P-NN'`) as well as its text. This costs one read for each entry in question. A history can be long; the read is bounded to the entries that the claim names.
