## 11. Parallel reductions after the switch-over

**Will the `SUM`s and `DOT`s run in parallel?** "Those over more than 16 elements, yes. By reading, almost none of microGPT's do. After the switch-over a compiled call's arguments fork where the parallelism analyzer marks them, and the one library's range joins a reduction's two halves as one call's arguments. So a reduction over more than 16 elements forks its halves (F-350; P-175). The rung you propose is already built: making reductions fork is P-175, your decision, and 'reductions left in sequence' was declined there. But the range stops splitting at 16 elements (`grainSize`, `Library/RangeInternals.fss:20`), and microGPT's vectors are 16, 16, 4 and 4 wide (`MicroGptFlat.fss:17`). So a `SUM`, `DOT` or `BIG MAX` over one of them runs in sequence, and only `vocabSize` 27 splits once, into 16 and 11 (F-350; `reviews/batch-30-review.md`, section 2). That is by reading, not measured. What will fork is elsewhere. The library's matrix product splits down to single elements (`Library/FortressLibrary.fss:3129-3187`; F-356), and microGPT's products, as in `x4 lm^T`, are the library's. Strided ranges split to single elements too (`StridedFullParScalarRange`, batch-30 review). The model's row loops are parallel `for`s (`FlatArrays.fss:145-163`)."

**Will four threads make a step faster?** "On the evidence, no: slower. A Java model of microGPT's step in the library's fork/join shape, unboxed, with plain loops at the leaves, takes 19 to 32 times the plain step on one thread, 55 times on two and 80 times on four. Even with the matrix product stopped at blocks of 16, it is 1.4 to 4.8 times slower (F-356). Threads pay off only from about a 128 by 128 product with blocks of 64, or a dot product of about 260,000 elements; microGPT's products are 16 by 16 to 64 by 16. This is a floor for compiled Fortress, not a measurement of it. It was measured once, on a shared machine of 4 CPUs, and compiled Fortress adds boxing and checks on top. The direction is clear: at these sizes, forking costs more than it saves."

**What we compare a step with:** "The pure-Java microGPT, `java-baseline/`, which P-7 names. It takes about 0.10 ms a step at batch 1 without the Adam update and 0.113 ms with it (F-355). The 0.10 ms is the median of 10 fresh JVMs, 100.8 to 104.0 µs, from one session on a shared 4-CPU machine: good to about ±5 % on that machine, not across machines. It is the careful scalar program, not the fastest Java. The Vector API in the products gives 75 µs. Whether the target is the scalar program or the fastest Java is your open review-queue item 524. The default as landed is the scalar program. For scale, NumPy took 0.20 ms and pure Python 132 ms in the same session. Our only compiled timing so far is one loop, about 100 times slower than Java (F-358), an order of magnitude and no more."

That tells us whether forking costs on the real program what the model predicts. (2) Then give the matrix product a grain, stopping its split at blocks rather than single elements. F-356 makes this the largest part of the fork cost, and it follows your own rule in P-175, 'you need the right forking constants'. The strided range can get the same treatment.
