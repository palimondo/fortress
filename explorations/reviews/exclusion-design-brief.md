# The exclusion fork: a design brief for the route decision

## 1. The decision in plain words

- A **generic** is a type with a parameter, `List[\T\]`; an **instantiation** fills the parameter, `List[\ZZ\]`. The rule at issue, the designers' **multiple instantiation exclusion**, says no type may be two different instantiations of one generic at once: nothing may be both a `List[\String\]` and a `List[\ZZ\]` (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 1`).
- The designers adopted it in 2010-2011 to make one shortcut safe. Fortress lets a generic function have a special version for one instantiation, `tail[\X\](x: List[\X\])` beside `tail(x: List[\ZZ\])`, and compiled code picks between them at run time by the value's type. If one value could be both instantiations, the special version could run for a caller who was promised the other type, and the program would go wrong with no type error. The rule forbids such a value, and the checker's return type rule trusts that (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 1-2`).
- The collision: the library's numbers nest. `ZZ32` is inside `ZZ64`, inside `ZZ`, inside `QQ`, inside `RR64`, inside `Number`, and each level says "I am `Equality` at my own type" (and ordering, min, max). So one `ZZ32` is `Equality` at five instantiations at once, which is exactly what the rule forbids. 61 of the checker's 103 errors on the interpreter's library are this, at 23 declarations (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 5`).
- Why it is on the critical path: one library, the interpreter's, is to become the library the compiler checks (`POSITIONS.md:46`). The checker refuses its tower. Rung P relaxed the check alone and found that a program then type-checks and dies at run time, so the relaxation by itself is no fix (`REPORT.md`, "The stop"). One of three things has to give: the rule, the tower's nesting, or the shortcut.
- What every route shares: the `DistributesOver` markers go, since they are read nowhere; B and C both need the call-site fix; and the two code-generator defects and the overloading checker's blindness to symbolic operators are holes under every route (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 2c`; `patents-forest-rule.md § 3`; `scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 4-5`).
- Route A, keep the rule and flatten the tower to the compiler prelude's shape: no checker work, 14 library headers rewritten, 367 places that rely on the nesting, the interpreter taught coercion (150-200 lines); the error count goes 103 to 22 (`price-keep-the-rule.md`, the short answer).
- Route B, drop the rule, make the code generator take a generic's instantiation from the call site (43 lines), and forbid the shortcut by enforcing the specification's overload sentence: 179 library pairs and 12 tests refused under the narrower reading; the count goes 103 to 24 (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md`, the answer; `patents-forest-rule.md § 4`).
- Route C, the forest rule of the group's 2012 patents: keep the rule for ordinary generics and let a self-typed trait be carried at several instantiations when they line up by subtyping; about 30 checker lines measured, 40 to 70 derived, dispatch changes unsized, seven library declarations completed; the count goes 103 to 37 (`patents-forest-rule.md § 4`, the routes).
- A reader who stops here is choosing between: making the library's numbers look like Java's (A); making the compiler follow the specification's model and giving up the specialisation shortcut (B); or building the type group's unpublished refinement that keeps everything (C).

## 2. The nine steps, compressed

### 2.1 The refresher

- A **self-typed trait** takes its own subject as the parameter: `trait Equality[\T extends Equality[\T\]\]`, so `ZZ32 extends Equality[\ZZ32\]` says "a ZZ32 can be compared with a ZZ32". The algebra traits (`Equality`, the orders, `StandardMin`/`Max`/`MinMax`, `Integral`) all have this shape (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 5`).
- **Dispatch** is choosing at run time which declaration of an overloaded name runs. A **static argument** is the type the checker fills a generic's parameter with at one call; the **call site** is that place in the program. The **return type rule** says a more specific overload's result must fit the less specific one's (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 1`; `multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md`, terms).
- **Coercion** is a declared conversion the compiler inserts when no declaration applies without it, `ZZ64` declaring `coerce(z: ZZ32)`; the specification uses it between machine widths and resolves it statically (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 3-4`).

### 2.2 What each path does today, measured

- Compiled: refuses the double membership with four errors; accepts the specialised overload and dispatches it correctly; and its return type rule solves for one instantiation only, which is sound only under the rule (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 2`). With the check relaxed, `ProbeMIEPick` links and dies with `IncompatibleClassChangeError` under every placement (`REPORT.md`, "Measured").
- Compiled, where a generic gets its instantiation: at the call site when nothing can be dispatched, from the run-time value when it can, and the value records one instantiation per generic, the last listed (`dispatch-and-route-c.md § 1`). Two code-generator defects were found beside this, neither involving double membership: a cast to an uninstantiated return type, and `ZZ32` spelled two ways (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 4`).
- Interpreter: accepts the double membership, picks the first-listed instantiation from the run-time value, and can fail at the next typed binding; refuses the Naden-shaped specialisation at load ("non-ground types need exclusion"); on the tower the value's own level wins (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 3`).
- Interpreter, on overload sets: its check depends on the names of static parameters, refusing `ScopeArms` (one generic names `Y`) and loading `ScopeArmsX` (both name `X`); on the latter it answers from the run-time value where the compiled path answers from the call site (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 6`).
- So the two paths took opposite sides of the trade, and either side alone is sound; rung P's relaxation combined the two unsound halves (`REPORT.md`, the table).

### 2.3 What the specification says, dated

- It allows the double membership: exclusion is the smallest relation with the listed properties, none of them about instantiations (`Specification/basic/types-vals-vars.tex:184-189`); "trait declarations are allowed to extend other instantiations of themselves", with `Empty extends List[\T\]` (`basic/trait-parameters.tex:339-397`). It forbids the shortcut: overloads may not differ in static parameters, and "static parameters do not enter into the determination of which declarations are applicable" (`basic/overloading.tex:100-107`). It fixes static arguments at the call site (`trait-parameters.tex:374`), yet one sentence has them inferred before applicability for dynamic calls, citing an empty chapter (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 6`).
- Dated: all four passages are 2008-2009 text, last touched by Sukyoung Ryu no later than 2009-11-06 and byte-identical to the 2012 HEAD; the overload sentence predates the rule's entry into the checker (2010-05-28) by a year and was never revised after. The one passage that moved in 2010 is the advanced chapter's Meet Rule preamble, its author lost in the cut history. `Specification-1.0-frozen/` is not an independent snapshot for three of the files (`spec-sentences-dated.md § 1-5`).
- On `typecase`: the specification has no "unreachable clause" error, so the checker's refusal of a clause as unreachable is sound exactly when its exclusion question is; under the specification's relation `ProbeTypecaseMIE`'s clause is reachable, and walk reaches it (`REPORT.md`, "The specification, read around every line cited").
- Between machine widths the specification's mechanism is coercion, not subtyping: no sentence makes `ZZ32` a subtype of `ZZ64`, and its one example declares `ZZ64 coerce(z: ZZ32)`. What it does state as subtyping is ℤ inside ℚ inside ℝ (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 4`).

### 2.4 Where it sits in the tower

- Today: `ZZ32 <: ZZ64 <: ZZ <: AnyIntegral <: QQ <: RR64 <: Number`, each level carrying the self-typed traits at its own type, and `Number` itself carrying four at `Number`; so every level adds an instantiation, and `ZZ32` has `Equality` at `Number`, `QQ`, `ZZ`, `ZZ64` and `ZZ32` (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 5`).
- The 61 errors by family: tower 13 declarations, comparisons 4, `Maybe` 4, reductions 2; ten of the 23 are objects that only inherit the conflict (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 5`). The tower's, the comparisons' and `Maybe`'s chains all follow declared subtyping; only the reductions' markers do not (`patents-forest-rule.md § 2`).
- Row 330 leans on the nesting: "ℤ sits under ℚ under ℝ64 in the tower, so an integer result goes back into float arithmetic by promotion" (`POSITIONS.md:49`).

### 2.5 What the library already does

- Two deliberate double memberships: the tower's self-typed traits, and the reductions' markers, `SumReduction` under "Hack to permit any Number to work non-parametrically" with `DistributesOver` at several instantiations (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 5`). The markers are read nowhere; their only reader is commented out (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 2c`).
- The compiler prelude, the team's own compiled-world library, stayed inside the rule by flattening: Guy Steele commented out the comparison `extends` clauses on 2011-07-15, `ZZ32` and `ZZ64` were siblings under a `Number` without algebra by 2011-07-22, and widening is 15 `coerce` lines with no coercion from an integer into a float (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 5, § 7`; `price-keep-the-rule.md § 1`). `Library/CompilerAlgebra.fsi:16, :24` spell `Equality` and `StandardTotalOrder` with `comprises T`, the patents' self-typed form (`patents-forest-rule.md § 2`).
- The compiled side's own tests widen by coercion and never by hand (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 3`). The interpreter has no coercion at all (`coordinator/FACTS.md:52`); the team left "TODO add checks for COERCE" at its dispatch site, and its phase order already lifts every `coerce` into a callable function (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 3`).
- Where the designers departed from Java: reified generics with run-time dispatch over them. Java's rule exists for erasure, not for dispatch, and the OOPSLA paper borrowed the rule without that reason (`literature.md § 5`). Naden's hole opens only in Fortress's combination (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 6`).
- The team's own way past a conflict the checker refuses, an open level with a `NOT YET` comment and hand-written excludes, was tested for the `AnyIntegral` closure and does not carry the float operators (`POSITIONS.md:45`, `:48`); it is not a fourth route here.

### 2.6 The peers, by family

- JVM languages: Java, Kotlin and Scala forbid the double membership and have flat numbers with widening; Java's stated reason is erasure (`literature.md § 5`). X10 follows Java's rule, its numbers are structs, widening is an implicit chain, overloads are chosen statically (`literature.md § 3`).
- Static-resolution languages: C#, F#, Rust and Haskell allow the double membership and choose instances and overloads at compile time; their numbers are flat (C#'s `INumber<TSelf>` on sibling structs). Swift cannot even write two conformances, and its integer proposal "does not solve the integer promotion problem" (`literature.md § 5-6`).
- Julia: single inheritance, concrete types final, `Int32` not below `Int64`, mixing by promotion, and "no notion of soundness" (`literature.md § 2`). Cecil, the multiple-dispatch ancestor, erases type arguments from dispatch and keeps the algebra at the root only (`literature.md § 4`).
- The Fortress group after 2012: the Welterweight draft (2012) states the rule as an ancestors meet rule with its self-typed dispatch step commented out; POPL 2019 (Park, Hong, Steele, Ryu) keeps the invariant exclusion, checks the static return type at run time, and has no self types and no tower; the 2012 patents' forest rule is the only text of theirs that admits the tower's chain (`literature.md § 1`; `patents-forest-rule.md § 5`).
- No peer nests numeric subtyping with self-typed traits at each level under run-time dispatch, and nobody published a resolution of that or a proof that the rule is necessary (`literature.md`, closing).

### 2.7 The history, dated

- 2008-05-06, Maessen: `ZZ32 extends { StandardTotalOrder[\ZZ32\], ZZ64 }`; 2008-07-02: `Integral` becomes parametric; 2008-07-15: `ZZ32 extends { ZZ64, Integral[\ZZ32\] }`, the double membership is in; 2008-07-21: `NN32`, `NN64`, `QQ` added and `AnyIntegral extends QQ` (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 7`; `library-scalar-extension-review.md:281-287`).
- By 2009-11-06, Ryu: the five spec passages in their final form (`spec-sentences-dated.md § 5`).
- 2010-05-28, Hilburn: exclusion of two instantiations of one invariant generic enters the checker; 2010-07-26: extended to all ancestors as `checkP`, "as described in the paper" (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 7`). 2010: the advanced Meet Rule preamble rewritten (`spec-sentences-dated.md § 3`).
- 2011-07-15 and 2011-07-22, Steele: the compiler prelude's tower made flat (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 7`). 2011-08-12: the OOPSLA paper's exclusion text (`REPORT.md`, "What the record did not have"). 2011-11-10, Naden's workshop paper: specialisation motivated by the library, the exclusion assumed (`literature.md § 1`).
- 2012-01-20: `CompilerAlgebra` spells `comprises T`; 2012-06-06, Steele: a comprises-based subtyping rule commented out, "Gets a stack overflow error" (`patents-forest-rule.md § 2, § 4`). 2012: the Welterweight draft submitted to OOPSLA (`literature.md § 1`). 2012-07-19: the import root that holds `checkP` (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 7`).
- 2012-08-31, the last day: Naden's write-up, "more work needs to be done ... the numerical hierarchy", and the four patents filed with the forest rule (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 1`; `patents-forest-rule.md § 1`). 2019: POPL, the rule kept, self types gone (`literature.md § 1`).

### 2.8 The derivation from his principles

- Finish the designers' intent (`POSITIONS.md:24`): they built both the rule (compiler, 2010-2012) and the nested tower (library, 2008, kept to the end) and never reconciled them; Naden left it open, Steele flattened for the compiled world, the patents sketched a way to keep both. There is no one intent to finish; there are two implemented halves and one unimplemented sketch.
- Implementation reality outweighs the early spec (`POSITIONS.md:80`): the overload sentence is 2009 text, never revised, enforced by neither path; it is the weakest fact here, and route B rests on it. The rule is implemented and published. But the tower's nesting is implementation reality too, and the interpreter, also theirs, ran it for four years.
- JVM defaults where sensible, corrected for a high-performance, mathematically honest language (`POSITIONS.md:72`): the JVM default is the flat tower (an `int` is not a `long`), and Swift, Rust, Julia and X10, his named counterparts, all take it with explicit or coerced widening. The honest mathematics, ℤ inside ℚ inside ℝ, survives as coercion under A and as subtyping under B and C. On the JVM a compiled `ZZ32` is already a boxed class (`FZZ32`), so unboxing is the code generator's static-type work under every route (reasoning, not measured).
- One library (`POSITIONS.md:46`): A keeps the interpreter's library as the one library but gives its tower the prelude's shape, and the judge struck A for that (`multiple-instantiation-exclusion.md § 8`); B and C leave the shape alone. The decision as recorded was to delete the prelude, not to refuse every choice it made.
- `double[]` and sizes not sidestepped (`POSITIONS.md:41`): mostly orthogonal. A makes the specification's bound `T extends AdditiveGroup[\T\]` usable on `RR64`, which the shipped tower fails (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 4`; `coordinator/FACTS.md:99`); B's call-site fix guards `nat` parameters out (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 3`). C4's `T extends Number` stays valid under every route.
- The library's own practice is the standard (`POSITIONS.md:40`): the practice is the nesting, and A ends it; B and C keep it. microGPT: 37 sites rely on the nesting, all through `Number`'s catch-alls, 7 mixing an integer with a float; under A they need coercion or `asFloat`, and under B and C the model's text changes nowhere (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 4`).
- Interpreter performance is irrelevant to every decision (`POSITIONS.md:38`): no route is argued from speed here; microGPT's 941K calls through `Number`'s `+` at one line are an interpreter count and carry no weight (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 4`).

## 3. The three routes, priced from the notes

### Route A: keep the rule, flatten the tower

- Checker: nothing. The count goes 103 to 22, 82 errors gone (61 exclusion, 2 `comprises`, 19 in `RangeInternals` by a mechanism not traced) and 1 new; still masked at `FortressLibrary.fsi:2526` and never reaching the component (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 5`). The tripwire test `XXXExclusionRelaxRungP` is deleted in the same change (`REPORT.md`, "The three homes").
- Code generator: nothing this route requires; the two side defects stay.
- Library: 14 declaration headers (58 lines in the `.fsi`, 62 in the `.fss`, one in each `FortressBuiltin` file); `Number` gives up 52 api declarations and 57 bodies and `RR64` takes its own arithmetic on; 15 inherited methods restated on the leaves (24 method-leaf pairs); `SUM` and `PROD` rewritten, three options open; the `DistributesOver` markers dropped; `AnyIntegral comprises { ZZ, ... }` becomes well formed and the `NOT YET` accommodation of 2026-09-21 goes (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 1, § 6`).
- Interpreter: coercion called at dispatch and at typed bindings, 150-200 lines in six files, the compiled side's own mechanism (remedy i); or, without it, 160 library and 207 test sites converted by hand and the same in every future program (remedy ii). One line, `FortressLibrary.fss:4121`, is reached by every passing test, so the interpreter rung lands first (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 3, § 6`).
- The compiled side's precedent for remedy (i): of the 40 programs in `library_tests/`, 12 name a type wider than `ZZ32` and 9 bind an integer literal to one, every one through the prelude's `coerce`, none by hand (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 3`).
- Tests: under (i) no test text changes by design, but some outputs can: `x: ZZ64 = 2147483647; x + 1` wraps today because the value stays 32-bit; new coercion tests on both paths; one `typecase` site converted regardless (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 6`).
- Forecloses: the specification's `Empty extends List[\T\]` on the compiled path (any keep route does); ℤ as a subtype of ℚ, which becomes `RR64 coerce(x: ZZ)`; row 330's promotion by subtyping, which survives as static coercion on the compiled path and needs remedy (i) under walk, its unbounded half untouched; "any Number non-parametrically" (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 4`). Keeps the shortcut.
- Not settled: generic inference over mixed widths, where the flat join is `AnyIntegral` (`lo:hi`); whether the compiled checker coerces a functional method's `self`; 212 candidate lines no test runs; the component's type check; which `SUM` replacement (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 6`). And a divergence: the interpreter would choose coercions at run time where the specification and the compiled path choose them statically (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 3`).
- Risk: the lowest share of derived work; every number is from a run-time survey over all 392 interpreter tests. The risk is direction, not arithmetic: the library's shape moves toward the prelude's, and `Number` loses its catch-alls.

### Route B: drop the rule, instantiate at the call site, enforce the overload sentence

- Checker: rung P's 22-line relaxation, in every analyzer, since only the broad placement reaches `typecase` reachability (`REPORT.md`, "What the placement question looks like"); the sentence, 19 lines in `OverloadingChecker` (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 5`). Count 103 to 24 on today's tree (`patents-forest-rule.md § 4`).
- Code generator: 43 lines in `OverloadSet.java`, the arms of a generic dispatcher renamed into the dispatcher's own parameters; `MieDispatchSub` then prints `Marker[Blue]`; the prelude is byte-identical and all 43 generic-overload compiler tests print the same. Not built: generic dotted methods, functional methods, `opr` and `nat` parameters (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 3`).
- Library: under the narrower reading (`own`, a functional method's own parameters) 179 api pairs refused, 11 of them errors already, in families whose repair is padding to one parameter list or renaming (juxtaposition 80, `DOT` 48, `CMP` 25, `MIN`/`MAX` 16, range helpers 9, `__cond` 1), plus six other library files; the interpreter accepts the padding. Under the wider reading (`lifted`) 335, refusing the tower's own implementations of `Integral`'s methods, "which the tower cannot survive". Symbolic operators, which the checker never examines, add 329 pairs by text scan (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 5`).
- Interpreter: no change for the double membership (it already accepts it) or the shortcut (it already refuses it); on `ScopeArmsX` the two paths already answer differently (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 6`).
- Tests: 6 gated compiler tests and 6 gated interpreter tests refused, 3 expected failures too (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 5`); `ProbeMIEPick` becomes a gated compile-error test; `Compiled12.invariantInference` goes red under the `skip` variant (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 3`).
- Forecloses: the shortcut (`tail`-style specialisation), which the specification never allowed and the interpreter never ran. Keeps `Empty`, the tower, row 330 and C4's bounds as they are.
- Not settled: which reading of "static parameters" a functional method gets, the note recommends neither; whether the sentence covers operators and arities; the specification's own two models of when static arguments are fixed; the dotted-method remainder; the gate not run (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 6`).
- Added up here, not in the scope note: the tower's specialised method implementations (`ZZ64`'s `TIMES` beside `Integral[\I\]`'s; `QQ`'s and `Number`'s `MIN`, `MAX`, `MINMAX`) pass the narrower reading and are the inner-level hole the forest probes show, a `ZZ32` reaching `ZZ64`'s body where generic code was promised a `ZZ32` (`patents-forest-rule.md § 3`, "In the library"). The hole is the nesting's, not the rule's, so B keeps it unless it also takes C's completion below (seven declarations plus the derived 40-70 lines) or the wider reading, which flattens by refusal.
- Risk: the smallest measured code and the largest unmeasured reach: 179 is the checker's count of the names it examines, the 329 operator pairs are a text scan, and components and `tests/` were scanned as text only.

### Route C: the patents' forest rule

- Checker: `checkP` lets two instantiations of a self-typed generic exclude only when their arguments are unordered, about 30 lines, in every analyzer, built and measured; `comprises T` on the seven traits in `.fsi` and `.fss` (the shadow accepted the library's bound as the test; under the patents' literal test nothing lifts); the covariant reading of `comprises T`, 3 lines; and a return type rule that checks every self-typed level below a specialisation unless a more specific declaration covers it, 40-70 lines, not built, derived from the probes and from no source (`patents-forest-rule.md § 2, § 4`). Count 103 to 37, the 13 marker errors staying until the markers go.
- Code generator: dispatch made to agree with the checker's static choice under the covariant reading, not sized; and the 43-line call-site fix, still needed on a chain (`patents-forest-rule.md § 3, § 4`).
- Library: the markers dropped (2 declarations); seven inner-level specialisations completed at every level below (`ZZ64`'s `TIMES`; `QQ`'s and `Number`'s `MIN`, `MAX`, `MINMAX`); `Integral comprises I` beside `AnyIntegral comprises { ZZ }` unmeasured (`patents-forest-rule.md § 3, § 6`).
- Interpreter: the probe pair with `comprises T` overflows its overload check's stack; without the specialisation it runs (`patents-forest-rule.md § 3`).
- Tests: all four of rung P's probes behave as under the stock checker; new tests for the forest, the covariant reading and the return rule (`patents-forest-rule.md § 4`).
- Forecloses: `Empty extends List[\T\]` and `Both` stay illegal (`patents-forest-rule.md § 1`). Keeps the tower, row 330, the shortcut and C4's bounds.
- Not settled: the return type rule has no source; whether `comprises T` is read covariantly; which instance a checked call takes on a chain (the compiled checker takes the top, the interpreter and the patents the bottom); the checker-dispatch order mismatch under the covariant reading; symbolic operators still escape the overloading check (`patents-forest-rule.md § 6`; `dispatch-and-route-c.md § 2`).
- Risk: the highest share of derived work. Of five pieces two are built; the soundness rule is ours; no paper published the forest and the checker never implemented it; the team's own comprises-based subtyping attempt overflowed in 2012.

### Hybrids, and the counts that disagree

- The forest for the tower plus the call-site fix, without the overload sentence, is route C as the forest note prices it: the sentence is not among its five pieces, and its overload half is the completion rule (`patents-forest-rule.md`, the routes). The notes hold no cheaper cut of C: dropping the return type rule leaves `ForestGeneric`'s hole open.
- A flat machine-width tower (`ZZ32`, `ZZ64`, `NN32`, `NN64` as coercing siblings, the specification's own example) with ℤ inside ℚ inside ℝ kept as subtyping would still need the forest rule for the upper chain, since `ZZ` carries `Equality` at `ZZ`, `QQ` and `Number`; its price is A's conversion sites for the fixed widths only plus all of C. No note splits the survey's sites by level, so this hybrid is named here, not priced.
- The counts disagree by tree and by instrument. 93 to 33 (`REPORT.md`) was rung P's relaxation on the pre-L base `d610695c0`; 103 to 24 (`patents-forest-rule.md § 4`) is the same switch on today's tree after rung L's api repairs; 103 to 22 (`price-keep-the-rule.md § 5`) is the flat library under the stock checker, and its two fewer are the `comprises` errors the flat `AnyIntegral` makes well formed; 103 to 37 is the forest. All four are the gate's instrument, which stops the `FortressLibrary` api at its hierarchy stage. The scope note's 203 (`scope-call-site-dispatch.md § 5`) is the zero probe's stack, which repairs that stop so the overloading check reaches the api; it is a deeper instrument, so its 203 to 382 under the sentence cannot be laid beside 22, 24 or 37.

## 4. Weighing, as he asked

- The premise (`POSITIONS.md:80`): the specification "was written in a vacuum early on", "as a wish"; the type group "knew much more about what's feasible"; "when that wish met reality, then the spec must change"; strong weight on "whatever Naden was thinking and where he was driving the design".
- Applied to the dated facts, the premise selects: the rule stays, because it is the group's implemented and published position (2010-2011, Steele among the authors, restated in 2012 and 2019); the overload sentence carries no weight, because it is 2009 text never revised and enforced by neither path; and `Empty extends List[\T\]` and ℤ-inside-ℚ-as-subtyping carry little, because the compiled checker never implemented them. That puts route B outside the premise: its footing is the spec sentence and the interpreter.
- Between A and C the premise's own facts split. For A: Steele's flattening is the group's implemented resolution of this exact conflict in the compiled world (2011-07), and their last published line (POPL 2019) has no self types and no tower. For C: the patents are the latest thing they wrote (2012-08-31, after the flattening, Naden and Steele among the inventors) and they keep the tower; but they are claims, never built, their self-typed dispatch step is commented out in Welterweight, and the checker's one comprises-based attempt overflowed (2012-06-06).
- Where the facts complicate the premise: the interpreter and its library are the group's too, and they kept the nesting and refused the shortcut to the end; the sentence B rests on was never enforced, but neither was the nesting ever checked by the compiled checker; and "where Naden was driving" points two ways on one day, the self-type bound `add[\X <: Ring[X]\]` (a flat tower's idiom) in his write-up and the forest in the patent.
- What the premise does not settle: it ranks texts by date and by whether they were implemented, and the two implemented halves are both the group's, their reconciliation implemented by no one. So between A and C the premise leaves a judgement, not a ranking, and the judgement below is made on what is measured against what is derived.
