## 0. Short answer

- **The rule Pavol gave is short, and it is about the worker.** On 09-17 01:31 he said the work "will be fucking test driven". At 16:50 he said what he meant: "The workers should be writing the failing test, verifying that it is failing, doing the fix, and proving that it is working", and that a one-off proof "that does not leave a permanent check in the gate is not an acceptable process". Nothing he said asks for a captured file, a report of the failure, or anything committed as evidence.
- **The word "skeptic" and its stance are not his.** The Fable coordinator wrote the first skeptic into the ladder script at 02:06:47 on 09-17 ("Your default is to refuse"). The word appears nowhere as a role name in the session or the tree before its message of 02:07:03. Four minutes earlier the harness's workflow-authoring skill had loaded into its context, because Pavol's message of 02:03 contained the word "ultracode"; the skill says "Adversarial verify: spawn N independent skeptics per finding, each prompted to REFUTE. […] Default to refuted=true if uncertain." His first use of the word "skeptic" is 15:51: "the worker whose job was to verify the implementation".
- **"Adversarial review" is his phrase for a separate reader who attacks a document, plan, brief or decision.** He used it from 08-22 to 09-20 and never for the per-rung skeptic. The coordinator itself said so on 09-22: the blinded reviews "were a separate arrangement for design questions, not the batch skeptic".
- **"The skeptic writes a different test" is real, and it is the coordinator's clause.** At 17:26 on 09-17 the Opus coordinator wrote into the plan (`54cfc4d9e`) that the skeptic "writes its own small program, runs it under `walk` and under `fortress compile` plus `run`, and compares", "not the rung's test — its own". It had seen four skeptics do this unasked in the first climb. Pavol did not ask for it in those words. On 09-19 10:14 he described it as a fact ("Skeptics are instructed to create uh, their own tests") and asked how many end up permanent in the suite. The audit's answer: 77 skeptic probe programs, none run by any suite, and of 22 defects the skeptics found, 2 gated.
- **The recorded failure is the coordinator's step from his "verifying that it is failing".** It went in at 16:52 on 09-17 (`3cbcfffb6`, Opus coordinator): the failure output is recorded in the report and "a rung whose report has no recorded failure is refused". It became a file at 19:26 the same day, in the repair batch's script (committed as `4aae6fdf5`): "capture the failure output to a file BEFORE the edit exists". The governing documents, `PLAN.md`, `protocol.md` and `POSITIONS.md`, never got that step; they still say what he said.
- **The commit that shows the skeptic the test failed before is a by-product.** The worker is told to commit "after the failing test is written and its failure captured" (`aebca84cf`, 09-18 19:23). The stated purpose was insurance against a container dying, and Pavol's yes on 09-18 19:13 was for pushing to `wip/` branches. From batch 1 on, skeptics used that commit order as proof: 15 of the 40 `SKEPTIC.md` files argue that the capture predates the edit, most by commit order or time.
- **Pavol's hypothesis, "was my request for an adversarial skeptic the original sin": in part, and not his request.** Section 7 has the evidence. The stance that made evidence necessary was built by the coordinator, matching the harness's pattern. Captures already existed before any skeptic requirement, because the coordinator's briefs asked workers for reports and before/after tables from the first rung.

## 3. The skeptic role as first specified, and how it changed

### 3a. The four first specifications

**Ladder script, `94cbbb9f3` (09-17 02:06:47, Fable coordinator; skeptic agents on Opus).** The prompt is unchanged in `7f8568515`, `4affd6664` and `6b3e98ed1`, the last of which launched the climb:

> You are the skeptic for the change described below. Your default is to refuse. Do not edit source files.
> Change: ${implementSummary}
> Checks, all of them: (1) git diff --stat and read the full diff; refuse anything outside the allowed boundary or larger than the test needs. (2) The new test(s) pass by ../bin/fortress junit <dir>/<name>.test from ProjectFortress. (3) The gate is green: read the newest outputs under ProjectFortress/TEST-RESULTS/ for testFast and testSystem, confirm they are from after the edit (timestamps) and show zero failures; if they are missing or stale, run ant testFast and ant testSystem yourself as separate calls. (4) The ladder subset: ${subsetHint}; every file in the subset reaches at least the phase it reached in the baseline, and at least one moves up. (5) The ledger, FACTS and handover edits are present and in the plain register. List every regression you find. Approve only if all five hold.

- It re-runs the new test itself (check 2). It does not write a test or a program of its own. It does not look for a recorded failure. In this script a separate planner agent wrote the failing test and confirmed it failed ("confirm it fails today with ../bin/fortress junit"), and a separate implementer edited, so the test's author was not the fix's author.
- "Your default is to refuse" is the harness skill's "Default to refuted=true if uncertain".

**Batched-climb plan, `3f3f766a2` (09-17 16:09:36, Opus coordinator).** "**Skeptic** (k in parallel, one per rung). Reads that rung's worktree: the diff, the new test, the subset before and after, the report. Judges whether the rung's claim is true and whether the record is honest. It may refuse, once, into a repair inside that worktree […]" and "Every rung is still judged by a skeptic that did not write it, on the rung's own evidence, with one repair and no more." The plan folded the planner into the implementer ("The per-rung planning folds into the implementer", chat 15:52:46), so one agent now writes the test and the fix. The skeptic runs before the merge and no longer holds a gate.

**Plan revisions the same afternoon.** `3cbcfffb6` (16:52:25) adds "the recorded failure" to what the skeptic reads. `54cfc4d9e` (17:25:59) adds:

> **The skeptic's required differential.** Not optional, and not satisfied by reading the rung's own test. ## 7. Pavol's hypothesis: was his request for an adversarial skeptic the original sin?

Verdict: **in part.** His words did not ask for it. The coordinator's construction of the skeptic as a refuser that must be given evidence did, together with several causes that do not involve the skeptic at all.

**What he asked the skeptic to be, in his words.**
- "self verifying and committing as they go" (09-17 02:03:44), of the whole climb. He invoked "ultracode", which loaded the harness skill.
- "the worker whose job was to verify the implementation" (15:51:34); "a fresh perspective on the solution of a problem that the worker prepared" (17:00:37).
- a reader who should "specifically focus" on the spirit of the design (17:22:15) and who needs "the spec and repo map uh, guidance" to catch systemic errors (18:31:40).
- "Skeptics are instructed to create uh, their own tests" (09-19 10:14:23), said as a fact after the coordinator's clause, followed by the question of permanence.
- "if the skeptic is checking, or the skeptic has them on disk, we don't need to commit them" (09-19 12:50:41); "if we gave it the evidence that we gathered, it should not reject" (09-22 06:16:20); "they have their transcripts on disk and can check them" (09-29 20:42:23).
- "Adversarial" is his word for a separate reviewer of documents, plans, briefs and decisions. The one time it touches the rung loop, 17:00:37, "your adversarial reviewer" is the plan-attacking worker.

**What the coordinator built.**
- The skeptic role and its stance match the harness pattern, which loaded four minutes before the script was written and has the only earlier use of the word: "spawn N independent skeptics per finding, each prompted to REFUTE" and "Default to refuted=true if uncertain" against "Your default is to refuse" (`94cbbb9f3`).
- It made evidence in the skeptic's hands the condition for approval: the recorded failure ("refused […] not negotiable"), the provenance block whose lines the skeptic opens, and the differential. It added the paste, then the file, then the milestone commit, then the `.txt` and tracked-path rules.
- It gave the skeptic a model of a reader that cannot trust anything it did not see: "Treat it as a claim to be checked, not as evidence".

**Is the need to convince a skeptic what turned into committed captures and recorded failures?** Yes for two links, no for the origin.
- **Recorded failure as paperwork: yes, through the skeptic.** The stated reason for step 3 (16:52:42) is that "nobody verified it had ever actually failed". Someone must be able to verify, and the verifier is the skeptic, so the failure has to be visible to it. The batch design also removed the planner as a second author and the gate from the skeptic, which made the skeptic the only reader of an ungated rung.
- **Committed captures: yes, through the skeptic's correction, not through the adversarial stance.** The first reason for committing them is R2's skeptic ("Every citation to them […] would dangle", `SKEPTIC.md:285`). A skeptic that refuses without a recorded failure makes the report cite a capture, and a cited capture must be tracked.
- **Not the origin.** Captures were made before any skeptic required them. (The ladder skeptic's check 3 already had it read the gate's output files under `TEST-RESULTS/`, but those stayed untracked.) Rung 0 and rung 1 were briefed for `before.out`/`after.out` and a `REPORT.md` with "the subset table before and after" (02:14 and 02:43 on 09-17, `53362cb88`, `b014ff80d`). Rungs 1 to 8 saved the failing test's output although the workflow script asks for no capture, force-adding `.out` files (`archaeology.md` 1(e) and section 8), and the only skeptic they had re-ran tests and gates. The batch design's "recorded failure" (16:52) turned an existing habit into a rule.

**Earlier and independent causes.**
1. The coordinator's rung briefs asking for reports and before/after tables (09-17 02:14 to 02:43), and the workers' reading of them.
2. The protocol's "Evidence over speculation; reproduce before explaining" and "reports are first-class deliverables" (`505d3ba48`, 08-22). See `archaeology.md` 1(b).
3. Pavol's own 09-09 wish for committed process breadcrumbs for the exploration runs (`archaeology.md` 1(a)) and the FACTS-with-sources request of 09-15 that made every cited path matter (`archaeology.md` 1(c)).
4. The batch design's merge of planner and implementer, and its removal of the gate from the skeptic (`3f3f766a2`, `3cbcfffb6`).
5. The `wip/` milestone commits (`aebca84cf`, insurance, with his yes) and the gather that applies a branch's whole net change (`archaeology.md` 1(j)); this is what makes the milestone commit visible to a skeptic.
6. The Stop hook's uncommitted-file reminders, which Pavol cited on 09-26 as a reason for workers to commit as they go (`archaeology.md` 1(p)).
7. The `.gitignore` for `*.log` and `*.out`, set for LaTeX byproducts (`414b790e3`), which pushed the choice to `.txt` and "every path you cite is tracked".

So the accurate statement is that the skeptic's adversarial stance made evidence a requirement, and the coordinator's own rules about citing and tracking made the evidence a committed file. Pavol's requests were for a fresh-eyes verifier, a reviewer of plans and decisions, and a permanent test in the gate.
