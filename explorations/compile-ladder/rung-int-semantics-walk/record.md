# Record lines for rung I (`rung-int-semantics-walk`)

## FACTS.md

Four lines.

- **Under `walk`, a native can raise a Fortress exception that a Fortress `catch` sees** (rung I, `explorations/compile-ladder/rung-int-semantics-walk/REPORT.md`, section 4.1): `throw new FortressError((FObject) Driver.getFortressLibrary().getRootValue(name))` from inside a `builtinPrimitive` method reaches `Evaluator.forTry` unchanged (`MethodClosure.java:85-86` has no `catch`) and matches `catch e IntegerOverflow`; `Int.overflow()` (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/interpreter/glue/prim/Int.java:256-259`) is the helper and `WellKnownNames.integerOverflowException` the name. Measured by the 25 `overflows(...)` assertions of `ProjectFortress/tests/IntSemanticsRungI.fss`, whose helper catches nothing but `IntegerOverflow`. Before this rung no native raised one; the interpreter's mapping of every other Java exception is unchanged (`FunctionClosure.java:188-196`), so row 336's division by zero and row 347's `Int.rc` still end the run.
- **The interpreter's integer rules after rung I** (`<landing>`): `LSHIFT`/`RSHIFT` on `ZZ32`, `ZZ64`, `NN32` and `NN64` saturate at the width and reverse on a negative count, the count read by the class of its value (an `NN64` count of `2^63` or more is a count beyond the width, a `ZZ` count is read exactly); the unbounded `ZZ`'s shift count is exact at any size, a negative one giving `0` or `-1` and an unrepresentable left shift a catchable `IntegerOverflow`; `GCD` and `LCM` on `ZZ32` and `ZZ64` are nonnegative, `0 LCM 0` is `0`, and an unrepresentable result, including `MIN GCD 0` and `MIN GCD MIN`, is a catchable `IntegerOverflow`; `ZZ64.narrow` keeps the low 32 bits. The spec's `shift` is declared (next line).
- **Every functional-method name of the library is reserved in every program that imports it**: the disambiguator's `checkForShadowingVar` (`ProjectFortress/src/com/sun/fortress/scala_src/disambiguator/ExprDisambiguator.scala:745-763`) rejects a parameter or local named `even` or `numerator` with `Variable even is already declared.` (`explorations/compile-ladder/rung-int-semantics-walk/probes/ShadowEven.txt`), and the specification forbids a parameter to shadow within the reach of a functional method, which is the whole component and every importer (`Specification/basic/declarations.tex:426-432`, `:435-436`, `:454-461`, `:533`). So declaring the spec's `shift(self, k)` on `ZZ` in `Library/FortressLibrary.fsi` alone stops every `walk` run on the library's own `Range` parameters named `shift`: 12 api sites, then 6 in `FortressLibrary.fss`, 30 in `RangeInternals.fss` and a local in `List.fss:311` (`probes/shift-collision-layer1.txt` to `layer4.txt`). Rung I (`<landing>`) landed it with the renames the specification requires, as ruled in `explorations/compile-ladder/rung-int-semantics-walk/JUDGE.md`: `shift(self, k:AnyIntegral): ZZ` is declared on `trait ZZ` (`Library/FortressLibrary.fsi:544`, the line after `even`; body `Library/FortressLibrary.fss:885-886`, after `RSHIFT` at `:883-884`, bound to `BigNum$LShift`), and the colliding parameters and the `List.fss:311` local are renamed to `amount` (4 sites in `FortressLibrary.fsi`, 6 in `FortressLibrary.fss`, 8 in `RangeInternals.fsi`, 30 in `RangeInternals.fss`, 1 in `List.fss`); the array-origin `.shift(` is unaffected (`probes/harness-shift-landed.txt`). No other file in the tree binds `shift` (the only other `shiftLeft(shift: I)` is `CompilerLibrary/FortressLibrary.fsi:2061-2064`, on no source path), so a `walk` program may no longer bind it, as it may not bind `even`; the compiled path, with no prelude declaration, keeps no such reservation.
- **`ProjectFortress/tests/RangePrototype.fss` relied on the interpreter's `LCM` carrying the sign of the product**: its prototype intersections took their direction from `resultStride DIV other'.stride` with `resultStride = this.stride LCM other.stride`, and went red when `LCM` became nonnegative (`probes/harness-regression-1.txt:6-8`); rung I made the two lines compute that signed stride explicitly, `(this.stride DIV (this.stride GCD other.stride)) DOT other.stride` (`:66`, `:171`), with no assertion changed. The library's own `RangeInternals.fss` never depended on the sign (`:442-445`, `:513-517`).
