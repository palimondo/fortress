LEFT AND REPORTED: NEITHER THE DECISION NOR Y'S SECTION SETTLES THE NEW TEXT (the batch record's
stop "A passage of the list whose new text neither the decision nor Y's section settles: the rung

P1  Specification/basic/types-vals-vars.tex:583-604, section "Intersection and Union Types": with
    trait S comprises {U,V}, trait T comprises {V,W}, U excludes W, "because of the comprises clauses
    of S and T and the excludes clause of U, any subtype of both S and T must be a subtype of V. Thus,
    V = S intersected with T", under the type-level definition of :583-585. The passage reads the
    clauses at the type level. Under R1 a program may add a parameterized trait G[\X\] extending S
    and T, unlisted, whose extenders are all below V; every value of G[\ZZ32\] is a V, but
    G[\ZZ32\] is a subtype of S and T and not of V. The example's own program has no such trait and
    its conclusion holds for it; whether the general reasoning is restated at the value level, or a
    trait that sits between two closed traits is refused, the decision does not say.
P2  Specification/advanced/overloading.tex:275-307, the same example: "Thus, V = S intersected with
    T, and the declaration f(V) 'disambiguates' f(S) and f(T), i.e., it is applicable to and more
    specific for any call to which both f(S) and f(T) are applicable". With P1's G, a call f(g) for
    g : G[\ZZ32\] has f(S) and f(T) applicable and f(V) not applicable by its static type. Same
    question as P1, with the overloading rules' guarantee at stake.
P3  Specification/basic/functions.tex:370-415, abstract function declarations: "the union of the
    argument types of the concrete declarations for f must be equal to T"; with Molecule, "the
    programmer must provide a definition for every immediate subtype of Molecule, or it is a static
    error". Molecule's immediate subtypes are its listed types, so the example holds; for a closed
    trait with a parameterized immediate subtrait between it and its listed types (AnyIntegral with
    Integral), "every immediate subtype" and "every listed type" differ, and the decision does not
    say which the rule means.
P4  Specification/basic/components/source-code.tex:428-434, a team draft note (item 2): a trait with a
    comprises clause "is considered to have a concrete method declaration ... provided that all its
    immediate subtraits are considered to have concrete method declaration with that signature".
    The same question as P3, in a note of proposals.
