Batch 7R is the ranges batch you decided at 19:23 UTC: the one library's ranges count with `ZZ32` only, as the compiler's own library already does. It is one run of two rungs, after batch 7 (H, A and B) has landed and before batch N, the inference rule with the numeral switch (your yes of 19:30 UTC). A rung is one fix, built test-first by one worker in its own copy of the tree and judged by a second worker, the skeptic, before the batch is merged and the whole test suite (the gate) runs once.

In plain words: today the library's range code, `Library/RangeInternals` (about 2,100 lines) and the `#` and `:` operators, is written once for any integer type, called `I`. The compiled checker cannot check most of it, because the language cannot convert a numeral such as `1` into a type known only as `I`. Written for `ZZ32` alone, most of those errors go away, and a range that starts with a numeral, such as `0#n`, stays legal once batch N gives a numeral its own type.

**No question.** Your decision settles what the batch builds. The choices left inside it are the rungs' own, each reported as a decision with the ways not taken. Three things follow from the record, and one word from you changes each (below).

**The rungs.**
- **J, ranges over `ZZ32`.** `RangeInternals` and the range operators of `FortressLibrary` lose their integer type parameter. A two- or three-dimensional range is a pair or triple of `ZZ32` ranges. The public range traits (`Range`, `FullRange` and the rest) keep their type parameter, because an array indexes with pairs and triples through them. The change uncovers a crash of the compiled checker, and J fixes it beside the library, with a few lines of Java: the checker asks whether a value is a `GeneratorZZ32`, a name only the compiler's own library declares.
