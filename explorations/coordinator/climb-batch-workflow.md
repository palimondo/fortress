# The batch workflow script, stage by stage

The script runs *a* batch, not batch 1. Everything a batch changes lies between the `MANIFEST` and `END MANIFEST` markers: the batch number, its decision record, its intro and overlap sentences, the first free ledger row, and one entry per rung — `id`, `slug`, `path`, `branch`, `tail`, `blurb`, `expectedMinutes`, `writesState`, `expectedMoves`, the three optional fields of the checker-count stage, `testIsStage`, `expectedCheckerCount` and `expectedCheckerCrash`, the optional `landsOnlyWith` (below), and the optional `briefing` and `checks`, the rung's briefing and the sub-list of it that the checking roles read ("Shared prefix"). Batch 1's manifest is left in place as the example.

## Manifest and scatter (item 8)

`expectedMinutes` sorts the scatter, longest-expected worker first: 0–12 minutes when the guess is right, free when it is not. k stays 4. The two-agent cap and the FIFO handing of a freed slot to the next queued agent are a comment on the manifest block with their `FACTS.md` sources; rung chains (decision 2(a)) are not implemented and the comment says why — replayed on batch 1's walls they save 0.3 minutes. Ledger numbering follows manifest order, not scatter order, and the script maps the results back.

`landsOnlyWith`, added 2026-09-26 for batch 6 (`CLIMB-BATCH-6.md` section 8, item 4), is a list of rung ids a rung lands only with: batch 6's T, the number chapters, names F, the flat library they describe. After the scatter, and before anything is handed on, `applyLandsOnlyWith` withholds every approved rung any of whose named rungs is not approved (stopped, dropped, its worker dead, withheld itself, or absent from the run), and repeats until nothing moves. A withheld rung keeps its approval in `approvedAs`, takes the state `withheld` and a `withheldReason` naming the rung it waited for and that rung's state, and reaches the gather on the not-landed list, so its source is never applied and its findings are folded as for any rung that did not land; its `expectedMoves` and checker declarations no longer count at the gate. Until then such a rule lived in the batch intro alone, which only the gather's reading enforced (batch 5's rule for rung Z's `cP` piece; `climb-batch-6-review.md`, change 10). A rung that is not in the run cannot be approved, so an entry that names one is withheld: batch 6's O names F only when it runs beside F (`FOLLOWUP ? [] : ['F']`).

## What a batch commits (2026-09-29)

A batch commits on a `wip/` branch what it commits on `main`, and nothing else: the Fortress change (source, library, specification text, tests), its reports (`REPORT.md`, `SKEPTIC.md`, `JUDGE.md` and a specification rung's `decision-record.md`, and the batch's `RECORD.md`), the ledger rows, the `FACTS.md` and `PLAN.md` lines, the handover line, and a script that is reusable. No captured output, log, raw output, probe program, copy of a script or test, list or dump is committed anywhere. An agent's scratch lives under `tmp/<slug>/` in its worktree, which `.gitignore:64` (`/tmp/`) ignores, and goes with the worktree when the commit stage removes it; the workers' transcripts, which the backup keeps, are the record of how a worker got where it did, and its test's failure before the fix is shown by its skeptic's own run, not by a file. The source is Pavol's decision of 2026-09-29 (`POSITIONS.md`, what a batch commits); the practice stage by stage is `postmortem-2026-09-29/synthesis.md`, section 1.

## Preparing a batch record

An Opus planning worker drafts `CLIMB-BATCH-<n>.md` and commits it. On Pavol's yes each time, one Fable worker then reviews it in place: it edits the record directly, writes one reason per change in a short review note (`climb-batch-6-review.md` is the example), and commits, so that the diff between the two versions shows exactly what the review changed. The coordinator reads that diff, fixes what is left, and brings the questions in section 1 to Pavol one per message; the batch launches on his go (batches 5 and 6; his words in the two entries of 2026-09-26 on the review of those manifests, in `POSITIONS-history.md`).

The planner builds each rung's briefing, writes it into the rung's section of the record, and the manifest carries it as the rung's `briefing` ("Shared prefix"). Its purpose is in-context learning (Pavol, 2026-09-27): the agents were not trained on Fortress, the language was never finished, and what they write from other languages' habits contradicts the specification and the library unless they have absorbed what the project has gathered. So a briefing carries, as keys of `explorations/coordinator/tools/facts-extract.sh`, decisions first: the `POSITIONS.md` entries, ledger rows and earlier judges' rulings the rung rests on; the specification's sections its subject touches, whole sections by their `\section` or `\subsection` heading and never grep hits; the probe and review notes already written on the subject, found through `INDEX.md`, by section; the library code that is the precedent for the same kind of problem, by the declarations it starts at; and the `FACTS.md` entries and map rows and sections of its area. Relevance decides what goes in, not size. The planner marks the sub-list the skeptic, the repair round and the judges read as `checks`: the decisions and ledger rows their checks need, and the specification's sections and the precedent code those checks compare against, for O the overflow subsections of `opr-overview.tex` and the glue natives it edits. Each list goes through `facts-extract.sh --check` on the tree the batch is cut from, every key matching exactly one place, and the tool's size line gives the reading it costs. Batch 6's four manifest entries are the worked examples, O's being the one batch 6b runs.

A record asks a rung for no corpus-wide output comparison, no ladder 'before', no microGPT check, no specification build log and no whole-suite run. The prefix allows one whole-suite run, at most once per code state in a rung's chain, and only to a rung whose edit changes the checker's or walk's source (Java or Scala under `ProjectFortress/src/`): the compiler and library tracks for the checker, the interpreter suite for walk. The worker runs it after its last edit, and a repair round after its own, with the verdict lines, command and commit in `REPORT.md`; the skeptic reads that result and does not run it again. Every other rung leaves the suites' verdict to the gate. A stop the record reserves that turns on a test's verdict (a verdict changed other than the rung's own, a set walk loaded refused after) is judged by the gate on the merged tree, and the record says so rather than asking a rung for a suite run (climb batch 7b's review, finding 5, approved 2026-09-30). In 7b rung C's chain ran the compiler and library tracks whole five times, twice on an unchanged tree, about 8 minutes of the box each, and found nothing its targeted runs had not. Rung W ran the interpreter suite once, because its record's verdict stops asked for what only a suite run shows. It asks for a measurement only where a test or a decision needs it, names it once for the whole batch when two rungs share a base, and states the one new thing it is for (protocol principle 5). A count of the programs a change reaches, when a decision needs one, is the planner's probe, taken before the record is written. A one-time count of changed interpreter outputs is asked for only where a decision of Pavol's names it for that rung (rows 330 and the numeral switch's Q-walk, rung Q's measurement), one edit pass against one base pass the coordinator starts before the launch; the record's section 7 holds no copy of the manifest, which `plan-<n>/manifest/` generates.

## Gather

Rungs are applied in ascending order of lowest edited line in the shared files, and a cited `file:line` that an earlier applied rung shifted is re-anchored by symbol (item 5). Each `recommendedRows` entry is opened or refused in one sentence (item 1). ## Review beside the gate

Both run in one `parallel()` — the substitute the review recommended for the rejected rung chains, 14.7 minutes per batch off the serial tail. - `agent()` throws an error whose message names a usage or rate limit (`LIMIT_ERROR`: "usage limit", "rate limit", "weekly limit", "hit your … limit", "too many requests", 429 and the like). The role is not run again.
- A skeptic or a judge comes back with nothing after its three attempts. A null does not say why the agent died, and 7b's limit came back as null, so this is the case that would have stopped 7b.

Once stopped, `callAgent` throws at the head of every attempt without starting an agent. A stage of the scatter that throws drops its rung to null, as the harness's `pipeline()` does. Below the scatter the script throws before it reads any rung's state, so no rung is marked dropped, withheld or not landed, and the gather does not start. On the merged tree the thrown error ends the run where it stands. The error and one log line say why, that nothing was decided, and how to resume: `resumeFromRunId` with the same script and the same args, once the cause is gone. The finished agents come back from the journal, and the stopped role runs again.

The script has no clock and cannot wait out a limit, so a clean stop is the whole fix. A limit that comes back as null still gets its three immediate attempts, since a null cannot be told from a death. At a limit those attempts are refused at once and write nothing. A rung worker, the gather, the review, the gate and the commit that come back with nothing, with no limit error thrown, keep their paths: `worker-died`, gather unresolved, `review-missing.1`, the gate run once more, not landed. That leaves one case open. A limit that comes back as null while the last rung's worker runs alone, every other chain done, still marks that rung `worker-died`.
