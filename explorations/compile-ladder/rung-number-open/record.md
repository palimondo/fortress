## Revival change

**The change.** The team's 2012 interpreter library declared `Number` with `comprises { RR64 }`, an abstract `asFloat(self): RR64` and `opr =(self, b:Number):Boolean = asFloat(self) = asFloat(b)`, so every number compared with every other through a float (`git show a874948ac:Library/FortressLibrary.fss`, lines 349-357); the specification's revised text said that the library declares `asFloat` on `Number` (`Specification/basic-lib/numbers.tex:46-49` before this change). The tree's `Number` now declares nothing (`Library/FortressLibrary.fss:380-381`), as the team's compiler prelude's (`ProjectFortress/LibraryBuiltin/CompilerBuiltin.fss:510-511`); each number type declares `asFloat`; two numbers of different types compare by `=` only where a coercion carries one into the other, by a pair of declarations per coercion, and otherwise by the fallback `opr =(a:Any, b:Any) = a SEQV b` (`Library/FortressLibrary.fss:96`) or a written `asFloat(x) = y`; a program's own type below `Number` needs no `asFloat` and compares by its own `=` or the fallback. Tests: `tests/NumberOwnTypeNoAsFloatWalk`, `NumberEqualityOwnTypeWalk`, `NumberMixedEqualityWalk`. A program's own trait that declares a functional `+` or `-` must exclude `Number` (or arrays) beside the library's array operators (`tests/NumberOpenTraitPlusBesideArrayWalk`). The reason: with `Number` open, an abstract `asFloat` forces a program's complex number to give a float, and an `=` built on it calls different complex numbers equal (a measured run: 'Cx(1,2) = Cx(3,4): true' through `exactValue`'s unlisted case); the prelude, the team's later library, declares nothing on `Number`; and the fallback `=` over `Any` applies to two numbers without a coercion, so without the pairs it answered every mixed `=` (12 walk tests failing in a measured run, among them 'a BigNum: 120 =/= a Int: 120').
