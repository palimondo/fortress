microGPT in Fortress: a transformer written as mathematics

A complete GPT — tokenizer, autograd, model, loss, Adam, sampling — expressed in Sun Labs' Fortress language so that its executable definitions typeset as the formulas of the papers, verified against Andrej Karpathy's microgpt to machine precision. The article teaches two things side by side: how a GPT works, and how to build the Fortress building blocks that let its mathematical core be written so directly.

1. How to read the figures

Fortress (Sun Labs, 2003–2012) was designed so that programs read like whiteboard mathematics: Unicode or ASCII-spelled operators, juxtaposition as a user-definable multiplication, big operators such as SUM defined in libraries, and a typesetter that turns the ASCII you type into the notation you mean. The four lines below are a running Fortress program; on the left is what Fortify makes of them.

{{fig:intro_intro}}

Conventions to keep in mind: SUM[x_i <- x] x_i^2 is a real reduction over the elements of x (typeset ∑ with its binding below); <| e | x <- g |> is a list comprehension (⟨ e | x ← g ⟩); x DOT w is the operator named DOT (·); identifiers such as alpha or x_i are typeset as α and xi; a name in [\ … \] is a static (type or size) argument and typesets in white brackets ⟦…⟧; keywords are in typewriter. Fortress code is implicitly parallel: the iterations of a comprehension or a reduction may run in separate threads, and the language keeps that order-independence visible rather than hiding it.

Three colors of code appear in this article. Python excerpts (blue) are quoted verbatim from the pinned reference. Fortress figures (cards) are typeset from the actual program. Plain grey blocks are interpreter output or shell commands.
