# The ask form

Everything put to the curator, a design question or a request for a yes, goes as one ask per message, in plain text in chat, never in a dialog tool such as AskUserQuestion. A design question is answered from the evidence before it reaches the curator, and the ask carries that evidence.

## The five parts, in this order

1. **The question**, named, in one line.
2. **The context**, in short bullets. Where the subject is new to the curator, a refresher comes first: why the design exists, what the peers do, how to judge it. Then the evidence, in this order: what the mathematics says; what each path does today, measured; what the specification says, under every spelling; what the library already does in the same family, and where the designers departed from Java; what the peers do; what the commits say; then the derivation from the principles on record.
3. **The options**, numbered, each with what it does, what it touches in the tree and what it costs or forecloses. The library's own way is checked before the fork goes out, and it is named first among the options when it is one.
4. **The ask**: one option by number, with "Yes:" saying what a yes commits the curator to afterwards and "No:" what a no costs or does instead.
5. **The recommendation**, one line, last, never in place of the explanation.

An ask runs about 1.5K to 3K characters; with a refresher, about 5K.

## A request for a yes

It says, in the message itself, what the work would do, what it produces, what it touches in the tree, what it costs in tokens, what a yes commits the curator to and what a no costs. No label stands in for an explanation.

## Around the form

- Decisions go one at a time, each with a default the curator can accept without reading the argument. The argument lives in the committed review, not in chat.
- Where the coordinator thinks the curator is wrong, it says so and shows why.
- A term new to the curator is defined in the sentence that uses it.
- A change to a line of the model program is shown as a diff.
- A document for the curator's approval, a whole plan or a skill, is published as a rendered page, and the curator gets its link, not a diff. A single decision is never a page.
