## 2. The one reading everything rational rests on

The original design has three rational value sets, ℚ (finite), ℚ\* (with ±∞) and ℚ# (with 0/0), and puts each in a type. Answer 6 rewrites the subtype lists "as the library's run-time check methods (`check`/`check_star`, returning `Maybe`)" (`POSITIONS.md:158`). That is the library's model: one wide type, refined at run time. `RR64` holds the infinities and NaN, and `check` and `check_star` pick out its finite and its non-NaN values (`Library/FortressLibrary.fsi:301-304`; bodies `Library/FortressLibrary.fss:395-399`). The library's one rational type `QQ` is wide in the same way: `Ratio(1,0)`, `Ratio(-1,0)` and `Ratio(0,0)` are values of it (`Library/FortressLibrary.fss:628-631`, made by the integer division at `:953-960`), and `QQ` declares `isNaN`, `isInfinite` and `isFinite` (`Library/FortressLibrary.fsi:393-396`). Measured under walk on the base (`probes/examples/RatValues.walk.txt`): `1/0 is 1/0, isInfinite true`; `0/0 is 0/0, isNaN true`; `1/0 + -1/0 is 0/0`; `typecase 1/0 of QQ: QQ`; `check` on `1/0` gives `Nothing` and `check_star` on `0/0` gives `Nothing`. The compiled path has no `QQ` (its prelude is flat and has none; `CLIMB-BATCH-6.md` section 3, F, "For the skeptic"), so there is no second path to run. The specification already says the same in its operator chapter: "For rational results, division by zero produces 1/0" (`Specification/basic/operators/opr-overview.tex:166`; the first wording of this record cited `:182-183`, corrected in the repair round, `JUDGE.md` section 1.3).

So the one type ℚ holds the finite rationals, +∞, −∞ and 0/0; the refinements the original expressed by ℚ and ℚ\* are the run-time checks answer 6 names. If ℚ held only the finite rationals, the two checks would have nothing to refine and the library's rational type would contradict the chapter. This is the rung's reading of answer 6, taken as settled; section 5 states the alternative and why it is not taken.

The integers are the other way round: the library's integer types hold no infinity (`Library/FortressLibrary.fss:647-648`, "For now, no infinite integers"), and the operator chapter's integer division by zero throws (`opr-overview.tex:165`; first cited as `:181`). The original's ℤ\* has no counterpart.

## 5. Decisions taken inside the rung

1. **ℚ holds ±∞ and 0/0** (section 2). Taken as answer 6's meaning, with the library and the operator chapter. Rejected: ℚ keeps only the finite rationals and division by zero throws. That contradicts the library's `Ratio`, the operator chapter's "division by zero produces 1/0" (`opr-overview.tex:166`) and answer 6's checks, and it would change the language's arithmetic, which no decision does. It is the reading with the most consequence, and it comes back to Pavol with the rest.
2. **ℚ's field and order clauses leave the listing**, and nothing replaces them. Rejected: (a) keeping them with ℚ≠ written ℚ, the brief's signature rule applied to a type argument, which would state a field whose 0 has an inverse; (b) the original design's clause for a type with every flag set, `AbelianGroup[\QQ,+,-\]` alone (`numbers-advanced.tex:276` at `e5414f5bf`), which the rung would be choosing from the superseded mechanism, and which fails at `+∞ + −∞ = 0/0`; (c) leaving them unrevised and reporting a stop, when section 2 and the chapter's own `:104` settle that they do not hold.
3. **The two `CMP` declarations become one, returning `Comparison`**: the brief's rule makes both parameters ℚ, and two declarations with one parameter type and two results are not a valid overload set; the library's declaration decides the result.
4. **`^` on the integers takes an exponent of type ℤ, not the library's `AnyIntegral`**: the library's `^` on integers is another method (its result is `RR64`), so "where the library does not declare the method, the unrefined type" applies. `AnyIntegral` is not a type of the specification. The judge upheld this decision (`JUDGE.md` section 1.4). Its consequence, which the first round did not see and the skeptic measured: a negative power is now admitted by the type, and nothing in the revised chapter gives its result. In the Working Draft ℤ was a subtype of ℚ, so a negative power went to ℚ's `^(self, power: ZZ)`, whose property is `x^y = 1/(x^(-y))` (frozen `numbers.tex:344-355`), and `2^(-1)` was 1/2; the revised ℤ entry still speaks only of "a nonnegative integer power" (`basic-integers.tex:498` at the repair round's tip). Walk answers `0.5`, a run-time `RR64`, and the compiled path `0`, a `ZZ32` (`probes/skeptic/SkIntPowerNeg.t1.txt`, `SkIntPowerType.t1.txt`). Neither the decisions nor rung F's table settle it, so under T's own stop the rung reports it and does not choose: the integer listing's callout (`basic-integers.tex:269-291`) and the Effect of Appendix I's "The integer trait" say that the Working Draft gave 1/2 and that the revised chapter does not give it; no normative sentence changed; row 441 of `record.md` (the judge's row (a)) records it, and it goes to Pavol (`probes/repair/for-pavol.txt`).
