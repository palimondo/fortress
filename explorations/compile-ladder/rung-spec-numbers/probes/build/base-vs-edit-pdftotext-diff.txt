> The number types
> Affected sections Section 33.1; Section 33.2.
> Change The eighteen rational types and the twelve integer types of the original, with N and N∗ , each a subtype of
> others, are replaced by seven number types the Fortress standard libraries declare: Z , Z64 , Z32 , N64 , N32 ,
> Q and R64 , siblings under Number, with the coercions the library declares: Z64 from Z32 and N32 ; N64
> from N32 ; Z from the four fixed-size types; Q from the five integer types; R64 from Z32 and from integer
> numerals. Every other conversion between them is explicit. Q includes +∞ , −∞ and 0/0 ; the integer types
> hold no infinite value. A subset such as the positive values is tested at run time, and the two checks the library
> declares, check and check ∗ on R64 , returning MaybeJR64K , are stated. The sentences that the type system
> tracks the refined types through arithmetic are removed, and so are the sentences that Q is a field and totally
> ordered and that Z∗ is not a ring; Q , which holds 0/0 , is stated to be neither totally ordered nor a field.
> Rationale Route A makes the library’s numeric types siblings under Number, each with its own algebra, a wider
> type converting a narrower one by coercion, and Z inside Q by coercion. The revival decided on 26 September
> 2026 that the refined types become the library’s own run-time checks, which return a Maybe, and that their
> original design is kept as a superseded design (Section I.1.13); and that a conversion between number types is a
> coercion where it is exact and stays explicit where it can lose precision, the coercion into R64 being declared
> from Z32 and the numerals only. The Fortress standard libraries never declared the refined types, and the trait
> that defines them is refused by instantiation exclusion (Section I.1.1). Q holds the values that Q∗ and Q#
> added, as the library’s rational type does, and as Chapter 16 already says: “For rational results, division by zero
> produces 1/0 .” The type that holds 0/0 is “neither totally ordered nor a field” in the original’s own words. The
> team’s later Types chapter (Luchangco, 2012) keeps the statement that the numeric types are mutually exclusive
> (Section 6.9).
> Effect A program names Q or Z where it named a refined type, and tests a sign or finiteness at run time. A generic
> call or a range over two different integer types writes its static argument until the rule that infers the narrowest
> type both coerce into is written into Chapter 20 together with its implementation. The conversion from N32
> into R64 , though exact, stays explicit, since the decision names only Z32 and the numerals. The library’s R32
> stays below R64 , as before, a value object that adds no instantiation of its own.
